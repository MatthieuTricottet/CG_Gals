\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Given their high apparent densities and low velocity dispersions \citep{Hickson82,Hickson+92}, compact groups appear to be ideal sites for the frequent tidal encounters and mergers that alter galaxy masses, luminosities, sizes, morphologies, and star-formation rates (e.g., \citealt{Mamon92}). \citet{MendesdeOliveira&Hickson94} provided the classic report of an excess of early-type galaxies in compact groups relative to the field (building on the photometric morphologies of \citealt{HKA89} and the merger-remnant expectations of \citealt{ZepfWhitmore93}), in line with the broader morphology--density relation \citep{Dressler80}; multi-wavelength studies of compact-group galaxies likewise report altered infrared, UV, star-formation, and stellar-population properties relative to looser environments \citep{Johnson+07,Tzanavaris+10,Bitsakis+11,Coenda+12}, and their H\,{\sc i} deficiency has been organised into an evolutionary sequence \citep{VerdesMontenegro+01}.

The interpretation of such contrasts is nevertheless ambiguous. Projected selection can include chance alignments, and many Hickson-like systems need not be isolated bound entities: apparent compact groups may be transient alignments, dense substructures, or cores embedded in looser groups and richer haloes \citep{Mamon86,DiazGimenez&Mamon10,DiazGimenez+20,Zheng&Shen21,Taverna+23}. The relevant observational question is therefore not only whether compact-group galaxies differ from field galaxies, but whether they remain different from galaxies occupying regular-group analogues selected with comparable luminosity, redshift, and membership constraints. Recent structural studies find evidence for transformation in compact groups, especially non-isolated systems \citep{Montaguth2023,Montaguth2025,Montaguth2026MassSize,Montaguth2026NonIsolated}; \citet{Montaguth2026NonIsolated} compared non-isolated compact-group galaxies with surrounding host populations, but not with ordinary-group quartets reproducing compact-group membership, luminosity-ranking, or projected-core selection.

In our previous work \citep{Tricottet+25}, we found that, once the direct consequences of the compact-group selection are accounted for, compact groups of four galaxies have group-scale properties broadly similar to those of regular groups of four galaxies and to the eligible cores of regular groups. Here we ask how the galaxy-population contrast changes across three ordinary-group comparison samples: genuine four-member groups (\RG{}), the four brightest eligible members of richer groups (\CB{}), and BGG-centred projected cores (\CC{}), the closest analogue of a compact configuration. We adjust for stellar mass, redshift, BGG or satellite status, and group-scale properties, ask how much of each contrast is associated with where the galaxies sit within their groups, and compare compact-group members with the other members of their own host groups. Section~\ref{sec:data} describes the samples, the classifications, and the statistical approach; Sect.~\ref{sec:results} presents the results; Sect.~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses their physical interpretation; and Sect.~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Data and classifications}\label{sec:data}
\subsection{Samples}\label{sec:samples}
We use the compact-group and control samples constructed in our previous study \citep{Tricottet+25}. Galaxy properties are taken from the SDSS DR12 catalogue of \citet{Tempel+17}, while regular groups are drawn from the SDSS DR7 group catalogue of \citet{Lim+17}; the two catalogues were matched through SDSS object identifiers to define the Lim--Tempel working catalogue. Compact groups are selected from the modified Hickson compact-group catalogue of \citet{DiazGimenez+18}: reliable HMCGs with exactly four members, all present in the Lim--Tempel catalogue, satisfying the volume- and luminosity-complete limits $0.005 \le z_{\rm BGG} \le 0.0452$ and $M_{r,\rm BGG}\leq -21.81$, with all four galaxies lying within 3 magnitudes of the brightest group galaxy (BGG). In contrast to our previous study, we remove CGs classified as \emph{split} following \citet{Zheng&Shen21}, which reduces the compact-group sample from <<CG4_Groups_withsplit_N>> to <<CG4_Groups_nonsplit_N>> groups. Following \citet{Zheng&Shen21}, the remaining systems are \emph{isolated} when the four members constitute their whole Lim--Tempel group, \emph{predominant} when they contribute at least half of the host-group $r$-band luminosity, and \emph{embedded} otherwise (\nGrIso, \nGrPre, and \nGrEmb{} systems).
%\footnote{The embedded and predominant labels in \citet{Tricottet+25} were interchanged relative to the \citet{Zheng&Shen21} definitions adopted here. \subsection{Classifications}\label{sec:ssfr_classification}
Star-formation classes are based on SDSS total specific star-formation rates. The inherited DR12 group catalogues store \(\log\mathrm{sSFR}=\texttt{sfr\_tot\_p50}-\texttt{lgm\_tot\_p50}\). In the MPA-JHU catalogues, \(-9999\) is the missing-value sentinel of the \texttt{galSpecExtra} table \citep{Stoughton02,Brinchmann+04,Kauffmann+03b}, so rows with unavailable components are treated as \emph{missing data}: they are excluded from the classification and from every sSFR-based figure and fraction, and are reported as counts only (Table~\ref{tab:sSFR_status}). All quenched and star-forming fractions are computed among classified galaxies. The missing-sSFR incidence is higher in \CG{} (<< CG4_Gals_NNosSFR_pc|fmt(0) >>\% of members, << CG4_BGG_NNosSFR_pc|fmt(0) >>\% of BGGs) than in the reference sample (<< SDSS_NNosSFR_pc|fmt(0) >>\%)<% if d1.get('n_missing') %>. Tracing the stored spectrum identifiers shows why: << d1.get('by_type', {}).get('no-galSpecExtra') >> of the << d1.get('n_missing') >> missing rows are BOSS-instrument spectra, which the MPA-JHU tables (legacy reductions only) never covered, << d1.get('by_type', {}).get('sentinel') >> are legacy spectra whose SFR fit returned the sentinel, and << d1.get('by_type', {}).get('no-specObjID', 0) >> have no spectrum identifier; the missing rows have higher, not lower, median signal-to-noise than the classified ones<% endif %>. They are, however, concentrated among galaxies with a close neighbour: << (100 * crd.missing_data.rates.crowded.missing_ssfr)|fmt(0) >>\% of the galaxies with another galaxy of the \citet{Lim+17} catalogue within 55 arcsec have no sSFR, against << (100 * crd.missing_data.rates.uncrowded.missing_ssfr)|fmt(0) >>\% of the others (Appendix~\ref{app:sdss_query}). Galaxies with a measured sSFR are split into quenched and star-forming classes along the valley between the two populations in the \(\log M_\star\)--\(\log\mathrm{sSFR}\) plane of the reference sample, located with a two-component Gaussian mixture fitted to its binned distribution; the fit, the AGN handling, and alternative classifications are described in Appendix~\ref{app:ssfr_method} (Fig.~\ref{fig:sSFR_classification}).

\subsection{Statistical approach}\label{sec:stats}

Our main question is whether compact-group galaxies retain distinct properties once differences in stellar mass, redshift, BGG or satellite status, and group properties are taken into account. The three controls address physically different comparisons, so we compare \CG{} with each one separately. For each control, binomial logistic models fitted to the galaxies, or to the satellites only, of \CG{} and that control quantify the adjusted E-versus-S and quenched-versus-star-forming contrasts. The covariates are stellar mass, redshift, a BGG/satellite indicator, the total \(r\)-band luminosity of the quartet, and the quartet velocity dispersion \(\sigma_v\); because \(\sigma_v\) from four tracers is noisy, every analysis that uses it is repeated without it (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness_details}). Odds ratios from models with different covariates are not directly comparable, so for the key contrasts we also give the fraction expected for galaxies with the covariate values of the \CG{} galaxies if they followed the relation of the control sample, to be compared with the observed \CG{} fraction (Appendix~\ref{app:stat_conventions}). Within-host models compare compact-group members with their non-compact co-members, and one-to-one matching of galaxies and of quartets is used as a robustness check (Appendix~\ref{app:pooled_models}).

The independence unit is the \emph{physical} Lim group: standard errors are clustered by physical group, and bootstrap intervals resample physical groups, separately within \CG{} and the control, or matched pairs, because the four galaxies of one group are not independent. We report two-sided p-values with effect sizes and confidence intervals for the planned morphology, star-formation, host, and size analyses, and reserve multiplicity control for the exploratory batteries. The p-value conventions, the covariate set, the overlap coefficient, and the treatment of the overlapping control samples in pooled summaries are collected in Appendix~\ref{app:stat_conventions}.

\subsection{An early-type-like satellite excess linked to dense group cores}\label{sec:morph_local_density}

Compact groups of four galaxies host more early-type-like satellites than any of the three ordinary-group comparison samples, while their BGGs resemble those of the controls. The three adjusted contrasts are ordered in the same sense as the characteristic compactness of the controls, with the smallest contrast against the projected cores \CC{}; this is a descriptive ordering, not a formal between-control trend (Sect.~\ref{sec:morphology}). Much of the contrast is positional. Compact-group satellites sit where early-type-like galaxies are common in every group, close to the BGG, and the decline of the E-class fraction with projected distance that we find in all four samples is the morphology--density relation as seen within groups \citep{Postman&Geller84}. At common projected distance a difference remains against all three controls (Sect.~\ref{sec:position}), so compact-group satellites are more early-type-like than control satellites at the same distance from their BGG. Within their own hosts, compact-group members resemble their co-members at the same distance, but that comparison has little leverage because the two populations hardly overlap in radius. The two results would be reconciled if the inner regions of the groups that host compact groups were more evolved than those of typical groups, rather than the compact-group members differing from their immediate neighbours, but the data cannot separate these possibilities. The within-host comparison extends the surrounding-host analysis of \citet{Montaguth2026NonIsolated} by asking whether morphology differs beyond shared host and central location; samples, morphology definitions, and structural measurements differ, so the results are complementary. Present-day stellar mass may itself encode part of a galaxy's environmental history, so fixed-mass estimates describe the remaining association rather than the total accumulated transformation.

The local projected-density comparison of Sect.~\ref{sec:tidal_index} is consistent with this picture without proving it: the tidal-index term is strongly associated with E-class morphology and attenuates the \CG{} coefficient<% if discussion_ell_base.get('status') == 'ok' and discussion_ell_tidal.get('status') == 'ok' %> (from \(<< discussion_ell_base.get('cg4_odds_ratio')|fmt(2) >>\) to \(<< discussion_ell_tidal.get('cg4_odds_ratio')|fmt(2) >>\))<% endif %>, as expected if compact groups select galaxies in locally dense projected configurations where repeated close companions, rather than the catalogue label, are linked to the early-type-like population. Because the index is entangled with the selection and its residual term is not stable on common support, a projected snapshot cannot show that local density drives the morphological difference. This reading is consistent with compact groups tracing the high-density, high-interaction tail of group environments---some genuinely compact, others dense cores or substructures inside larger haloes---and with the stronger structural signatures reported for non-isolated compact groups in recent S-PLUS work \citep{Montaguth2023,Montaguth2025,Montaguth2026MassSize,Montaguth2026NonIsolated}. The \nGrIso{} isolated compact groups have few E-class satellites (Appendix~\ref{app:zheng_shen}), but they are too few to test a contribution from the larger-scale host<% set t7 = referee.get('T7', {}) %><% if t7.get('logM200_sensitivity', {}).get('pooled_elliptical_plus_logM200', {}).get('status') == 'ok' %>, and adding the host halo mass leaves the adjusted contrasts essentially unchanged (Appendix~\ref{app:selection})<% endif %>.

Stellar-population diagnostics could in principle separate an accumulated enrichment history from a shorter star-formation timescale, but we defer a stellar-metallicity analysis for two reasons. The quenching excess at fixed morphology is modest and depends on the control sample (Sect.~\ref{sec:star_formation}), so a strangulation-type metallicity test of the kind proposed by \citet{Peng+15} would have little leverage here, and the single-fibre SDSS spectra sample only the central \({\sim}2\) kpc of the galaxies at the sample redshifts, where the gradients of both metallicity and age are steepest.

\subsection{GZ1 E-class morphologies and transition galaxies}\label{sec:s0_degeneracy}

Our E class is a smooth early-type-like proxy with a substantial S0 content (Sect.~\ref{sec:ssfr_classification}). Galaxy Zoo~1 cannot determine whether the excess comprises S0s, ellipticals, or both; we reserve that question for a dedicated, pre-registered analysis of the DS18 classes by environment. This caveat matters when comparing with the S-PLUS studies of compact-group transition galaxies and \(R_{\rm e}\)--S\'ersic-index trends \citep{Montaguth2023,Montaguth2025,Montaguth2026MassSize,Montaguth2026NonIsolated}: their structural measurements and transition populations probe pathways, whereas our three control samples test which apparent \CG{} signatures survive across genuine four-member, luminosity-selected, and BGG-centred projected-core quartets.

\subsection{Why the star-formation and size signals are weaker}\label{sec:weaker_signals}

Beyond morphology, the evidence for a compact-group effect on star formation is weaker. At fixed morphology and stellar mass the quenched fraction of \CG{} satellites exceeds that of \CB{} satellites, but it is not detectably higher than in the projected cores \CC{} or in \RG{}, and the \CB{} difference weakens at fixed projected distance; the quenched odds ratios of Table~\ref{tab:adjusted_models} therefore combine the different morphology mixes with, at most, a modest and control-dependent excess. The H\(\alpha\) deficit points elsewhere: star-forming-like emission is not rarer in \CG{} than in the controls at fixed mass and morphology, and the missing strong emitters are those with high [N\,{\sc ii}]/H\(\alpha\). In the controls many such spectra are composites or LINER- and Seyfert-like systems, in which star formation, if present, coexists with other sources of ionisation; their scarcity in \CG{} could reflect fewer galaxies caught in that mixed state, weaker nuclear or extended low-ionisation emission, or aperture differences, and single-fibre line ratios cannot decide among these. The spectroscopic measurements are also affected by crowding. \CG{} members have another catalogued galaxy within the 55-arcsec fibre-collision scale about twice as often as control members (<< (100 * crd.crowded_fraction.CG4)|fmt(0) >> against << (100 * crd.crowded_fraction_pooled_controls)|fmt(0) >>\%), such galaxies lack an sSFR about << (mdr.crowded.missing_ssfr / mdr.uncrowded.missing_ssfr)|round|int >> times more often than the others, and companions that never received a spectrum are absent from the catalogues altogether. Assigning the missing galaxies to either extreme class leaves the adjusted contrasts qualitatively unchanged, but short-lived starburst or post-starburst phases in the closest pairs could be under-represented, and the MPA-JHU aperture corrections \citep{Brinchmann+04,Salim+07} may be less secure for interacting or blended members. We therefore read the sSFR and main-sequence results as conservative, not as proof that compact groups leave star formation unaffected.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We have tested whether galaxies in \CG{} compact groups remain special after removing split systems and comparing them with \RG, \CB, and \CC{}. The conclusions apply to this luminous, volume-limited, exactly four-member non-split HMCG sample rather than to all compact groups.

The main result is an early-type-like satellite excess. \CG{} satellites are more often in the GZ1 E class than the satellites of all three regular-group comparisons after accounting for galaxy and group properties; the difference is strongest against \CB{} and \RG{}, weakest against the projected cores \CC{}, and absent for BGGs. Much of it is associated with the central positions of the compact-group satellites: at their projected distances from the BGG, the control samples predict E-class fractions of << rpm.Control4B.log_r.fraction_control_standardised|fmt(2) >>, << rpm.Control4C.log_r.fraction_control_standardised|fmt(2) >>, and << rpm.RG4.log_r.fraction_control_standardised|fmt(2) >>, against the observed << rpm.Control4B.log_r.fraction_cg4|fmt(2) >>. Within their hosts, compact-group members resemble co-members at the same distance, but the two populations overlap too little in radius for that comparison to be decisive. A projected tidal-density proxy likewise attenuates the \CG{} coefficient, but it is entangled with the compact-group selection.

Evidence for additional quenching is weaker and depends on the comparison: at fixed morphology and stellar mass, \CG{} satellites are more often quenched than \CB{} satellites but not detectably more than \CC{} or \RG{} satellites, and star-forming \CG{} galaxies show no robust main-sequence offset. The strong-H\(\alpha\) deficit is a deficit of strong emission with high [N\,{\sc ii}]/H\(\alpha\) rather than of star-forming-like emission, an exploratory result whose physical origin remains open. <% if sz.get('status') == 'ok' %><% set conclusion_adj_sat = sz.get('adjusted', {}).get('satellites', {}) %>At fixed stellar mass the seeing-corrected satellite size estimate is \(<< conclusion_adj_sat.get('cg4_delta_dex')|fmt(3) >>\) dex with an interval including zero, so we do not claim a compact-group size effect. <% endif %>Colours, magnitude gaps, \(D_n4000\), and projected phase-space trends identify no simple alternative driver.

We therefore interpret \CG{} systems as hosting satellites shifted towards early-type-like morphologies, in part as expected for galaxies near the centres of groups but with an excess that remains at fixed projected position, consistent with structural transformation accumulated in dense group cores and with preprocessing. The data do not establish whether this transformation preceded the quenching or accompanied it. Explicit E--S0--spiral classifications, bulge--disc structure, and stellar-population diagnostics will be needed to determine whether the satellite excess reflects disc fading, tidal heating, or merger-driven transformation.

\section{sSFR classification details}\label{app:ssfr_method}

We identify BPT-classifiable galaxies by requiring measured values of both emission-line ratios
\[
\log_{10}\!\bigl([\mathrm{N\,II}]\lambda6584/\mathrm{H}\alpha\bigr)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\log_{10}\!\bigl([\mathrm{O\,III}]\lambda5007/\mathrm{H}\beta\bigr).
\]
Among these galaxies, we flag AGN-like systems if they satisfy either
\[
\log_{10}\frac{[\mathrm{N\,II}]}{\mathrm{H}\alpha} > 0,
\]
or if they lie above the empirical demarcation of \citet{Kauffmann+03a}:
\[
\log_{10}\frac{[\mathrm{O\,III}]}{\mathrm{H}\beta}
> \frac{0.61}{\log_{10}([\mathrm{N\,II}]/\mathrm{H}\alpha)-0.05} \;+\; 1.3,
\]
in which case they are excluded from the calibration sample. This calibration flag is deliberately more inclusive than the three-class BPT scheme of Sect.~\ref{sec:other_properties}, in which composite galaxies lie between the \citet{Kauffmann+03a} and \citet{Kewley+01} demarcations and only galaxies above the \citet{Kewley+01} line are labelled AGN-like, because its sole purpose is to keep possible AGN contamination out of the sSFR calibration. Galaxies with missing diagnostic line ratios are BPT-unclassified, not proven non-AGN; they are retained for the sSFR status assignment when valid SDSS stellar mass and sSFR estimates are available.
Reference-sample rows with unavailable \texttt{specsfr\_tot\_p50}, and group-sample rows with unavailable \texttt{sfr\_tot\_p50} or \texttt{lgm\_tot\_p50}, including the \(-9999\) sentinel, have no valid sSFR estimate; they are treated as missing data throughout: excluded from the mixture fit where applicable, never assigned an sSFR class, omitted from every sSFR figure, and reported as counts only (Sect.~\ref{sec:ssfr_classification}).

\section{Statistical conventions}\label{app:stat_conventions}

\paragraph{Independence unit.} The unit for every inferential contrast among the group catalogues is the \emph{physical} Lim group: standard errors are clustered by Lim group (unifying the overlapping control definitions, with \CG{} systems assigned to their host Lim group, or to their own identifier when they have no Lim counterpart), and bootstrap intervals resample physical groups, separately within \CG{} and the control sample, or matched pairs (quartet matching) and treated compact groups (galaxy matching). Pooled models across the three controls are secondary summaries computed after deduplicating the overlapping control samples to one row per physical galaxy, with the kept label following the priority \RG{} \(>\) \CB{} \(>\) \CC{} (Appendix~\ref{app:pooled_models}). Purely descriptive reference-sample diagnostics report their own unit where they appear.

\paragraph{Tests and p-values.} Descriptive comparisons use two-sided Fisher exact tests for \(2\times2\) tables and rank-sum or bootstrap comparisons for distributions and median differences; Spearman and Pearson coefficients are diagnostics of monotonic or linear trends only. Paired sign-flip permutation p-values use add-one tail counts, \(p = (k+1)/(B+1)\) with \(k\) the number of permutations at least as extreme as the observation; bootstrap sign-crossing p-values are \(2\min[(k_{\le 0}+1)/(B+1),(k_{\ge 0}+1)/(B+1)]\), capped at unity, where \(k_{\le 0}\) and \(k_{\ge 0}\) count resampled differences on either side of zero; displayed p-values are floored at \(10^{-4}\). Adjusted odds ratios come from binomial generalised linear models with cluster-robust covariance; continuous covariates are standardised, so coefficients of continuous terms are per standard deviation of the fitted frame. Multiplicity control is reserved for exploratory screens: Holm adjustment for the colour, measurement-availability, and magnitude-gap screens, and Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate control for the dominance, morphology--environment, and second-order screens.

<% if t3.get('mc_gapper') %>
\paragraph{Covariates.} The adjustment set is fixed: stellar mass, redshift, a BGG/satellite indicator (the only rank information used), the total \(r\)-band luminosity of the quartet, and the quartet velocity dispersion \(\sigma_v\). The velocity dispersion is the gapper estimate \citep{Wainer&Thissen76} on the four selected members' redshifts, converted as \(c\,\sigma_z/(1+\bar z)\), for \emph{all} samples, including \CB{} and \CC{} quartets embedded in richer hosts (this is how the spurious Lim 3688 quartets of Sect.~\ref{sec:samples} reach \({\simeq}2000\kms\)). With four tracers the estimator is intrinsically noisy: in a Gaussian Monte Carlo of \(10^5\) quartets, \(\sigma_{\rm gapper}/\sigma_{\rm true}\) has median << t3['mc_gapper']['median_ratio']|fmt(2) >> with a 16--84\% range of << t3['mc_gapper']['p16_ratio']|fmt(2) >>--<< t3['mc_gapper']['p84_ratio']|fmt(2) >>. The group-level covariates are complete for every quartet, and stellar mass is available for every galaxy with a GZ1 or sSFR class; the pipeline stops rather than dropping a covariate that is less than 65\% complete in a model frame (70\% for matching variables). No group-mass covariate is used (Appendix~\ref{app:selection}).
<% endif %>

\paragraph{Overlap coefficient.} Where two distributions are compared for common support (compactness in Sect.~\ref{sec:samples}, \(\log T_i\) in Sect.~\ref{sec:tidal_index}), the overlap coefficient is the integral of the minimum of the two densities, estimated as 60-bin histograms over the common range; it is 0 for disjoint and 1 for identical distributions.

\paragraph{Fractions at the \CG{} covariates.} For a binomial model fitted to \CG{} and one control, the control fraction at the \CG{} covariates is the mean, over the \CG{} galaxies, of the predicted probability with the \CG{} indicator set to zero; with the indicator set to one, the same average reproduces the observed \CG{} fraction. Their difference is a difference of fractions at the covariate distribution of the \CG{} galaxies, which, unlike an odds ratio, can be compared between models with different covariates. Intervals come from 2000 bootstrap draws that resample physical groups separately within \CG{} and the control, refitting the model at each draw.

The per-galaxy difference between the two measures, \(d = \log R_{e,r} - \log R_{50,r}\), averages \(<< size_md.get('mean_delta_dex')|fmt(3) >>\) dex<% if size_md.get('mean_by_morphology', {}).get('elliptical') and size_md.get('mean_by_morphology', {}).get('spiral') %>, and is larger for GZ1 E-class galaxies (\(<< size_md.get('mean_by_morphology', {}).get('elliptical', {}).get('mean')|fmt(3) >>\) dex) than for S-class galaxies (\(<< size_md.get('mean_by_morphology', {}).get('spiral', {}).get('mean')|fmt(3) >>\) dex), as expected if the Petrosian aperture preferentially truncates concentrated profiles<% endif %>. <% if size_md.get('spearman_vs_neighbour_separation') %>Its rank correlation with the nearest-neighbour separation is \(\rho_\mathrm{S} = << (size_md.get('spearman_vs_neighbour_separation', {}).get('rho', 0) + 0)|fmt(3) >>\) (<< pval_text(size_md.get('spearman_vs_neighbour_separation', {}).get('p')) >>), so residual blending does not drive a strong measure-dependent size systematic.<% endif %>
<% endif %>

<% if size_tidal.get('status') == 'ok' and size_tidal.get('baseline', {}).get('status') == 'ok' and size_tidal.get('with_tidal_index', {}).get('status') == 'ok' %>
Adding the pairwise projected tidal index of Sect.~\ref{sec:tidal_index} to the all-galaxy size model changes the \CG{} coefficient from \(<< size_tidal.get('baseline', {}).get('cg4_delta_dex')|fmt(3) >>\) dex to \(<< size_tidal.get('with_tidal_index', {}).get('cg4_delta_dex')|fmt(3) >>\) dex on the pairwise complete-case sample<% if szv.get('absorbed_by_tidal_index') %>; the local projected-density proxy attenuates the size coefficient in the same way it attenuates the morphology coefficient<% else %>; with a near-null baseline coefficient, the attenuation diagnostic used for morphology is not informative here<% endif %>.
<% endif %>

<% if size_radial_cg.get('status') == 'ok' and size_radial_ctrl.get('status') == 'ok' %>
The radial diagnostic is exploratory: mass- and redshift-adjusted satellite size residuals correlate with projected BGG-centric distance with \(\rho_\mathrm{S} = << size_radial_cg.get('spearman_rho')|fmt(2) >>\) (<< pval_text(size_radial_cg.get('spearman_p')) >>) in \CG{} and \(\rho_\mathrm{S} = << size_radial_ctrl.get('spearman_rho')|fmt(2) >>\) (<< pval_text(size_radial_ctrl.get('spearman_p')) >>) in the pooled controls. A more negative inner-region residual would be the tidal-truncation expectation; the observed trends are similar in \CG{} and the controls, so the data do not isolate a \CG{}-specific inner-region size suppression.
