\section{Data}
\subsection{Samples}
We use the compact-group and control samples constructed in our previous study \cite{Tricottet+25}.
We briefly recall their construction here. Galaxy properties are taken from the SDSS DR12 catalogue
of \citet{Tempel+17}, while regular groups are drawn from the SDSS DR7 group catalogue of \citet{Lim+17}; the two
catalogues were matched through SDSS object identifiers to define the Lim--Tempel working catalogue. Compact groups
are selected from the modified Hickson compact-group catalogue of \citet{DiazGimenez+18}. Starting from the reliable
HMCGs with exactly four members, we retain only systems in which all galaxies are present in the Lim--Tempel catalogue and
impose the volume- and luminosity-complete limits $0.005 < z_{\rm BGG} < 0.0452$ and $M_{r,\rm BGG}\leq -21.81$, with
all 4 galaxies lying within 3 magnitudes of the brightest group galaxy. This defines the parent \CG{} sample of
78 compact groups. The regular-group comparison samples are built from Lim--Tempel groups satisfying the same redshift
and BGG-luminosity limits, with at least 4 members and at least 3 satellites within 3 magnitudes of the BGG.
From this parent control catalogue, we define \RG{} groups as regular groups with exactly four members, excluding
systems identical to a \CG{}, and we additionally use the \CB{} and \CC{} samples, formed respectively from
the 4 brightest galaxies and from the BGG plus its 3 closest projected companions in each parent regular group,
after removing groups that share galaxies with \CG{}s.
In contrast to our previous study, we remove CGs classified as \emph{split} following \cite{Zheng&Shen21}.
This reduces the compact-group sample from <<CG4_Groups_withsplit_N>> to
<<CG4_Groups_nonsplit_N>> groups. We also remove the systems associated with Lim group 3688 from our \CB{} and \CC{} samples. This group contains 6 galaxies
with redshifts between 0.032 and 0.034, and 1 with a redshift of 0.048. This group appears to be spurious, and the
outlying galaxy, although not the BGG, is selected in both our original \CB{} and \CC{} samples, significantly affecting the group velocity
dispersion ($1979$ and $1981\, \kms$, respectively).

We use SDSS DR <<DATA_RELEASE>> to retrieve stellar masses, star formation rates (SFRs), and Galaxy Zoo morphologies
\citep{GalaxyZoo1}. We specifically extract the columns \texttt{sfr\_tot\_p50},
\texttt{specsfr\_tot\_p50}, and \texttt{lgm\_tot\_p50} from the \texttt{galSpecExtra} table, and
\texttt{p\_el\_debiased} and \texttt{p\_cs\_debiased} from the \texttt{zooSpec} table.

We select non-AGN galaxies by first placing them on the classical BPT diagnostic of \cite{Veilleux87} and requiring measured values of both emission-line ratios
\[
\log_{10}\!\bigl([\mathrm{N\,II}]\lambda6584/\mathrm{H}\alpha\bigr)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\log_{10}\!\bigl([\mathrm{O\,III}]\lambda5007/\mathrm{H}\beta\bigr).
\]
A galaxy is flagged as an AGN if it satisfies either
\[
\log_{10}\frac{[\mathrm{N\,II}]}{\mathrm{H}\alpha} > 0,
\]
or if it lies above the empirical demarcation of \cite{Kauffmann+03a}:
\[
\log_{10}\frac{[\mathrm{O\,III}]}{\mathrm{H}\beta}
> \frac{0.61}{\log_{10}([\mathrm{N\,II}]/\mathrm{H}\alpha)-0.05} \;+\; 1.3,
\]
in which case it is removed from the non-AGN sample.  All remaining galaxies—including those with missing line ratios—are retained as non-AGN.
The non-AGN selection process is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:BPT}.


\begin{figure}
    \centering
     \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{BPT_diagram.pdf}
     \caption{BPT diagram showing our separation between non-AGN and AGN galaxies.}
     \label{fig:BPT}
\end{figure}


Galaxies with \texttt{specsfr\_tot\_p50} equal to -9999 in the SDSS are classified as quenched
(for display purposes, they are assigned the arbitrary low value $10^{<<sSFR_QUENCHED>>}$ Gyr\(^{-1}\)).
The remaining galaxies are separated into
star-forming and passive populations using a two‐component Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) in the \(\log M_*\)–\(\log\mathrm{sSFR}\) plane.
Its parameters are determined by minimizing the Kullback–Leibler divergence between the empirical and model densities
(\cite{Kullback1951,McLachlanPeel2000}). For any trial parameter vector \(\boldsymbol{\theta}\), we reconstruct the mixture means
\(\{\boldsymbol{\mu}_i\}\), covariances \(\{\mathbf{\Sigma}_i\}\), and weights \(\{w_i\}\) via a Cholesky‐like decomposition and
logistic mapping, optionally constraining the second component’s mean to isolate the passive population (\cite{Dempster1977}).
The KL divergence is estimated by constructing a 2D histogram of the data and summing \(p_\mathrm{data}\ln(p_\mathrm{data}/p_\mathrm{GMM})\),
with a small regularization \(\epsilon\) to avoid singularities (\cite{Tojeiro2009,Bisigello2018}). We optimize \(\boldsymbol{\theta}\)
via L-BFGS-B (with fallback to Nelder–Mead) over multiple guided and random initializations, selecting the solution with lowest divergence
(\cite{Pedregosa2011,Bishop2006}). Initial means are seeded by a median split in \(\log\mathrm{sSFR}\) to improve convergence and robustness
against local minima (\cite{Rousseeuw1987}). In the final fit, one component naturally aligns with the high-\(\mathrm{sSFR}\) “star‐forming
sequence,” while the other captures the low-\(\mathrm{sSFR}\) “passive” cloud, providing a data‐driven division that agrees with previous
multi‐Gaussian decompositions of the SFR–\(M_*\) plane (\cite{Wuyts2011,Hahn2019}). The decision boundaries, defined as the
loci where the posterior probabilities of adjacent components are equal, provide an objective criterion for
delineating star‐forming from passive galaxies.

\subsection{Morphology}
Morphologies are determined via the Galaxy Zoo citizen‐science decision tree, in which each galaxy image receives multiple
independent volunteer classifications that are aggregated into debiased vote fractions \citep{GalaxyZoo1}. Those fractions are provided in the
\texttt{zooSpec} table of the SDSS database through the \texttt{p\_el\_debiased} and \texttt{p\_cs\_debiased} columns. We classify a galaxy as
``elliptical'' if \texttt{p\_el\_debiased} is greater than 0.5, and as ``spiral'' if \texttt{p\_cs\_debiased} is greater than 0.5.
All other cases are classified as ``uncertain''. The morphologies obtained for each sample are shown in Table~\ref{tab:morphologies}.
Barnard's two-sided exact tests were used to compare the fraction of each morphology between \CG{} and each control sample. The resulting p-values are
<<pval_Control4B_Elliptical_vs_CG_pc>> for ellipticals and <<pval_Control4B_Spiral_vs_CG_pc>> for spirals versus the \CB{} sample,
 <<pval_Control4C_Elliptical_vs_CG_pc>>  and <<pval_Control4C_Spiral_vs_CG_pc>>  versus \CC{} and  <<pval_RG4_Elliptical_vs_CG_pc>>
 and <<pval_RG4_Spiral_vs_CG_pc>>  versus \RG. The galaxies in \CG{} systems, which we identified as cores of richer groups in
 \cite{Tricottet+25}, have morphology distributions that differ significantly from those of ordinary four-member groups. Figure~\ref{fig:galaxies_morph_sfr} displays sSFR versus stellar mass for galaxies in the SDSS selection and in \CG{}.

\section{Relation to recent structural studies of compact-group galaxies}

A complementary interpretation of the present results is provided by the recent series of studies by
\citet{Montaguth2023,Montaguth2025,Montaguth2026NonIsolated,Montaguth2026MassSize}.
Using multiwavelength S-PLUS imaging and single-S\'ersic structural fits, these authors identified a population of
``transition'' galaxies, broadly corresponding to red systems with relatively low or intermediate S\'ersic index, whose
location in the $R_{\rm e}$--$n$ plane suggests ongoing structural transformation in compact groups. In particular,
\citet{Montaguth2023} found that compact-group transition galaxies include a population that is smaller and more
centrally concentrated than analogous field galaxies, while \citet{Montaguth2025} showed that this signature is
stronger in non-isolated compact groups embedded in larger structures. \citet{Montaguth2026NonIsolated} further
compared non-isolated compact groups with their surrounding host-group galaxies and found that the compact-group
members display higher quenched fractions, a larger early-type fraction, and distinct transition-galaxy
($R_{\rm e}$--$n$) distributions. Finally, \citet{Montaguth2026MassSize} showed that the environmental dependence of the
stellar mass–size relation is weak for early- and late-type galaxies, but strongest for transition galaxies, whose
mass–size slopes and S\'ersic-index distributions become more early-type-like in groups and compact groups.

Our analysis addresses a related but stricter question. Rather than comparing compact groups primarily with the
field, or non-isolated compact groups with their immediate host structures, we ask whether compact-group galaxies
remain different from galaxies in regular four-member group analogues after controlling for stellar mass, redshift,
rank, and available group-scale quantities. In this framework, the most robust residual signal is morphological:
compact-group membership remains associated with an enhanced probability of being classified as elliptical and a
reduced probability of being classified as spiral. By contrast, the passive and star-forming satellite fractions are
more sensitive to the adopted control sample and to the adjustment or matching procedure.

The two sets of results are therefore not contradictory. The structural work of
\citet{Montaguth2023,Montaguth2025,Montaguth2026NonIsolated,Montaguth2026MassSize} identifies the likely
nature of the transformation: disk fading, increasing central concentration, and/or bulge growth in galaxies occupying
an intermediate region between blue late-type disks and red early-type systems. Our results indicate that, when
compact groups are compared with regular-group analogues rather than with the field alone, the clearest surviving
signature is the accumulated morphological state of the galaxy population, whereas an instantaneous star-formation
offset is less robust. This suggests that compact groups should not be interpreted simply as environments with
ongoing enhanced quenching at the present epoch. They are more plausibly very dense regions in which past
interactions, local tidal density, and preprocessing in larger haloes have already shifted part of the galaxy population
toward earlier morphologies.

A useful way to reconcile these views is to regard the compact-group signal as primarily structural and cumulative.
In that picture, the compact configuration may accelerate morphological transformation, but some of the observed
differences may also reflect the fact that many compact groups are the dense cores or substructures of larger systems.
Future work combining the structural diagnostics of S-PLUS-like analyses, especially $R_{\rm e}$, S\'ersic index,
transition-galaxy classifications, and mass–size residuals, with the matched regular-group control framework used
here would provide a direct test of whether the compact-group transformation is unique to compact configurations
or is the high-density limit of more general group preprocessing.

\section{Conclusion}

After removing systems classified as split, we find that compact groups differ from regular four-member groups mainly through their galaxy population mix. Galaxies in CG$_4$ are more frequently elliptical and less frequently spiral than galaxies in RG$_4$, with highly significant differences in both fractions. This morphological difference is also present among BGGs: CG$_4$ BGGs are more often elliptical than RG$_4$ BGGs.

The star-formation difference is more specific. CG$_4$ satellites are significantly less frequently star-forming than RG$_4$ satellites, while the star-forming fraction of BGGs does not differ significantly between the two samples. Moreover, among galaxies that remain star-forming, CG$_4$ galaxies do not show a significant offset from RG$_4$ galaxies relative to the star-forming main sequence.

The colour analysis does not indicate a simple uniform reddening of compact-group galaxies. <% if r.Colour_robustness_summary.baseline_blue_offset -%>The pooled mass- and redshift-adjusted comparison supports a blue CG$_4$ satellite offset.<% else -%>The pooled mass- and redshift-adjusted comparison does not support a statistically robust blue CG$_4$ satellite offset.<% endif %> <% if r.Colour_robustness_summary.full_model_blue_offset -%>Negative coefficients appear after adding morphology and sSFR class, but<% else -%>The coefficients remain non-significant after adding morphology and sSFR class, and<% endif %> the result is <% if r.Colour_robustness_summary.control_dependent -%>sensitive to the choice of control catalogue<% else -%>consistent across the control catalogues<% endif %>. Neither the sSFR-class nor morphology splits isolate a significant effect among star-forming spirals. The radial residual trends instead indicate that outer CG$_4$ satellites are bluer than inner satellites, consistent with stronger processing toward the BGG. The BGG and mean satellite colours are correlated, but <% if r.Colour_bgg_satellite_significant_slope_count == 0 -%>the corresponding CG$_4$ relations do not differ significantly from those of the control catalogues.<% else -%>some of the corresponding CG$_4$ relations differ significantly from those of the control catalogues.<% endif %> <% if r.Colour_bgg_domination_significant_adjusted|length == 0 -%>Likewise, the apparent colour difference between dominated and non-dominated BGGs disappears after controlling for stellar mass.<% else -%>Some colour differences between dominated and non-dominated BGGs remain significant after controlling for stellar mass.<% endif %>

The most robust compact-group signal is morphological rather than an instantaneous star-formation offset. After controlling for stellar mass, redshift, rank and available group-scale properties, CG$_4$ membership remains associated with enhanced elliptical and reduced spiral probabilities, whereas passive status and satellite star-forming status do not remain significant in the corresponding adjusted models. The matched-control analysis supports the same morphological excess and gives only borderline evidence for an enhanced passive fraction.

The additional diagnostics do not favour a simple fossil-group or AGN-driven explanation. The magnitude-gap distributions of CG$_4$ and the pooled controls are statistically similar, and AGN-like activity is not independently enhanced after mass and environment adjustment. The limited H\(\alpha\) diagnostic shows a lower strong-emission fraction in CG$_4$ than in the control catalogues, but the absence of both \(D_n4000\) and H\(\delta_A\) prevents a direct post-starburst or recently-quenched classification.

Taken together, these results suggest that compact groups host a more morphologically evolved galaxy population, with an enhanced fraction of early-type galaxies and a reduced fraction of star-forming satellites. Because the passive CG$_4$ signal does not remain significant in both the adjusted and matched analyses, the apparent star-formation specialness is at least partly explained by environment, mass/rank mix, or pre-processing in dense halo cores. The morphology signal is more robust, but the projected tidal-index experiment suggests that local pairwise density may absorb part of that signal, so this result should be interpreted as evidence for accumulated transformation in very dense environments rather than as a uniquely isolated compact-group effect.
