\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Given their high apparent densities and low velocity dispersions \citep{Hickson82,Hickson+92}, compact groups appear to be ideal sites for the frequent tidal encounters and mergers that alter galaxy masses, luminosities, sizes, morphologies, and star-formation rates (e.g., \citealt{Mamon92}). \citet{MendesdeOliveira&Hickson94} provided the classic report of an excess of early-type galaxies in compact groups relative to the field (building on the photometric morphologies of \citealt{HKA89} and the merger-remnant expectations of \citealt{ZepfWhitmore93}), in line with the broader morphology--density relation \citep{Dressler80}. Comparisons with loose groups and the field have since found broad differences: \citet{Coenda+12} found higher fractions of red and early-type galaxies in SDSS compact groups than in loose groups and the field, with smaller, more concentrated galaxies of higher surface brightness; multi-wavelength studies likewise report altered infrared, UV, star-formation, and stellar-population properties \citep{Johnson+07,Tzanavaris+10,Bitsakis+11}, and H\,{\sc i} deficiency has been organised into an evolutionary sequence \citep{VerdesMontenegro+01}.

The interpretation of such contrasts is nevertheless ambiguous. Projected selection can include chance alignments, and many Hickson-like systems need not be isolated bound entities: apparent compact groups may be transient alignments, dense substructures, or cores embedded in looser groups and richer haloes \citep{Mamon86,DiazGimenez&Mamon10,DiazGimenez+20,Zheng&Shen21,Taverna+23}. The relevant observational question is therefore not only whether compact-group galaxies differ from field galaxies, but whether compact-group satellites differ from those of ordinary-group cores of similar compactness, selected with comparable luminosity, redshift, and membership constraints. Structural studies with the Southern Photometric Local Universe Survey (S-PLUS) find evidence for transformation in compact groups, especially non-isolated systems \citep{Montaguth2023,Montaguth2025,Montaguth2026MassSize,Montaguth2026NonIsolated}; \citet{Montaguth2026NonIsolated} compared non-isolated compact-group galaxies with the other galaxies of their host structures, but not with ordinary-group quartets reproducing compact-group membership, luminosity ranking, or projected-core selection.

In \citet[hereafter Paper~I]{Tricottet+25}, we found that, once the direct consequences of the compact-group selection are accounted for, compact groups of four galaxies (\CG) have group-scale properties broadly similar to those of ordinary groups of four galaxies and to the eligible cores of ordinary groups. Here we ask how the galaxy-population contrast changes across three ordinary-group comparison samples: regular (ordinary) groups with exactly four members (\RG{}), the four brightest eligible members of groups with at least four members (\CB{}), and projected cores centred on the brightest group galaxy (BGG; \CC{}), the closest analogue of a compact configuration. By separating satellites from BGGs and comparing both full samples and quartets of similar projected separation, we test whether compact-group satellites differ from those of ordinary-group cores of similar compactness. Section~\ref{sec:data} describes the samples, the classifications, and the statistical approach; Sect.~\ref{sec:results} presents the results; Sect.~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses their physical interpretation; and Sect.~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Data and classifications}\label{sec:data}
\subsection{Samples}\label{sec:samples}
We use the compact-group and control samples constructed in Paper~I. Galaxy properties are taken from the catalogue of \citet{Tempel+17}, based on SDSS DR12 \citep{York+00,Alam+15}, while ordinary groups are drawn from the catalogue of \citet{Lim+17}, built on SDSS DR7 \citep{Abazajian+09} with the halo-based group finder of \citet{Yang+07}; the two catalogues were matched through SDSS object identifiers to define the Lim--Tempel working catalogue. Compact groups are selected from the modified Hickson compact-group (HMCG) catalogue of \citet{DiazGimenez+18}: reliable HMCGs with exactly four members, all present in the Lim--Tempel catalogue, satisfying the volume and BGG-luminosity limits $0.005 \le z_{\rm BGG} \le 0.0452$ and $M_{r,\rm BGG}\leq -21.81$. HMCG membership uses the Hickson three-magnitude concordance in apparent $r$ magnitude, while control eligibility uses catalogue absolute magnitudes (Appendix~\ref{app:cg_core_overlap}). Following \citet{Zheng&Shen21}, a compact group is \emph{split} if its four members do not all belong to a single Lim--Tempel group; removing split systems leaves <<CG4_Groups_nonsplit_N>> of the initial <<CG4_Groups_withsplit_N>> compact groups, which are \emph{isolated} when the four members constitute their whole Lim--Tempel group, \emph{predominant} when they contribute at least half of the host-group $r$-band luminosity, and \emph{embedded} otherwise (\nGrIso, \nGrPre, and \nGrEmb{} systems).
\footnote{The embedded and predominant assignments were interchanged in parts of Paper~I, including its Sect.~3.3. We correct the inherited catalogue labels to the definitions adopted here. Statements about embedded systems in Paper~I apply to predominant systems and vice versa; its results for isolated systems and for the full sample, which do not use these two labels, are unaffected.}

The three control samples are built from Lim--Tempel groups satisfying the same redshift and BGG-luminosity limits, with at least four members and at least three satellites within 3 absolute magnitudes of the BGG, and serve different purposes (Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic}). \RG{} contains the regular (ordinary) groups with exactly four members and is the most direct equal-membership comparison. \CB{} contains the four brightest eligible members of ordinary groups with at least four members and tests whether the \CG{} population resembles the luminous core of an ordinary group. \CC{} contains the BGG and its three closest projected companions among members within 3 magnitudes of the BGG; as in the Paper~I definition, the magnitude criterion is applied before companions are ranked by projected distance. Selecting the nearest companions makes \CC{} a projected-core sample, without imposing the surface-density requirement of the compact-group catalogue. <% set core0 = r.get('cg4_pc_quartet_overlap', {}).get('non_isolated_projected_core', {}) %>In << core0['n_exact_four_member_identity'] >> of << core0['n_non_isolated'] >> non-isolated compact groups, all four members coincide with the candidate \CC{} core of the same host before exclusion (Appendix~\ref{app:cg_core_overlap}). \subsection{Classifications}\label{sec:ssfr_classification}
Star-formation classes are based on the MPA-JHU total specific star-formation rates \citep{Brinchmann+04,Kauffmann+03b} stored in the DR12 group catalogues. Rows without a valid estimate are treated as \emph{missing data}: they are excluded from every sSFR-based fraction and figure and reported as counts only (Table~\ref{tab:sSFR_status}), so all quenched and star-forming fractions are computed among classified galaxies. \subsection{Statistical approach}\label{sec:stats}

Our main question is whether compact-group galaxies retain distinct properties once differences in stellar mass, redshift, BGG/satellite status, and group properties are taken into account. The three controls address physically different comparisons, so we compare \CG{} with each one separately. Galaxy-level logistic models quantify the adjusted E-versus-S and quenched-versus-star-forming contrasts; group-level matching compares the satellite mix of whole quartets; and within-host models compare compact-group members with their non-compact co-members. The covariates are stellar mass, redshift, BGG/satellite status, quartet luminosity (the summed \(r\)-band luminosity of the four members), and the quartet velocity dispersion \(\sigma_v\); because \(\sigma_v\) from four tracers is noisy, every analysis that uses it is repeated without it (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness_details}).

We account for dependence among galaxies in the same group by clustering standard errors at the level of the \emph{physical} Lim group and resampling by physical group or matched pair, as appropriate. We report two-sided p-values with effect sizes and confidence intervals for the primary morphology, star-formation, matching, within-host, and size analyses, and reserve multiple-testing correction for the secondary diagnostics. The p-value conventions, the completeness requirement for the control variables, the overlap coefficient, and the treatment of the overlapping control samples in pooled summaries are collected in Appendix~\ref{app:stat_conventions}.

\subsection{An early-type-like satellite excess linked to dense group cores}\label{sec:morph_local_density}
<% set dsub = (freeze if freeze else {}).get('submission', {}) %><% set ddrop = dsub.get('paired', {}).get('differences', {}).get('full-separate_slopes', {}) %><% set dbw = dsub.get('below_window', {}).get('samples', {}) %><% set bpd = subm.get('battery_per_dex', {}) %>

The morphology signature lies primarily in the satellites. Its persistence after stellar-mass adjustment indicates differences beyond the present-day mass distributions, although mass itself may record earlier environmental evolution. BGGs offer less contrast: the luminous-BGG selection places them at high masses where most are already early-type and quenched (Tables~\ref{tab:sSFR_status} and \ref{tab:morphologies}).

\citet{Martinez+13} compared compact and loose groups of similar total luminosity. Compact-group BGGs were brighter and more often Galaxy Zoo ellipticals overall, but at fixed BGG magnitude the elliptical fractions were comparable within errors, whereas colour- and concentration-based early-type fractions remained higher in compact groups. Our weak adjusted BGG contrast resembles their fixed-magnitude Galaxy Zoo result; their colour and concentration comparisons show how much depends on the early-type definition.

Most of the satellite excess over \CC{} reflects the larger separations of the ordinary-group cores (Fig.~\ref{fig:compactness}, Table~\ref{tab:group_match_per_control}). Restricting \CC{} to the separation range of \CG{} removes about << (100 * ddrop.get('estimate', 0) / cpt['full']['difference'])|round(-1)|int >>\% of it (paired reduction \(<< sgn(ddrop.get('estimate')) >>\) points, \(<< pmath(ddrop.get('p_cross', 1)) >>\)), and neither host richness nor host luminosity accounts for the remainder. At matched separation, the contrast is not distinguishable from zero, although its interval still admits an excess of << cpt['separate_slopes']['high']|fmt(0) >> points, and the << dbw.get('CG4', {}).get('quartets') >> most compact \CG{} quartets lie below the common range, where both samples have high E-class fractions but few hosts. At fixed stellar mass, smaller separations accompany higher E-class probabilities within \CG{}, \CB{}, and \CC{} (Table~\ref{tab:battery})<% if bpd.get('cg4_vs_control4c_slope') %>, and the per-dex slopes of \CG{} and \CC{} are compatible (\(p \approx << bpd['cg4_vs_control4c_slope']['p']|fmt(2) >>\))<% endif %>. Pre-processing in the host halo may build up an early-type satellite population before the compact configuration is observed. Radial segregation could raise the fraction of these satellites in dense cores, where dynamical friction favours the inward migration of more massive satellites and earlier arrivals have had more time to orbit and interact. Repeated tidal encounters, galaxy harassment, and minor mergers may heat or restructure discs and contribute to the S0 population, consistent with the structural trends reported in S-PLUS \citep{Montaguth2023,Montaguth2025,Montaguth2026MassSize,Montaguth2026NonIsolated}. As discussed in Sect.~5.2 of Paper~I, chance alignments of host members can give a small projected separation even for galaxies far apart in three dimensions; they can dilute a signal produced by close encounters, while their shared host can still contribute a morphological excess.

The shared-host models address location inside the same halo. \citet{Montaguth2026NonIsolated} found that non-isolated compact groups host more early-type and quenched galaxies than the other galaxies of their host structures, especially above \(\log(M_\star/\msun)=10.2\). Our within-host comparison agrees when radius is ignored<% if t2_ell_nr_cl.get('status') == 'ok' %> (odds ratio \(<< t2_ell_nr_cl.get('odds_ratio')|fmt(2) >>\), \([<< t2_ell_nr_cl.get('ci95')[0]|fmt(2) >>, << t2_ell_nr_cl.get('ci95')[1]|fmt(2) >>]\))<% endif %>, but adjusting for BGG-centric radius lowers the estimate to \(<< host_ell.get('cg_member_odds_ratio')|fmt(2) >>\) (\([<< host_ell.get('cg_member_ci95')[0]|fmt(2) >>, << host_ell.get('cg_member_ci95')[1]|fmt(2) >>]\)), which rests on only << t2rs['n_hosts_with_overlap'] >> hosts with radial overlap between member and non-member satellites (Sect.~\ref{sec:morphology}); position within the host may therefore account for part of that contrast. Our explicit quartet controls complement their approach by showing how the comparison itself changes the inferred signature.

\subsection{GZ1 E-class morphologies and transition galaxies}\label{sec:s0_degeneracy}
<% set s42 = sz.get('morphology_strata', {}) %>

Our E class is a smooth early-type-like proxy with a substantial S0 contribution (Sect.~\ref{sec:ssfr_classification}). The DS18 cross-match covers << t10_match.get('deduplicated_controls', {}).get('fraction_matched')|pct(0) >>--<< t10_match.get('CG4', {}).get('fraction_matched')|pct(0) >>\% of the sample, and \(P_{\rm S0}\) distinguishes S0s from ellipticals only for matched DR7 galaxies with \(T\mathrm{Type}\leq0\). Within that early-type subset, the GZ1 classification of S0s depends strongly on inclination, and splitting the sample by control and environment leaves too few galaxies to estimate the elliptical and S0 contributions reliably (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness_details}). \citet{Montaguth2026MassSize} found in S-PLUS that early-type galaxies follow environment-independent mass--size relations, that late types in compact groups are only modestly smaller at fixed mass, and that transition galaxies (red discs) are smaller in groups than in the field. Our size offsets by morphology agree<% if s42.get('elliptical_satellites', {}).get('status') == 'ok' and s42.get('spiral_satellites', {}).get('status') == 'ok' %>: at fixed stellar mass, \CG{} E-class satellites differ from the controls by \(<< sgn(s42['elliptical_satellites']['cg4_delta_dex'], 3) >>\)~dex and S-class satellites by \(<< sgn(s42['spiral_satellites']['cg4_delta_dex'], 3) >>\)~dex, neither significantly (Appendix~\ref{app:size_details})<% endif %>. Their hybrid structural--colour classes and size definitions differ from our GZ1 E/S classes and seeing-corrected Simard radii, and our E class mixes ellipticals with S0s, so a transition-galaxy size deficit need not appear in our classes.

\subsection{Star formation and sizes follow morphology}\label{sec:weaker_signals}

Morphology accounts for part of the full-sample quenching contrast, and the residual at fixed morphology is poorly constrained (Sect.~\ref{sec:star_formation}). A clear morphological excess with a weak residual quenching signal is compatible with earlier processing in the host. If disc transformation and the decline in star formation occurred on overlapping timescales, the present-day fractions can retain a shift in the mix of morphological classes even when the quenching fractions within each class are similar. Constraining the relative timescales would require spectroscopic reconstructions of individual star-formation histories.

Fibre H\(\alpha\) equivalent width measures emission relative to the central stellar continuum, with possible contributions from AGN, shocks, and old stars, whereas MPA-JHU total sSFRs use photometric aperture corrections and statistical estimates for weak-line systems \citep{Brinchmann+04,Salim+07}. The full-sample H\(\alpha\) deficit is not an aperture or redshift effect, but its significance against \CC{} depends on the emission-line join, morphology removes about half of it for satellites, and fixing the separation removes no more (Sect.~\ref{sec:other_properties}). In the intermediate-mass bin, excluding AGN and composites weakens the deficit, and both separation models give total-sSFR quenched contrasts spanning zero. \section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
<% set cdrop = ddrop %><% set c4c_rows = freeze['primary_morphology'] %><% set c_sat_or = [c4c_rows['Control4B']['satellites']['cg4_odds_ratio'], c4c_rows['Control4C']['satellites']['cg4_odds_ratio'], c4c_rows['RG4']['satellites']['cg4_odds_ratio']] %><% set c_ht = dsub.get('paired', {}).get('host_terms', {}) %>

We have compared << CG4_Groups_nonsplit_N >> low-redshift, luminous, non-split compact groups of four galaxies with three ordinary-group controls: groups with exactly four members, quartets of the four brightest eligible members, and BGG-centred projected cores. Our main results are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Morphology: \CG{} satellites are more often in the GZ1 E class than the satellites of all three full controls, with adjusted odds ratios of << c_sat_or|min|fmt(2) >>--<< c_sat_or|max|fmt(2) >> (Table~\ref{tab:adjusted_models}). The excess persists across vote thresholds, an independent deep-learning classification, S\'ersic indices, crowding cuts, and the omission of \(\sigma_v\), for all galaxies and for satellites alone (Table~\ref{tab:morph_robustness}).
\item Compactness: against projected cores, the full-sample excess of \(<< sgn(cpt['full']['difference']) >>\) points falls to \(<< sgn(cpt['window_no_separation']['difference']) >>\) points in the common separation range and to << ppci(cpt['separate_slopes'], label='') >> at << cpt['centre_kpc']|fmt(0) >>~kpc. The reduction, \(<< sgn(cdrop.get('estimate')) >>\) points (\(<< pmath(cdrop.get('p_cross', 1)) >>\)), comes mainly from the widely separated \CC{} cores and is not explained by host richness or luminosity; separation-matched quartets give \(<< sgn(cpt['matched_sigma']['difference'], 0) >>\) to \(<< sgn(cpt['matched']['difference'], 0) >>\) points. The << dbw.get('CG4', {}).get('quartets') >> compact groups below << cpt['window_kpc'][0]|fmt(0) >>~kpc show no detected excess but are too few to constrain the densest regime.
\item Within hosts: compact-group members of predominant and embedded systems are more often in the E class than the other members of their hosts when radius is ignored (odds ratio << t2_ell_nr_cl.get('odds_ratio')|fmt(2) >>), but only << t2rs['n_hosts_with_overlap'] >> hosts offer radial overlap, and the radius-adjusted estimate (<< host_ell.get('cg_member_odds_ratio')|fmt(2) >>) is not significant.
\item Star formation and sizes: quenching shows no clear excess at fixed morphology. The fibre H\(\alpha\) deficit is halved by morphology, does not decrease further at fixed separation, and its significance depends on the emission-line join. Satellite sizes show no offset at fixed stellar mass (\(<< sz.get('adjusted', {}).get('satellites', {}).get('cg4_delta_dex')|fmt(3) >>\)~dex).
\end{enumerate}
These results answer the question posed in the Introduction. Paper~I found that, once the selection is accounted for, the group-scale properties of compact groups resemble those of ordinary groups and of their cores; their satellite populations behave alike. Compact groups are special mainly in the way dense group cores are special: their satellites are more often early-type than those of ordinary groups as a whole, but most of that difference is shared by ordinary-group cores of similar compactness. A compact-group-specific residual of up to << cpt['separate_slopes']['high']|fmt(0) >> points at fixed separation, or an excess confined to the densest systems, cannot be excluded with the present samples.

\section{sSFR classification details}\label{app:ssfr_method}

We identify BPT-classifiable galaxies by requiring measured values of both emission-line ratios
\[
\log_{10}\bigl([\mathrm{N\,II}]\lambda6584/\mathrm{H}\alpha\bigr)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\log_{10}\bigl([\mathrm{O\,III}]\lambda5007/\mathrm{H}\beta\bigr).
\]
Among these galaxies, we flag AGN-like systems if they satisfy either
\[
\log_{10}\frac{[\mathrm{N\,II}]}{\mathrm{H}\alpha} > 0,
\]
or if they lie above the empirical demarcation of \citet{Kauffmann+03a}:
\[
\log_{10}\frac{[\mathrm{O\,III}]}{\mathrm{H}\beta}
> \frac{0.61}{\log_{10}([\mathrm{N\,II}]/\mathrm{H}\alpha)-0.05} \;+\; 1.3,
\]
in which case they are excluded from the GMM calibration sample. This calibration flag is deliberately more inclusive than the three-class BPT scheme of Sect.~\ref{sec:other_properties}, in which composite galaxies lie between the \citet{Kauffmann+03a} and \citet{Kewley+01} demarcations and only galaxies above the \citet{Kewley+01} line are labelled AGN-like, because its sole purpose is to keep possible AGN contamination out of the sSFR calibration. Galaxies with missing diagnostic line ratios are BPT-unclassified, not proven non-AGN; they are retained for the sSFR status assignment when valid SDSS stellar mass and sSFR estimates are available, while the AGN-like analysis treats them separately.
Reference-sample rows with unavailable \texttt{specsfr\_tot\_p50}, and group-sample rows with unavailable \texttt{sfr\_tot\_p50} or \texttt{lgm\_tot\_p50}, including the \(-9999\) sentinel, have no valid sSFR estimate; they are treated as missing data throughout: excluded from the GMM calibration where applicable, never assigned an sSFR class, omitted from every sSFR figure, and reported as counts only (Sect.~\ref{sec:ssfr_classification}).
Galaxies with a measured sSFR are divided into quenched and star-forming classes with a two-component Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) in the \(\log M_\star\)-\(\log\mathrm{sSFR}\) plane \citep{McLachlanPeel2000}; the boundary is the locus where the two component posterior probabilities are equal, and the resulting classification for the SDSS non-AGN reference is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:sSFR_classification}. A fixed-threshold check confirms that the qualitative hierarchy is not tied to the exact GMM split (Sect.~\ref{sec:star_formation}).

\section{Statistical conventions}\label{app:stat_conventions}

\paragraph{Independence unit.} The unit for every inferential contrast among the group catalogues is the \emph{physical} Lim group: standard errors are clustered by Lim group (unifying the overlapping control definitions, with \CG{} systems assigned to their host Lim group, or to their own identifier when they have no Lim counterpart). The decomposition resamples physical hosts jointly across comparison sides, and matched-quartet inference resamples connected components of the bipartite graph linking matched \CG{} quartets to their control hosts. Pooled models across the three controls are secondary summaries computed after deduplicating the overlapping control samples to one row per physical galaxy, with the kept label following the priority \RG{} \(>\) \CB{} \(>\) \CC{} (Appendix~\ref{app:pooled_models}). Purely descriptive reference-sample diagnostics report their own unit where they appear.

\paragraph{Tests and p-values.} Descriptive comparisons use two-sided Fisher exact tests for \(2\times2\) tables and rank-sum or bootstrap comparisons for distributions and median differences; Spearman and Pearson coefficients are diagnostics of monotonic or linear trends only. Paired sign-flip permutation p-values use add-one tail counts, \(p = (k+1)/(B+1)\) with \(k\) the number of permutations at least as extreme as the observation; bootstrap sign-crossing p-values are \(2\min[(k_{\le 0}+1)/(B+1),(k_{\ge 0}+1)/(B+1)]\), capped at unity, where \(k_{\le 0}\) and \(k_{\ge 0}\) count resampled differences on either side of zero; displayed p-values are floored at \(10^{-4}\). Adjusted odds ratios come from binomial generalised linear models with cluster-robust covariance; continuous covariates are standardised, so coefficients of continuous terms are per standard deviation of the fitted frame. Multiple-testing correction is reserved for exploratory tests. We apply Holm adjustment \citep{Holm1979} to the colour, measurement-availability, and magnitude-gap tests and Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate control \citep{BenjaminiHochberg1995} to the dominance, morphology--environment, and other group-scale tests. Paired comparisons between model designs (Sect.~\ref{sec:morphology}) refit every model on the same resampled physical hosts and evaluate it on the same \CG{} reference satellites; their intervals and sign-crossing p-values come from the resampled differences.

With four tracers the estimator is intrinsically noisy: in a Gaussian Monte Carlo of \(10^5\) quartets, \(\sigma_{\rm gapper}/\sigma_{\rm true}\) has median << t3['mc_gapper']['median_ratio']|fmt(2) >> with a 16--84\% range of << t3['mc_gapper']['p16_ratio']|fmt(2) >>--<< t3['mc_gapper']['p84_ratio']|fmt(2) >>. Individual stellar masses can be missing (Fig.~\ref{fig:data_availability}). The << cg4_total_available - matched.get('n_cg4_matched') >> \CG{} galaxies omitted from individual matching lack a stellar mass (Appendix~\ref{app:matching_details}). \section{Main-sequence and quenched-sequence details}\label{app:baseline_details}

We model the star-forming main sequence as a function of stellar mass with polynomials of order 1--4 fitted to the star-forming galaxies of the SDSS non-AGN reference sample; the diagnostic is the residual RMS scatter, not a formal reduced \(\chi^2\), because no per-galaxy uncertainty model is used.
