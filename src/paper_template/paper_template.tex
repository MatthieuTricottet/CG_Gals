\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Given their high apparent densities and low velocity dispersions \citep{Hickson82,Hickson+92}, compact groups appear to be ideal sites for the frequent tidal encounters and mergers that alter galaxy masses, luminosities, sizes, morphologies, and star-formation rates (e.g., \citealt{Mamon92}). \citet{MendesdeOliveira&Hickson94} provided the classic report of an excess of early-type galaxies in compact groups relative to the field (building on the photometric morphologies of \citealt{HKA89} and the merger-remnant expectations of \citealt{ZepfWhitmore93}), in line with the broader morphology--density relation \citep{Dressler80}; multi-wavelength studies of compact-group galaxies likewise report altered infrared, UV, star-formation, and stellar-population properties relative to looser environments \citep{Johnson+07,Tzanavaris+10,Bitsakis+11,Coenda+12}, and their H\,{\sc i} deficiency has been organised into an evolutionary sequence \citep{VerdesMontenegro+01}.

The interpretation of such contrasts is nevertheless ambiguous. Projected selection can include chance alignments, and many Hickson-like systems need not be isolated bound entities: apparent compact groups may be transient alignments, dense substructures, or cores embedded in looser groups and richer haloes \citep{Mamon86,DiazGimenez&Mamon10,DiazGimenez+20,Zheng&Shen21,Taverna+23}. The relevant observational question is therefore not only whether compact-group galaxies differ from field galaxies, but whether they remain different from galaxies occupying regular-group analogues selected with comparable luminosity, redshift, and membership constraints. Recent structural studies point to transformation in progress in compact groups, with stronger signatures in non-isolated systems and host structures \citep{Montaguth2023,Montaguth2025,Montaguth2026MassSize,Montaguth2026NonIsolated}, but they do not by themselves answer this control question.

In our previous work \citep{Tricottet+25}, we found that, once the direct consequences of the compact-group selection are accounted for, compact groups of four galaxies have group-scale properties broadly similar to those of regular groups of four galaxies and to the eligible cores of regular groups. Here we ask the complementary question: even if compact groups are not strongly distinct as systems, are their member galaxies individually different? We compare \CG{} member galaxies with three regular-group analogues -- \RG{}, \CB{}, and \CC{} (Sect.~\ref{sec:samples}) -- controlling for stellar mass, redshift, galaxy rank, and available group-scale properties, and use complementary individual-galaxy, group-composition, and shared-host contrasts to identify which observable, if any, carries a residual compact-group signal.

Section~\ref{sec:data} describes the samples, the classifications, and the statistical design. Section~\ref{sec:results} presents the population contrasts, the matched and within-host comparisons, and the robustness and secondary diagnostics. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses the physical interpretation, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

Morphologies are drawn from Galaxy Zoo 1 \citep{Lintott+08,GalaxyZoo1}, a single-question classification in which volunteers assigned each galaxy image to one of several morphological categories. The resulting vote fractions, debiased for the redshift-dependent classification bias following \citet{Bamford+09}, are provided in the \texttt{zooSpec} table through the columns \texttt{p\_el\_debiased} (fraction voting ``elliptical'') and \texttt{p\_cs\_debiased} (fraction voting combined-spiral; Lintott et al.\ 2011). If either vote fraction is missing after merging Galaxy Zoo onto the compact-group and control catalogues, the row is treated as missing Galaxy Zoo data rather than uncertain. These are vote-fraction classes, not a full structural decomposition. In particular, the catalogue does not provide a clean E/S0 separation, so references below to elliptical or early-type galaxies should be read as Galaxy Zoo 1 elliptical-vote proxies for early-type morphology rather than as a bulge-disc structural classification.

\subsection{Statistical design}\label{sec:stats}

Descriptive baseline comparisons use two-sided Fisher exact tests for ordinary $2\times2$ categorical tables, and rank-sum or bootstrap comparisons for distributions and median differences. Spearman and Pearson coefficients are used only as diagnostics of monotonic or linear trends; small coefficients are interpreted as weak even when precisely estimated.

We organize the inference around a single astrophysical question: after accounting for stellar mass, redshift, galaxy rank, and group-scale differences, do compact groups retain a distinct galaxy population? Separate \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{} comparisons reveal how the answer changes from luminous ordinary-group members to projected cores and true four-member groups. Galaxy-level models establish the adjusted population contrast; group-level matching tests whether whole quartets contain a different satellite mix; and within-host models ask whether compact-subconfiguration members differ from their non-compact co-members. Binary complements are represented once: elliptical versus spiral among usable Galaxy Zoo classifications, and quenched versus star-forming among galaxies with valid sSFR.

Throughout, the independence unit for inferential contrasts among grouped catalogues is the \emph{physical} group: standard errors are clustered by Lim group (unifying the overlapping control definitions, with \CG{} systems assigned to their host Lim group), and resampling of grouped contrasts is blocked by physical group or matched group/pair block, since the four galaxies of one group are not independent observations. Pooled models across the three controls are reported only as secondary summaries, after deduplicating the overlapping control samples to one row per physical galaxy (Appendix~\ref{app:pooled_models}). Paired sign-flip permutation p-values use add-one tail counts, and bootstrap sign-crossing p-values use \(2\min[(k_{\le 0}+1)/(B+1),(k_{\ge 0}+1)/(B+1)]\), capped at unity; displayed p-values are floored at \(10^{-4}\). Purely descriptive reference-sample diagnostics report their own unit in the relevant appendix.

Throughout, the velocity dispersion \(\sigma_v\) that enters models and matching is the quartet-level gapper estimate \citep{Wainer&Thissen76} on the four selected members' redshifts, converted as \(c\,\sigma_z/(1+\bar z)\) --- for \emph{all} samples, including \CB{} and \CC{} quartets embedded in richer hosts, whose \(\sigma_v\) therefore describes the selected quartet rather than the host halo (this is how the spurious Lim 3688 quartets of Sect.~\ref{sec:samples} reach \({\simeq}2000\kms\)). With four tracers this estimator is intrinsically noisy: in a Gaussian Monte Carlo of \(10^5\) quartets, \(\sigma_{\rm gapper}/\sigma_{\rm true}\) has median << t3['mc_gapper']['median_ratio']|fmt(2) >> with a 16--84\% range of << t3['mc_gapper']['p16_ratio']|fmt(2) >>--<< t3['mc_gapper']['p84_ratio']|fmt(2) >>. Candidate covariates enter an adjusted model when non-missing for at least 65\% of that model's frame (matching variables require 70\%); in practice \(\sigma_v\) is complete for every quartet and always enters, the group-luminosity, stellar-mass, redshift, and rank covariates are likewise complete, and no harmonised group-mass covariate is available (Appendix~\ref{app:selection}). Because \(\sigma_v\) is noisy, all analyses that use it are repeated without it; the scientific conclusions are unchanged (Sects.~\ref{sec:primary_contrasts}, \ref{sec:matched}).
<% endif %>

Each control sample is analysed as a separate physical comparison, while elliptical/spiral and quenched/star-forming are each represented by one binary outcome. We report ordinary two-sided p-values for the planned morphology, star-formation, matching, host, and size analyses, together with effect sizes and confidence intervals. Multiplicity control is reserved for broad exploratory batteries: Holm adjustment for the colour and measurement-availability screens and for the magnitude-gap batteries, and Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate control for the larger dominance and morphology--environment screens. The paired sign-flip permutation p-value is used for group-level matches; bootstrap intervals quantify effect-size uncertainty. Colours, AGN-like fractions, \(\mathrm{H}\alpha\) equivalent widths, magnitude gaps, crossing-time trends, projected phase space, and the pairwise tidal index remain secondary diagnostics.

\subsection{Pairwise tidal index}\label{sec:tidal_index}

The pairwise analysis reconstructs projected separations and velocity differences for << tidal.get('n_galaxies_with_pairs') >> catalogue rows and refits adjusted models with and without the pairwise tidal index \(T_i = \sum_j M_{\star,j}/R_{ij}^3\) (in \(\msun\,\mathrm{kpc}^{-3}\)). The sum runs over each entry's \emph{selected quartet co-members only} --- not the full Lim host membership, a restriction shown to be innocuous in Appendix~\ref{app:selection} --- with no velocity window; \(R_{ij}\) converts the great-circle separation with the angular-diameter distance at the quartet's median redshift (proper kpc); co-members with unmeasured stellar mass contribute zero, making \(T_i\) a lower bound in those groups, and quartets with any member lacking coordinates or redshift are dropped whole. These exploratory models adjust for stellar mass and satellite/BGG status only, pool the three control catalogues \emph{without} objid deduplication (unlike the pooled models of Appendix~\ref{app:pooled_models}), and cluster standard errors by physical group. The two model variants estimate \emph{different quantities}. The model without the index estimates the \emph{total} association between compact-group membership and the outcome at fixed stellar mass and satellite status; the model with the index estimates the association \emph{at fixed projected local density}. Because the index is built from \(R^{-3}\)-weighted projected separations, it is mathematically entangled with the compact-group selection itself (compact groups are selected to have small projected separations), so conditioning on it partially conditions on the exposure. A reduction of the \CG{} coefficient when the index is added is thus \emph{conditional attenuation}, not mediation: it does not show that local density accounts for the morphology contrast, and it cannot, because the causal ordering of selection, density, and morphology is not identified by projected snapshots. The baseline models in this subsection are refit on the pairwise-analysis complete-case sample, so their coefficients need not be numerically identical to the corresponding coefficients in Sect.~\ref{sec:primary_contrasts} or Appendix~\ref{app:pooled_models}; because \(R_{ij}\) is projected, the term is a local projected-density and immediate-neighbour proxy rather than a direct tidal-history measurement.

\subsection{An early-type-like satellite excess linked to dense group cores}\label{sec:morph_local_density}

The clearest residual difference between \CG{} and regular-group controls is morphological: a higher Galaxy~Zoo~1 elliptical-vote fraction, carried primarily by satellites. The adjusted excess is largest against the luminosity-selected \CB{} population and true four-member \RG{} groups, and smallest against the projected cores \CC{}. Matched quartet composition follows the same ordering. The galaxy-level matched estimate points in the same direction, especially for satellite pairs, whereas the preferred within-host model shows no clear additional excess after accounting for host-centric radius. The convergent pattern is that \CG{} identifies an early-type-rich dense-core population, not that every galaxy property changes discontinuously at the compact-group boundary.

The scale of the morphology difference depends on what is held fixed. <% set d_satdec = (discussion_es.get('matched_controls', {}) or {}).get('satellite_decomposition', {}) %><% if d_satdec.get('status') == 'ok' %>For example, restricting the individual-galaxy match to satellites increases the elliptical-fraction difference to \(<< d_satdec.get('delta_cg4_minus_control')|fmt(3) >>\), showing the dilution produced by BGGs. <% endif %>Likewise, conditioning on host-centric radius removes the location contrast by construction. Present-day stellar mass may also encode part of a galaxy's environmental history, so fixed-mass estimates describe the remaining association rather than the total accumulated transformation. These qualifications narrow the physical claim without erasing it: the result is a satellite population shift associated with the dense configurations selected as compact groups.

The pairwise tidal-index experiment strengthens this interpretation while also narrowing it. <% if discussion_ell_base.get('status') == 'ok' and discussion_ell_tidal.get('status') == 'ok' -%>For elliptical morphology, the pairwise complete-case baseline \CG{} odds ratio is \(<< discussion_ell_base.get('cg4_odds_ratio')|fmt(2) >>\) (<< pval_text(discussion_ell_base.get('cg4_p')) >>). This proxy uses projected separations, so it may be inflated by line-of-sight alignments and is entangled with compact-group selection itself. We therefore read the tidal-index result as a local projected-density or immediate-neighbour proxy, not as a time-dependent tidal-field measurement. The \CC{} comparison is stringent because \CC{} is also selected as a projected compact core around a BGG. The persistence of a \CG{}--\CC{} morphology contrast after closest-neighbour exclusion, together with attenuation in the tidal-index model, is consistent with \CG{} selecting a more extreme part of the local-density distribution rather than establishing a separate causal channel.

This reading is consistent with compact groups tracing the high-density, high-interaction tail of group environments -- some genuinely compact, others dense cores or substructures inside larger haloes -- and with stronger structural signatures reported for non-isolated compact groups in recent S-PLUS work. The \nGrIso{} isolated \CG{}s are compatible with loose \RG{} groups in satellite early-type fraction (group-level permutation \(p = \pIsoRG\)) and have a lower satellite early-type fraction than the rest of \CG{} (\(p = \pIsoRest\)); this pattern is consistent with a larger-scale host contribution, but the very small isolated sample prevents a firm separation of explanations (Appendix~\ref{app:env_diagnostics}). The available \(M_{200c}\) is a host-halo abundance-matching mass rather than a harmonised quartet property; adding it to the adjusted models mildly attenuates the elliptical odds ratios and changes no conclusion.

\subsection{Elliptical-vote morphologies, S0 degeneracy, and transition galaxies}

The Galaxy Zoo variables identify high elliptical and combined-spiral vote fractions; they do not cleanly distinguish classical ellipticals from S0 galaxies, faded disks, or other bulge-dominated systems. The enhanced \CG{} elliptical fraction should therefore be read as an early-type-like appearance shift, not proof that compact groups specifically build classical ellipticals.

This caveat matters when comparing with recent S-PLUS studies of compact-group transition galaxies and \(R_{\rm e}\)--S\'ersic-index trends \citep{Montaguth2023,Montaguth2025,Montaguth2026MassSize,Montaguth2026NonIsolated}. Their transition population can plausibly overlap with our elliptical-vote class, so the approaches are complementary: their structural measurements describe likely transformation channels, while our regular-group controls identify the residual elliptical/spiral vote-fraction balance.<% if sz.get('status') == 'ok' %> The attached Simard \(R_{\rm e}\)--S\'ersic-\(n\) plane is therefore shown as context, not as a primary test (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:size_re_n_plane}).<% endif %>

Two guardrails apply: a negative \(\Delta\) at fixed mass can reflect tidal truncation or stripping, but also higher bulge dominance or residual blending in crowded fields. The morphology-stratified fits address the former, and the Petrosian cross-check, measure-difference diagnostic, and crowding exclusions bound the latter (Appendix~\ref{app:size_details}); future claims of compact-group size evolution should be read through these blocks together, not through a single pooled coefficient.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
<% set c_pc4c = ((es.get('matched_controls', {}) or {}).get('group_level_per_control', {}) or {}).get('Control4C', {}) %>

We have tested whether galaxies in \CG{} compact groups remain special after removing split systems, comparing them with \RG, \CB, and \CC{} through the individual-galaxy, group-composition, and shared-host contrasts defined in Sect.~\ref{sec:stats}. The inference applies to this luminous, volume-limited, exactly four-member non-split HMCG sample rather than to all compact groups.

The main result is an early-type-like satellite excess. \CG{} galaxies have a higher Galaxy~Zoo~1 elliptical-vote fraction than all three regular-group comparisons after accounting for observed galaxy and group properties. The excess is strongest against \CB{} and \RG{} and weakest against the projected cores \CC{}; matched quartet composition and satellite-mass-balanced variants show the same hierarchy. BGGs do not show a corresponding adjusted difference, and the preferred within-host analysis shows no clear excess beyond the central locations occupied by \CG{} members. Vote-threshold, continuous-vote, S\'ersic-index, and close-pair checks support the morphology result. A projected tidal-density proxy attenuates the coefficient, consistent with local density contributing to the pattern, although the projected data do not identify mediation or a unique causal mechanism.

The star-formation result is weaker and consistent with being largely associated with the differing vote-based morphology mix, with no additional conditional offset detected at fixed vote-based morphology. \CG{} satellites have a lower raw star-forming fraction, especially relative to \RG, but adjusted evidence is control-dependent, matched contrasts provide no evidence for a quenched-fraction difference, and star-forming \CG{} galaxies show no robust main-sequence offset.

At fixed stellar mass, redshift, and group-scale covariates, the seeing-corrected satellite size estimate is \(<< conclusion_adj_sat.get('cg4_delta_dex')|fmt(3) >>\) dex and its confidence interval includes zero; the matched Simard difference is \(<< conclusion_m_simard.get('mean_delta')|fmt(3) >>\) dex. The small Petrosian-only matched offset is not reproduced by the preferred size measure or across controls, so we do not claim a compact-group size effect.

The remaining secondary diagnostics do not identify a simple alternative driver: colours are control-sensitive and based on a biased matched subset, AGN-like fractions and control-specific magnitude gaps do not provide comparably robust parallel offsets, the \(\mathrm{H}\alpha\) diagnostic lacks age-sensitive spectral indices, and projected phase-space trends do not localize the contrast uniquely to the innermost satellites.

We therefore interpret \CG{} systems as hosting a population shifted towards early-type-like morphologies, consistent with accumulated structural transformation in dense group cores and/or preprocessing. This does not establish a compact-group-specific quenching mechanism at the present epoch, and the dynamically isolated subset is too small to isolate a distinct mode. Explicit E--S0--spiral and transition-galaxy classifications, bulge--disc structure, homogeneous halo masses, and age-sensitive stellar-population diagnostics will be needed to determine whether the satellite excess reflects disk fading, tidal heating, or merger-driven transformation.

\section{sSFR classification details}\label{app:ssfr_method}

We identify BPT-classifiable galaxies by requiring measured values of both emission-line ratios
\[
\log_{10}\!\bigl([\mathrm{N\,II}]\lambda6584/\mathrm{H}\alpha\bigr)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\log_{10}\!\bigl([\mathrm{O\,III}]\lambda5007/\mathrm{H}\beta\bigr).
\]
Among these galaxies, we flag AGN-like systems if they satisfy either
\[
\log_{10}\frac{[\mathrm{N\,II}]}{\mathrm{H}\alpha} > 0,
\]
or if they lie above the empirical demarcation of \citet{Kauffmann+03a}:
\[
\log_{10}\frac{[\mathrm{O\,III}]}{\mathrm{H}\beta}
> \frac{0.61}{\log_{10}([\mathrm{N\,II}]/\mathrm{H}\alpha)-0.05} \;+\; 1.3,
\]
in which case they are excluded from the GMM calibration sample. This calibration flag is deliberately more inclusive than the three-class BPT scheme of Sect.~\ref{sec:agn_env}, in which composite galaxies lie between the \citet{Kauffmann+03a} and \citet{Kewley+01} demarcations and only galaxies above the \citet{Kewley+01} line are labelled AGN-like, because its sole purpose is to keep possible AGN contamination out of the sSFR calibration. Galaxies with missing diagnostic line ratios are BPT-unclassified, not proven non-AGN. They are retained for the broad sSFR status assignment when valid SDSS stellar mass and sSFR estimates are available, while the emission-line/AGN-like analysis treats BPT-unclassified objects separately.
Galaxies with \texttt{specsfr\_tot\_p50} equal to \(-9999\) in the SDSS carry the MPA-JHU missing-value sentinel and have no valid sSFR estimate;
they are treated as missing data throughout: excluded from the GMM calibration, never assigned an sSFR class,
omitted from every sSFR figure, and reported as counts only (Sect.~\ref{sec:ssfr_classification}).
Galaxies with a measured sSFR are divided into quenched and star-forming classes with a two-component Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) in the \(\log M_\star\)-\(\log\mathrm{sSFR}\) plane \citep{McLachlanPeel2000}. The calibration excludes galaxies flagged as AGN-like where the required BPT line ratios are available, while BPT-unclassified galaxies with valid SDSS stellar mass and sSFR estimates can still receive a broad sSFR class. The quenched/star-forming boundary is the locus where the two component posterior probabilities are equal; the resulting classification for the SDSS non-AGN reference is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:sSFR_classification}. A fixed-threshold robustness check confirms that the qualitative hierarchy is not tied to the exact GMM split (Sect.~\ref{sec:secondary}).

The crowding checks mirror Sect.~\ref{sec:morph_crowding}: excluding galaxies with a projected neighbour within << size_crowd.get('threshold_arcsec')|fmt(0) >> arcsec leaves a satellite coefficient of \(<< size_crowd_excl.get('cg4_delta_dex')|fmt(3) >>\) dex (<< pval_text(size_crowd_excl.get('p')) >>), and adding the close-neighbour flag as a covariate gives \(<< size_crowd_flag.get('cg4_delta_dex')|fmt(3) >>\) dex (<< pval_text(size_crowd_flag.get('p')) >>).
<% endif %>

<% if size_md.get('status') == 'ok' %>
The per-galaxy difference between the two measures, \(d = \log R_{\rm chl,r} - \log R_{50,r}\), averages \(<< size_md.get('mean_delta_dex')|fmt(3) >>\) dex<% if size_md.get('mean_by_morphology', {}).get('elliptical') and size_md.get('mean_by_morphology', {}).get('spiral') %>, and is larger for elliptical galaxies (\(<< size_md.get('mean_by_morphology', {}).get('elliptical', {}).get('mean')|fmt(3) >>\) dex) than for spiral galaxies (\(<< size_md.get('mean_by_morphology', {}).get('spiral', {}).get('mean')|fmt(3) >>\) dex), as expected if the Petrosian aperture preferentially truncates concentrated profiles<% endif %>. <% if size_md.get('spearman_vs_neighbour_separation') %>Its rank correlation with the nearest-neighbour separation is \(\rho_\mathrm{S} = << (size_md.get('spearman_vs_neighbour_separation', {}).get('rho', 0) + 0)|fmt(3) >>\) (<< pval_text(size_md.get('spearman_vs_neighbour_separation', {}).get('p')) >>), so residual blending does not drive a strong measure-dependent size systematic.<% endif %>
<% endif %>

<% if size_tidal.get('status') == 'ok' and size_tidal.get('baseline', {}).get('status') == 'ok' and size_tidal.get('with_tidal_index', {}).get('status') == 'ok' %>
Adding the pairwise projected tidal index of Sect.~\ref{sec:tidal_index} to the all-galaxy size model changes the \CG{} coefficient from \(<< size_tidal.get('baseline', {}).get('cg4_delta_dex')|fmt(3) >>\) dex to \(<< size_tidal.get('with_tidal_index', {}).get('cg4_delta_dex')|fmt(3) >>\) dex on the pairwise complete-case sample<% if szv.get('absorbed_by_tidal_index') %>; the local projected-density proxy attenuates the size coefficient in the same way it attenuates the morphology coefficient<% else %>; with a near-null baseline coefficient, the attenuation diagnostic used for morphology is not informative here<% endif %>.
<% endif %>

<% if size_radial_cg.get('status') == 'ok' and size_radial_ctrl.get('status') == 'ok' %>
The radial diagnostic is exploratory: mass- and redshift-adjusted satellite size residuals correlate with projected BGG-centric distance with \(\rho_\mathrm{S} = << size_radial_cg.get('spearman_rho')|fmt(2) >>\) (<< pval_text(size_radial_cg.get('spearman_p')) >>) in \CG{} and \(\rho_\mathrm{S} = << size_radial_ctrl.get('spearman_rho')|fmt(2) >>\) (<< pval_text(size_radial_ctrl.get('spearman_p')) >>) in the pooled controls. A more negative inner-region residual would be the tidal-truncation expectation; the observed trends are similar in \CG{} and the controls, so the data do not isolate a \CG{}-specific inner-region size suppression.
<% endif %>
