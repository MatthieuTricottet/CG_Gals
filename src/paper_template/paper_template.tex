\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Given their high apparent densities and low velocity dispersions \citep{Hickson82,Hickson+92}, compact groups appear to be ideal sites for the frequent tidal encounters and mergers that alter galaxy masses, luminosities, sizes, morphologies, and star-formation rates (e.g., \citealt{Mamon92}). \citet{MendesdeOliveira&Hickson94} provided the classic report of an excess of early-type galaxies in compact groups relative to the field (building on the photometric morphologies of \citealt{HKA89} and the merger-remnant expectations of \citealt{ZepfWhitmore93}), in line with the broader morphology--density relation \citep{Dressler80}; multi-wavelength studies of compact-group galaxies likewise report altered infrared, UV, star-formation, and stellar-population properties relative to looser environments \citep{Johnson+07,Tzanavaris+10,Bitsakis+11,Coenda+12}, and their H\,{\sc i} deficiency has been organised into an evolutionary sequence \citep{VerdesMontenegro+01}.

The interpretation of such contrasts is nevertheless ambiguous. Projected selection can include chance alignments, and many Hickson-like systems need not be isolated bound entities: apparent compact groups may be transient alignments, dense substructures, or cores embedded in looser groups and richer haloes \citep{Mamon86,DiazGimenez&Mamon10,DiazGimenez+20,Zheng&Shen21,Taverna+23}. The relevant observational question is therefore not only whether compact-group galaxies differ from field galaxies, but whether they remain different from galaxies occupying regular-group analogues selected with comparable luminosity, redshift, and membership constraints. Recent structural studies find evidence for transformation in compact groups, especially non-isolated systems \citep{Montaguth2023,Montaguth2025,Montaguth2026MassSize,Montaguth2026NonIsolated}; \citet{Montaguth2026NonIsolated} compared non-isolated compact-group galaxies with surrounding host populations, but not with ordinary-group quartets reproducing compact-group membership, luminosity-ranking, or projected-core selection.

In our previous work \citep{Tricottet+25}, we found that, once the direct consequences of the compact-group selection are accounted for, compact groups of four galaxies have group-scale properties broadly similar to those of regular groups of four galaxies and to the eligible cores of regular groups. Here we ask how the galaxy-population contrast changes across three ordinary-group comparison samples: genuine four-member groups (\RG{}), the four brightest eligible members of richer groups (\CB{}), and BGG-centred projected cores (\CC{}), the closest analogue of a compact configuration. We adjust for stellar mass, redshift, galaxy rank, and group-scale properties, and combine individual-galaxy, group-composition, and shared-host contrasts. Section~\ref{sec:data} describes the samples, the classifications, and the statistical approach; Sect.~\ref{sec:results} presents the results; Sect.~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses their physical interpretation; and Sect.~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Data and classifications}\label{sec:data}
\subsection{Samples}\label{sec:samples}
We use the compact-group and control samples constructed in our previous study \citep{Tricottet+25}. Galaxy properties are taken from the SDSS DR12 catalogue of \citet{Tempel+17}, while regular groups are drawn from the SDSS DR7 group catalogue of \citet{Lim+17}; the two catalogues were matched through SDSS object identifiers to define the Lim--Tempel working catalogue. Compact groups are selected from the modified Hickson compact-group catalogue of \citet{DiazGimenez+18}: reliable HMCGs with exactly four members, all present in the Lim--Tempel catalogue, satisfying the volume- and luminosity-complete limits $0.005 \le z_{\rm BGG} \le 0.0452$ and $M_{r,\rm BGG}\leq -21.81$, with all four galaxies lying within 3 magnitudes of the brightest group galaxy (BGG). In contrast to our previous study, we remove CGs classified as \emph{split} following \citet{Zheng&Shen21}, which reduces the compact-group sample from <<CG4_Groups_withsplit_N>> to <<CG4_Groups_nonsplit_N>> groups. Following \citet{Zheng&Shen21}, the remaining systems are \emph{isolated} when the four members constitute their whole Lim--Tempel group, \emph{predominant} when they contribute at least half of the host-group $r$-band luminosity, and \emph{embedded} otherwise (\nGrIso, \nGrPre, and \nGrEmb{} systems).

\subsection{Classifications}\label{sec:ssfr_classification}
Star-formation classes are based on SDSS total specific star-formation rates. The inherited DR12 group catalogues store \(\log\mathrm{sSFR}=\texttt{sfr\_tot\_p50}-\texttt{lgm\_tot\_p50}\). In the MPA-JHU catalogues, \(-9999\) is the missing-value sentinel of the \texttt{galSpecExtra} table \citep{Stoughton02,Brinchmann+04,Kauffmann+03b}, so rows with unavailable components are treated as \emph{missing data}: they are excluded from the classification and from every sSFR-based figure and fraction, and are reported as counts only (Table~\ref{tab:sSFR_status}). All quenched and star-forming fractions are computed among classified galaxies. The missing-sSFR incidence is higher in \CG{} (<< CG4_Gals_NNosSFR_pc|fmt(0) >>\% of members, << CG4_BGG_NNosSFR_pc|fmt(0) >>\% of BGGs) than in the reference sample (<< SDSS_NNosSFR_pc|fmt(0) >>\%)<% if d1.get('n_missing') %>. Tracing the stored spectrum identifiers shows why: << d1.get('by_type', {}).get('no-galSpecExtra') >> of the << d1.get('n_missing') >> missing rows are BOSS-instrument spectra, which the MPA-JHU tables (legacy reductions only) never covered, << d1.get('by_type', {}).get('sentinel') >> are legacy spectra whose SFR fit returned the sentinel, and << d1.get('by_type', {}).get('no-specObjID', 0) >> have no spectrum identifier; the missing rows have higher, not lower, median signal-to-noise than the classified ones (Appendix~\ref{app:sdss_query})<% endif %>. Galaxies with a measured sSFR are split into quenched and star-forming classes with a two-component Gaussian mixture model (GMM) in the \(\log M_\star\)--\(\log\mathrm{sSFR}\) plane; the boundary and the AGN handling are given in Appendix~\ref{app:ssfr_method} (Fig.~\ref{fig:sSFR_classification}).

\subsection{Galaxy sizes}\label{sec:size_data}

Our primary size measure is the circular effective (half-light) radius of the pure S\'ersic fit in the $r$ band, \(R_{e,r}\), from the structural catalogue of \citet{Simard2011}: PSF-convolved GIM2D fits with re-derived sky levels and deblending designed for crowded fields, so the sizes are corrected for seeing. The catalogue is keyed to SDSS DR7 identifiers; we bridge our DR<<DATA_RELEASE>> objects to their DR7 counterparts through the SkyServer \texttt{<< r.get('size_dr7_bridge', 'SpecDR7') >>} cross-match table. Rows whose Simard redshift differs from the catalogue redshift by more than << size_cuts.get('z_match_tolerance')|fmt(3) >> are treated as wrong matches and dropped (<< size_totals.get('z_mismatch') >> rows)<% if size_totals.get('shred_merge', 0) %>, as are rows in which two catalogue galaxies of the same group resolve to a single DR7 identifier (<< size_totals.get('shred_merge') >> rows)<% endif %>. The Simard sizes, tabulated in kpc under the catalogue's own cosmology, are converted back to angular sizes with the catalogue plate scale and then to kpc with the same Planck 2015 conversion as every other projected quantity.

As a secondary, model-independent measure we use the SDSS Petrosian radii \texttt{petroR50\_r} and \texttt{petroR90\_r}, which also define the concentration index \(C = R_{90,r}/R_{50,r}\) and provide the like-for-like bridge to \citet{Coenda+12}. Petrosian radii are not corrected for seeing, and the Petrosian aperture misses roughly 20\% of the flux of an \(n=4\) profile, which biases \(R_{50,r}\) low for concentrated galaxies \citep{Blanton2001,Graham2005}. \subsection{Statistical approach}\label{sec:stats}

Our main question is whether compact-group galaxies retain distinct properties once differences in stellar mass, redshift, galaxy rank, and group properties are taken into account. The three controls address physically different comparisons, so we compare \CG{} with each one separately. Galaxy-level logistic models quantify the adjusted E-versus-S and quenched-versus-star-forming contrasts; group-level matching compares the satellite mix of whole quartets; and within-host models compare compact-group members with their non-compact co-members. The covariates are stellar mass, redshift, rank, group luminosity, and the quartet velocity dispersion \(\sigma_v\); because \(\sigma_v\) from four tracers is noisy, every analysis that uses it is repeated without it (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness_details}).

The independence unit is the \emph{physical} Lim group: standard errors are clustered by physical group and resampling is blocked by physical group or matched pair, because the four galaxies of one group are not independent. We report two-sided p-values with effect sizes and confidence intervals for the planned morphology, star-formation, matching, host, and size analyses, and reserve multiplicity control for the exploratory batteries. The p-value conventions, the covariate-completeness rule, the overlap coefficient, and the treatment of the overlapping control samples in pooled summaries are collected in Appendix~\ref{app:stat_conventions}.

\subsection{An early-type-like satellite excess linked to dense group cores}\label{sec:morph_local_density}

Compact groups of four galaxies host more early-type-like satellites than any of the three ordinary-group comparison samples, and the excess follows the compactness of the comparison: it is largest against the \({\sim}\SepLoose\)~kpc \CB{} and \RG{} quartets and smallest against the \({\sim}\SepCC\)~kpc projected cores \CC{} (Sect.~\ref{sec:morphology}). Within their own hosts, compact-group members show the excess only when their central location is not held fixed, as expected if they occupy the early-type-rich centres of their groups. The within-host comparison extends the surrounding-host analysis of \citet{Montaguth2026NonIsolated} by asking whether morphology differs beyond shared host and central location; samples, morphology definitions, and structural measurements differ, so the results are complementary. Restricting the individually matched comparison to satellites raises the E-class fraction difference<% if m_satdec.get('status') == 'ok' %> to \(<< m_satdec.get('delta_cg4_minus_control')|fmt(2) >>\)<% endif %>. Present-day stellar mass may itself encode part of a galaxy's environmental history, so fixed-mass estimates describe the remaining association rather than the total accumulated transformation.

The local projected-density comparison of Sect.~\ref{sec:tidal_index} is consistent with this picture without proving it: the tidal-index term is strongly associated with E-class morphology and attenuates the \CG{} coefficient<% if discussion_ell_base.get('status') == 'ok' and discussion_ell_tidal.get('status') == 'ok' %> (from \(<< discussion_ell_base.get('cg4_odds_ratio')|fmt(2) >>\) to \(<< discussion_ell_tidal.get('cg4_odds_ratio')|fmt(2) >>\))<% endif %>, as expected if compact groups select galaxies in locally dense projected configurations where repeated close companions, rather than the catalogue label, are linked to the early-type-like population. Because the index is entangled with the selection and its residual term is not stable on common support, projected snapshots do not identify the causal ordering of selection, density, and morphology. This reading is consistent with compact groups tracing the high-density, high-interaction tail of group environments---some genuinely compact, others dense cores or substructures inside larger haloes---and with the stronger structural signatures reported for non-isolated compact groups in recent S-PLUS work \citep{Montaguth2023,Montaguth2025,Montaguth2026MassSize,Montaguth2026NonIsolated}. The \nGrIso{} isolated compact groups are compatible with loose \RG{} groups in satellite E-class fraction and lie below the rest of \CG{} (Sect.~\ref{sec:cg_classes}); this suggests a possible larger-scale host contribution, but the isolated sample is too small to distinguish the explanations, and host halo mass adds nothing to the class contrast<% set t7 = referee.get('T7', {}) %><% if t7.get('logM200_sensitivity', {}).get('pooled_elliptical_plus_logM200', {}).get('status') == 'ok' %> or to the adjusted models (Appendix~\ref{app:selection})<% endif %>.

Stellar-population diagnostics could in principle separate an accumulated enrichment history from a shorter star-formation timescale, but we defer a stellar-metallicity analysis for two reasons. No quenching excess is detected at fixed morphology (Sect.~\ref{sec:star_formation}), so a strangulation-type metallicity test of the kind proposed by \citet{Peng+15} would have little leverage here, and the single-fibre SDSS spectra sample only the central \({\sim}2\) kpc of the galaxies at the sample redshifts, where the gradients of both metallicity and age are steepest.

\subsection{GZ1 E-class morphologies and transition galaxies}\label{sec:s0_degeneracy}

Our E class is a smooth early-type-like proxy with a substantial S0 content (Sect.~\ref{sec:ssfr_classification}). Galaxy Zoo~1 cannot determine whether the excess comprises S0s, ellipticals, or both; we reserve that question for a dedicated, pre-registered analysis of the DS18 classes by environment. This caveat matters when comparing with the S-PLUS studies of compact-group transition galaxies and \(R_{\rm e}\)--S\'ersic-index trends \citep{Montaguth2023,Montaguth2025,Montaguth2026MassSize,Montaguth2026NonIsolated}: their structural measurements and transition populations probe pathways, whereas our three control samples test which apparent \CG{} signatures survive across genuine four-member, luminosity-selected, and BGG-centred projected-core quartets.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We have tested whether galaxies in \CG{} compact groups remain special after removing split systems and comparing them with \RG, \CB, and \CC{}. The conclusions apply to this luminous, volume-limited, exactly four-member non-split HMCG sample rather than to all compact groups.

The main result is an early-type-like satellite excess. \CG{} galaxies are more often in the GZ1 E class than all three regular-group comparisons after accounting for galaxy and group properties; the difference is strongest against \CB{} and \RG{} and weakest against the projected cores \CC{}. BGGs show no corresponding difference, and within their hosts \CG{} members show no clear excess once central location is held fixed. A projected tidal-density proxy attenuates the \CG{} coefficient, but its entanglement with compact-group selection and imperfect common support preclude a causal interpretation.

The star-formation result is the morphology mix seen through a different lens: at fixed vote-based morphology the quenched fractions agree, the matched quenched-fraction difference is small, and star-forming \CG{} galaxies show no robust main-sequence offset. <% if sz.get('status') == 'ok' %><% set conclusion_adj_sat = sz.get('adjusted', {}).get('satellites', {}) %>At fixed stellar mass the seeing-corrected satellite size estimate is \(<< conclusion_adj_sat.get('cg4_delta_dex')|fmt(3) >>\) dex with an interval including zero, so we do not claim a compact-group size effect. <% endif %>Colours, nuclear activity, magnitude gaps, \(\mathrm{H}\alpha\) emission, \(D_n4000\), and projected phase-space trends identify no simple alternative driver.

We therefore interpret \CG{} systems as hosting a population shifted towards early-type-like morphologies, consistent with structural transformation accumulated in dense group cores and with preprocessing, and without evidence for a compact-group-specific quenching mechanism at the present epoch. The isolated subset is too small to establish a distinct mode. Explicit E--S0--spiral classifications, bulge--disc structure, and stellar-population diagnostics will be needed to determine whether the satellite excess reflects disc fading, tidal heating, or merger-driven transformation.

\section{sSFR classification details}\label{app:ssfr_method}

We identify BPT-classifiable galaxies by requiring measured values of both emission-line ratios
\[
\log_{10}\!\bigl([\mathrm{N\,II}]\lambda6584/\mathrm{H}\alpha\bigr)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\log_{10}\!\bigl([\mathrm{O\,III}]\lambda5007/\mathrm{H}\beta\bigr).
\]
Among these galaxies, we flag AGN-like systems if they satisfy either
\[
\log_{10}\frac{[\mathrm{N\,II}]}{\mathrm{H}\alpha} > 0,
\]
or if they lie above the empirical demarcation of \citet{Kauffmann+03a}:
\[
\log_{10}\frac{[\mathrm{O\,III}]}{\mathrm{H}\beta}
> \frac{0.61}{\log_{10}([\mathrm{N\,II}]/\mathrm{H}\alpha)-0.05} \;+\; 1.3,
\]
in which case they are excluded from the GMM calibration sample. This calibration flag is deliberately more inclusive than the three-class BPT scheme of Sect.~\ref{sec:other_properties}, in which composite galaxies lie between the \citet{Kauffmann+03a} and \citet{Kewley+01} demarcations and only galaxies above the \citet{Kewley+01} line are labelled AGN-like, because its sole purpose is to keep possible AGN contamination out of the sSFR calibration. Galaxies with missing diagnostic line ratios are BPT-unclassified, not proven non-AGN; they are retained for the sSFR status assignment when valid SDSS stellar mass and sSFR estimates are available, while the AGN-like analysis treats them separately.
Reference-sample rows with unavailable \texttt{specsfr\_tot\_p50}, and group-sample rows with unavailable \texttt{sfr\_tot\_p50} or \texttt{lgm\_tot\_p50}, including the \(-9999\) sentinel, have no valid sSFR estimate; they are treated as missing data throughout: excluded from the GMM calibration where applicable, never assigned an sSFR class, omitted from every sSFR figure, and reported as counts only (Sect.~\ref{sec:ssfr_classification}).
Galaxies with a measured sSFR are divided into quenched and star-forming classes with a two-component Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) in the \(\log M_\star\)-\(\log\mathrm{sSFR}\) plane \citep{McLachlanPeel2000}; the boundary is the locus where the two component posterior probabilities are equal, and the resulting classification for the SDSS non-AGN reference is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:sSFR_classification}. A fixed-threshold check confirms that the qualitative hierarchy is not tied to the exact GMM split (Sect.~\ref{sec:star_formation}).

\section{Statistical conventions}\label{app:stat_conventions}

\paragraph{Independence unit.} The unit for every inferential contrast among the group catalogues is the \emph{physical} Lim group: standard errors are clustered by Lim group (unifying the overlapping control definitions, with \CG{} systems assigned to their host Lim group, or to their own identifier when they have no Lim counterpart), and resampling of grouped contrasts is blocked by physical group or by matched group/pair block. Pooled models across the three controls are secondary summaries computed after deduplicating the overlapping control samples to one row per physical galaxy, with the kept label following the priority \RG{} \(>\) \CB{} \(>\) \CC{} (Appendix~\ref{app:pooled_models}). Purely descriptive reference-sample diagnostics report their own unit where they appear.

\paragraph{Tests and p-values.} Descriptive comparisons use two-sided Fisher exact tests for \(2\times2\) tables and rank-sum or bootstrap comparisons for distributions and median differences; Spearman and Pearson coefficients are diagnostics of monotonic or linear trends only. Paired sign-flip permutation p-values use add-one tail counts, \(p = (k+1)/(B+1)\) with \(k\) the number of permutations at least as extreme as the observation; bootstrap sign-crossing p-values are \(2\min[(k_{\le 0}+1)/(B+1),(k_{\ge 0}+1)/(B+1)]\), capped at unity, where \(k_{\le 0}\) and \(k_{\ge 0}\) count resampled differences on either side of zero; displayed p-values are floored at \(10^{-4}\). Adjusted odds ratios come from binomial generalised linear models with cluster-robust covariance; continuous covariates are standardised, so coefficients of continuous terms are per standard deviation of the fitted frame. Multiplicity control is reserved for exploratory screens: Holm adjustment for the colour, measurement-availability, and magnitude-gap screens, and Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate control for the dominance, morphology--environment, and second-order screens.

\paragraph{Overlap coefficient.} Where two distributions are compared for common support (compactness in Sect.~\ref{sec:samples}, \(\log T_i\) in Sect.~\ref{sec:tidal_index}), the overlap coefficient is the integral of the minimum of the two kernel density estimates, between 0 (disjoint) and 1 (identical).
