  \abstract
  {Compact groups combine the highest projected galaxy densities with low velocity dispersions, yet most of them lie inside larger groups. Whether their galaxies are special therefore depends on how they compare with the members of ordinary groups, not only with the field.}
  {We test whether the galaxies of compact groups of four members (\CG) differ from those of regular groups of four members (\RG), from the four brightest members of regular groups (\CB), and from the projected cores formed by the brightest group galaxy and its three nearest projected companions (\CC).}
  {For 62 non-split \CG{} systems we combine Galaxy Zoo~1 morphologies, stellar masses, specific star-formation rates, emission-line diagnostics, spectral indices, and seeing-corrected half-light radii. Each control is compared separately with covariate-adjusted models, galaxy- and group-level matching, and comparisons within shared host groups.}
  {The clearest difference is morphological. At fixed stellar mass, redshift, rank, and group properties, \CG{} galaxies, and especially their satellites, are more often classified elliptical: the odds ratios are \(2.1\), \(1.7\), and \(2.8\) against \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{}. Matched quartets show the same ordering: the excess of elliptical satellites is significant against \CB{} and \RG{}, and has the same sign but is not significant against \CC{} (\(\Delta = 0.11\), \(p = 0.08\)). Compact-group members occupy the elliptical-rich centres of their host groups, and no additional excess remains at fixed host-centric radius. At fixed morphology the quenched fractions do not differ, and star formation, colour, nuclear activity, magnitude gaps, and sizes show no comparably robust offsets.}
  {Compact groups of four galaxies host a population shifted towards elliptical morphologies, consistent with transformation accumulated in dense group cores and with preprocessing, without a present-day quenching mechanism specific to compact groups.}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Given their high apparent densities and low velocity dispersions \citep{Hickson82,Hickson+92}, compact groups appear to be ideal sites for the frequent tidal encounters and mergers that alter galaxy masses, luminosities, sizes, morphologies, and star-formation rates (e.g., \citealt{Mamon92}). \citet{MendesdeOliveira&Hickson94} provided the classic report of an excess of early-type galaxies in compact groups relative to the field (building on the photometric morphologies of \citealt{HKA89} and the merger-remnant expectations of \citealt{ZepfWhitmore93}), in line with the broader morphology--density relation \citep{Dressler80}; multi-wavelength studies of compact-group galaxies likewise report altered infrared, UV, star-formation, and stellar-population properties relative to looser environments \citep{Johnson+07,Tzanavaris+10,Bitsakis+11,Coenda+12}, and their H\,{\sc i} deficiency has been organised into an evolutionary sequence \citep{VerdesMontenegro+01}.

The interpretation of such contrasts is nevertheless ambiguous. Projected selection can include chance alignments, and many Hickson-like systems need not be isolated bound entities: apparent compact groups may be transient alignments, dense substructures, or cores embedded in looser groups and richer haloes \citep{Mamon86,DiazGimenez&Mamon10,DiazGimenez+20,Zheng&Shen21,Taverna+23}. The relevant observational question is therefore not only whether compact-group galaxies differ from field galaxies, but whether they remain different from galaxies occupying regular-group analogues selected with comparable luminosity, redshift, and membership constraints. Recent structural studies find evidence for transformation in compact groups, especially non-isolated systems \citep{Montaguth2023,Montaguth2025,Montaguth2026MassSize,Montaguth2026NonIsolated}; \citet{Montaguth2026NonIsolated} compared non-isolated compact-group galaxies with surrounding host populations, but not with ordinary-group quartets reproducing compact-group membership, luminosity-ranking, or projected-core selection.

In our previous work \citep{Tricottet+25}, we found that, once the direct consequences of the compact-group selection are accounted for, compact groups of four galaxies have group-scale properties broadly similar to those of regular groups of four galaxies and to the eligible cores of regular groups. Here we ask how the galaxy-population contrast changes across three ordinary-group comparison samples: genuine four-member groups (\RG{}), the four brightest eligible members of richer groups (\CB{}), and BGG-centred projected cores (\CC{}), the closest analogue of a compact configuration. We adjust for stellar mass, redshift, galaxy rank, and group-scale properties, and combine individual-galaxy, group-composition, and shared-host contrasts. Section~\ref{sec:data} describes the samples, the classifications, and the statistical approach; Sect.~\ref{sec:results} presents the results; Sect.~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses their physical interpretation; and Sect.~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Data and classifications}\label{sec:data}
\subsection{Samples}\label{sec:samples}
We use the compact-group and control samples constructed in our previous study \citep{Tricottet+25}. Galaxy properties are taken from the SDSS DR12 catalogue of \citet{Tempel+17}, while regular groups are drawn from the SDSS DR7 group catalogue of \citet{Lim+17}; the two catalogues were matched through SDSS object identifiers to define the Lim--Tempel working catalogue. Compact groups are selected from the modified Hickson compact-group catalogue of \citet{DiazGimenez+18}: reliable HMCGs with exactly four members, all present in the Lim--Tempel catalogue, satisfying the volume- and luminosity-complete limits $0.005 \le z_{\rm BGG} \le 0.0452$ and $M_{r,\rm BGG}\leq -21.81$, with all four galaxies lying within 3 magnitudes of the brightest group galaxy (BGG). In contrast to our previous study, we remove CGs classified as \emph{split} following \citet{Zheng&Shen21}, which reduces the compact-group sample from 78 to 62 groups. Following \citet{Zheng&Shen21}, the remaining systems are \emph{isolated} when the four members constitute their whole Lim--Tempel group, \emph{predominant} when they contribute at least half of the host-group $r$-band luminosity, and \emph{embedded} otherwise (\nGrIso, \nGrPre, and \nGrEmb{} systems).

The three control samples are built from Lim--Tempel groups satisfying the same redshift and BGG-luminosity limits, with at least four members and at least three satellites within 3 magnitudes of the BGG, and serve different purposes (Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic}). \RG{} contains regular groups with exactly four members and is the most direct equal-membership comparison. \CB{} contains the four brightest eligible members of each regular group and tests whether the \CG{} population resembles the luminous core of an ordinary group. \CC{} contains the BGG and its three closest projected companions among members within 3 magnitudes of the BGG, reconstructed here from the eligible parent groups by applying the magnitude criterion before ranking companions by projected distance; it is the most compact-core-like analogue, but not a compactness-matched sample: the \CG{}--\CC{} overlap coefficient in \(-\log R_{\rm pair,med}\) is 0.34, and 18\% of \CG{} groups lie above the \CC{} 95th percentile in compactness. Every control group containing at least one \CG{} galaxy is excluded, so no galaxy appears on both sides of any comparison; Appendix~\ref{app:cg_core_overlap} shows that this exclusion removes the hosts of 51 of the 56 non-isolated compact groups, whose members form the would-be \CC{} cores of those hosts. We also remove the systems associated with Lim group 3688, whose single outlying-redshift member ($z=0.048$, against $0.032$--$0.034$ for the other six) enters both the \CB{} and \CC{} quartets and inflates their velocity dispersions to $\simeq 2000 \kms$. Table~\ref{tab:samples} summarises the final samples; the \CB{} and \CC{} group counts differ because the luminosity-ranked and distance-ranked definitions retain different Lim groups after the compact-group exclusion.

We use SDSS DR16 \citep{Ahumada+20} to retrieve stellar masses, star formation rates \citep[SFRs;][]{Brinchmann+04,Kauffmann+03b}, emission-line measurements, and Galaxy Zoo morphologies \citep{Lintott+08,GalaxyZoo1}; Appendix~\ref{app:sdss_query} lists the tables and joins. Throughout, `SDSS non-AGN reference' denotes the AGN-filtered reference sample used for calibration and orientation: spectroscopic galaxies with \(0.005 \le z \le 0.0452\), extinction-corrected Petrosian \(r \le 17.77\), a valid MPA-JHU stellar mass, and finite Galaxy Zoo vote fractions after the AGN filter. It is a field-dominated reference population, not a control sample. All projected distances adopt the Planck 2015 cosmology \citep{Planck15}, as in our previous paper.

\subsection{Classifications}\label{sec:ssfr_classification}
Star-formation classes are based on SDSS total specific star-formation rates. The inherited DR12 group catalogues store \(\log\mathrm{sSFR}=\texttt{sfr\_tot\_p50}-\texttt{lgm\_tot\_p50}\). In the MPA-JHU catalogues, \(-9999\) is the missing-value sentinel of the \texttt{galSpecExtra} table \citep{Brinchmann+04,Kauffmann+03b}, so rows with unavailable components are treated as \emph{missing data}: they are excluded from the classification and from every sSFR-based figure and fraction, and are reported as counts only (Table~\ref{tab:sSFR_status}). All quenched and star-forming fractions are computed among classified galaxies. The missing-sSFR incidence is higher in \CG{} (7\% of members, 10\% of BGGs) than in the reference sample (1\%). Tracing the stored spectrum identifiers shows why: 332 of the 394 missing rows are BOSS-instrument spectra, which the MPA-JHU tables (legacy reductions only) never covered, 60 are legacy spectra whose SFR fit returned the sentinel, and 2 have no spectrum identifier; the missing rows have higher, not lower, median signal-to-noise than the classified ones (Appendix~\ref{app:sdss_query}). Galaxies with a measured sSFR are split into quenched and star-forming classes with a two-component Gaussian mixture model (GMM) in the \(\log M_\star\)--\(\log\mathrm{sSFR}\) plane; the boundary and the AGN handling are given in Appendix~\ref{app:ssfr_method} (Fig.~\ref{fig:sSFR_classification}).

Morphologies are drawn from Galaxy Zoo 1 \citep{Lintott+08,GalaxyZoo1}, whose vote fractions, debiased for the redshift-dependent classification bias \citep{Bamford+09}, are provided in \texttt{zooSpec} as \texttt{p\_el\_debiased} (elliptical) and \texttt{p\_cs\_debiased} (combined spiral). We classify a galaxy as elliptical (E) when \texttt{p\_el\_debiased} exceeds 0.5, as spiral (S) when \texttt{p\_cs\_debiased} exceeds 0.5, and as uncertain when both vote fractions are finite but neither threshold is exceeded; rows with a missing vote fraction are treated as missing Galaxy Zoo data. Galaxy Zoo~1 does not separate ellipticals from lenticulars, so our E class is a smooth, early-type class rather than a classical-elliptical class. A cross-match with the deep-learning morphologies of \citet{DominguezSanchez2018} (DS18; 2436 of 3857 unique galaxies) quantifies this: of the 942 matched E galaxies, 92\% have DS18 \(T\mathrm{Type}\leq0\), 54\% are early with \(P_{\rm S0}>0.5\), and 29\% with \(P_{\rm S0}\geq0.8\); conversely, DS18 early types with \(P_{\rm S0}>0.5\) (\(N = 730\)) fall 70\% in our E class and 19\% in S (63\% and 24\% for \(P_{\rm S0}\geq0.8\)), with the E share dropping from 77\% for face-on to 47\% for inclined systems (DS18 \(P_{\rm edge\text{-}on}<0.1\) versus \(\geq0.5\)), as expected if inclined S0s expose their discs. Throughout, ``elliptical'' and ``E'' therefore denote this smooth early-type proxy, which contains a substantial S0 population. We also retrieve the MPA-JHU age-sensitive indices \(D_n4000\) and H\(\delta_A\) \citep{Kauffmann+03b} for the stored spectra of the group galaxies, for descriptive use only (Sect.~\ref{sec:other_properties}, Appendix~\ref{app:selection}).





\subsection{Galaxy sizes}\label{sec:size_data}

Our primary size measure is the circular half-light radius of the pure S\'ersic fit in the $r$ band, \(R_{\rm chl,r}\), from the bulge+disk decomposition catalogue of \citet{Simard2011}: PSF-convolved GIM2D fits with re-derived sky levels and deblending designed for crowded fields, so the sizes are corrected for seeing. The catalogue is keyed to SDSS DR7 identifiers; we bridge our DR16 objects to their DR7 counterparts through the SkyServer \texttt{SpecDR7} cross-match table. Rows whose Simard redshift differs from the catalogue redshift by more than 0.005 are treated as wrong matches and dropped (9 rows). The Simard sizes, tabulated in kpc under the catalogue's own cosmology, are converted back to angular sizes with the catalogue plate scale and then to proper kpc with the same Planck 2015 conversion as every other projected quantity.

As a secondary, model-independent measure we use the SDSS Petrosian radii \texttt{petroR50\_r} and \texttt{petroR90\_r}, which also define the concentration index \(C = R_{90,r}/R_{50,r}\) and provide the like-for-like bridge to \citet{Coenda+12}. Petrosian radii are not corrected for seeing, and the Petrosian aperture misses roughly 20\% of the flux of an \(n=4\) profile, which biases \(R_{50,r}\) low for concentrated galaxies \citep{Blanton2001,Graham2005}. All sizes are required to lie between 0.1 and 50 kpc after re-conversion; S\'ersic fits pegged at the GIM2D bounds (\(n_g \le 0.55\) or \(n_g \ge 7.9\); 60 galaxies) are excluded from the primary sample, a cut that removes 2.5\% of the Simard-matched \CG{} members against 1.0--1.5\% in the controls and is therefore conservative with respect to a \CG{} early-type excess; Petrosian entries must satisfy \(R_{50,r}>0\), \(R_{90,r}>R_{50,r}\), and a finite positive uncertainty. The completeness of both measures is given in Appendix~\ref{app:selection}.


\subsection{Statistical approach}\label{sec:stats}

We organise the inference around one question: after accounting for stellar mass, redshift, galaxy rank, and group-scale differences, do compact groups retain a distinct galaxy population? The three controls are pre-specified, physically distinct comparison samples, so \CG{} is compared with each of them separately, and the results are reported as separate contrasts rather than as repeated tests of one null hypothesis. Galaxy-level logistic models give the adjusted population contrast (elliptical versus spiral among usable Galaxy Zoo classifications, quenched versus star-forming among valid sSFRs); group-level matching tests whether whole quartets contain a different satellite mix; and within-host models ask whether compact-subconfiguration members differ from their non-compact co-members. The covariates are stellar mass, redshift, rank, group luminosity, and the quartet velocity dispersion \(\sigma_v\); because \(\sigma_v\) from four tracers is noisy, every analysis that uses it is repeated without it (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness_details}).

The independence unit is the \emph{physical} Lim group: standard errors are clustered by physical group and resampling is blocked by physical group or matched pair, because the four galaxies of one group are not independent. We report two-sided p-values with effect sizes and confidence intervals for the planned morphology, star-formation, matching, host, and size analyses, and reserve multiplicity control for the exploratory batteries. The p-value conventions, the covariate-completeness rule, the overlap coefficient, and the treatment of the overlapping control samples in pooled summaries are collected in Appendix~\ref{app:stat_conventions}.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Morphology}\label{sec:morphology}

\CG{} galaxies are more often classified elliptical than the galaxies of every control. Tables~\ref{tab:sSFR_status} and \ref{tab:morphologies} give the raw class counts; among galaxies with a usable E or S classification, the \CG{} elliptical fraction is 59\% against 47\%, 48\%, and 32\% in \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{} (Fisher exact tests: \(p = 6.8\times 10^{-4}\), \(p = 1.9\times 10^{-3}\), and \(p<10^{-4}\)). The 38 \CG{} rows with uncertain or missing Galaxy Zoo classifications are excluded rather than assigned to either class. The shift is carried by the satellites: among usable BGG classifications the elliptical fraction is 67.9\% in \CG{} and 44.7\% in \RG{} (Fisher \(p\) = 1.00, 1.00, and 0.03 against \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{}), and restricted to star-forming galaxies the E/S mix is compatible with every control (two-sided Fisher exact test: \(p = 0.332\), \(p = 0.468\), \(p = 0.226\)), so the raw excess is not carried by star-forming ellipticals.

The excess is present at every well-populated stellar mass (Fig.~\ref{fig:mass_trends}). The morphology contrast is therefore not a mass effect, and the mass-binned view shows no \CG{}-specific quenching pattern.

The excess survives adjustment for galaxy and group properties. In logistic models of elliptical-versus-spiral classification with stellar mass, redshift, rank, group luminosity, and \(\sigma_v\) as covariates (standard errors clustered by physical group), the \CG{} odds ratio is \(2.14\) (95\% CI \([1.58, 2.90]\)) against \CB{}, \(1.67\) (95\% CI \([1.25, 2.24]\)) against \CC{}, and \(2.81\) (95\% CI \([1.79, 4.38]\)) against \RG{} (Wald tests: \(p<10^{-4}\), \(p = 5.9\times 10^{-4}\), \(p<10^{-4}\); Fig.~\ref{fig:primary_contrasts}). The ordering is physically suggestive: the excess is largest against the two \({\sim}\SepLoose\)~kpc environments (\CB{}, \RG{}) and smallest against the \({\sim}\SepCC\)~kpc projected cores (\CC{}), the control that most resembles a compact configuration, although the three estimates are not tested against one another; refitting without the noisy \(\sigma_v\) covariate moves the odds ratios by less than their standard errors (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness_details}).





The signal is carried by satellites. In the pooled secondary model over the deduplicated control catalogues (Appendix~\ref{app:pooled_models}), the satellite elliptical odds ratio is \(2.08\) (95\% CI \([1.51, 2.88]\)) (\(p<10^{-4}\)), whereas the BGG estimate is \(0.78\) (95\% CI \([0.39, 1.53]\)) (\(p = 0.464\)); the pooled all-galaxy odds ratio is \(1.77\) (\(p = 1.4\times 10^{-4}\)).

Individually matched galaxies confirm the shift. Matching 234 of the 248 \CG{} galaxies one-to-one to ordinary-group galaxies of the deduplicated control pool on stellar mass, redshift, rank, group luminosity, and \(\sigma_v\) (exact rank strata, common support, 0.2-SD caliper; maximum absolute standardized mean difference 0.08 after matching, Appendix~\ref{app:pooled_models}), the matched elliptical-fraction difference is \(0.14\) (95\% block-bootstrap interval \([0.04, 0.24]\); \(p = 0.007\)), rising to \(0.19\) for the 130 satellite pairs alone (\(p = 0.004\)).







Within their own host groups, compact-group members occupy the elliptical-rich centres rather than forming a distinct population. For the \nGrEmb{} embedded and \nGrPre{} predominant systems, whose quartets live inside richer Lim groups (54 distinct hosts, since None Lim groups each host two compact groups; 958 member galaxies, 224 of them in a \CG{}), we compare the \CG{} members with the non-\CG{} members of the same host by conditional logistic regression stratified by host, which removes every host-level property by design, with stellar mass, host luminosity rank, and projected host-centric radius as covariates. The elliptical odds ratio for \CG{} membership is \(1.47\) (95\% CI \([0.92, 2.35]\); \(p = 0.108\); 791 galaxies in 53 informative strata), but rises to \(1.90\) (\([1.23, 2.95]\); \(p = 0.004\)) when the radius covariate is dropped. The difference between the two fits is the pattern expected if \CG{} galaxies preferentially occupy the early-type-rich central regions of their hosts; the radius-adjusted interval still permits moderate effects. A fixed-effects GLM gives the same direction (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness_details}).


\subsection{Star formation at fixed morphology}\label{sec:star_formation}

The star-formation contrast is weaker, control-dependent, and largely a consequence of the morphology mix. In the raw comparison, Fisher's exact test on the star-forming fraction gives \(p = 0.481\), \(p = 0.262\), and \(p = 3\times 10^{-4}\) against \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{} for all galaxies, and \(p = 0.302\), \(p = 0.114\), and \(p = 2.3\times 10^{-4}\) for satellites (two-sided Fisher exact test), the deficit being clearest against \RG{}; the raw \RG{} contrast survives every extreme assignment of the missing-sSFR galaxies (Appendix~\ref{app:ssfr_method}). After adjustment for the same covariates as in Sect.~\ref{sec:morphology}, the quenched odds ratio is \(1.80\) (95\% CI \([1.26, 2.58]\)) against \CB{} (\(p = 0.001\)) but only \(1.25\) (95\% CI \([0.91, 1.71]\)) against \CC{} (\(p = 0.173\)) and \(1.40\) (95\% CI \([0.80, 2.47]\)) against \RG{} (\(p = 0.238\); Fig.~\ref{fig:primary_contrasts}).

At fixed morphology the samples are compatible. The conditional quenched fractions among sSFR-classified galaxies are \(P(\mathrm{Q}\,|\,\mathrm{E}) = \qEcg\), \qEcb, \qEcc, and \qErg{} for \CG{}, \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{} (homogeneity \(\chi^2\) test, \(p = \pQEhom\); Table~\ref{tab:quenched_by_morphology}), and a Kitagawa decomposition of each raw \CG{}-minus-control quenched-fraction difference into a morphology-mix term and a conditional term leaves the conditional terms consistent with zero: \(\condCB\) \([\condCBlo, \condCBhi]\) versus \CB{}, \(\condCC\) \([\condCClo, \condCChi]\) versus \CC{}, and \(\condRG\) \([\condRGlo, \condRGhi]\) versus \RG{} (group-blocked 95\% intervals). The individually matched quenched-fraction difference is likewise small, \(0.03\) (95\% CI \([-0.06, 0.12]\); \(p = 0.526\)). The quenched odds-ratio pattern is thus what the different morphology mixes imply, with no additional quenching at fixed vote-based morphology. Nor does the interpretation depend on the GMM boundary: with a fixed threshold, $\log_{10}(\mathrm{sSFR}/\mathrm{yr}^{-1})=-11.0$, the \CG{} and \RG{} satellite star-forming fractions are 41.4\% and 60.2\% (difference \(-18.8\) points; \(p = 5.3\times 10^{-4}\)), so the quenched/star-forming contrast remains the more model-dependent of the two.







Star-forming \CG{} galaxies do not sit off the star-forming main sequence (SFMS). Relative to an order-2 polynomial SFMS fitted to the star-forming galaxies of the SDSS reference sample (Appendix~\ref{app:baseline_details}), the median residual of \CG{} star-forming galaxies exceeds that of the controls by \(0.056\), \(0.058\), and \(0.046\) dex for \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{} (68\% group-bootstrap intervals \([0.03, 0.10]\), \([0.04, 0.10]\), \([-0.04, 0.10]\); sign-crossing \(p = 0.06\), \(p = 0.03\), \(p = 0.59\); Fig.~\ref{fig:sfms_residual_distributions}a), whereas the mean paired difference over the 39 individually matched pairs in which both members are star-forming is \(-0.15\) dex (\([-0.31, 0.01]\); \(p = 0.06\)). The two signs are not in conflict: they are different statistics of different subsets (on the same 39 pairs the median paired difference is \(-0.08\) dex, Wilcoxon \(p = 0.26\)), and both reflect the mass dependence of the residuals about the field-fitted relation: control star-forming galaxies above \(\log(M_\star/M_\odot) = 10.3\) lie \(\simeq 0.2\) dex below it while the 23 massive star-forming \CG{} galaxies do not, which raises the raw medians, whereas the matched star-forming controls are less massive than their \CG{} partners. We therefore treat SFMS residuals as a secondary diagnostic. Quenched \CG{} galaxies lie slightly below the quenched sequence of the reference sample (Fig.~\ref{fig:sfms_residual_distributions}b; order-4 fit, median offsets \(-0.07\), \(-0.06\), and \(-0.15\) dex against \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{}; \(p = 0.002\), \(p = 0.013\), \(p = 2\times 10^{-4}\)); MPA-JHU sSFRs of quenched galaxies are largely \(D_n4000\)-calibrated, so these residuals are weakly informative.

\subsection{Compact-group classes}\label{sec:cg_classes}

The elliptical excess belongs to the compact groups that live inside richer hosts. Figure~\ref{fig:cg4_classes} shows the satellite elliptical fraction \(f_{\rm E} = N_{\rm E}/(N_{\rm E}+N_{\rm S})\) for the three Zheng--Shen classes of Sect.~\ref{sec:samples}: \(f_{\rm E} = \feSatEmb\) for the \nGrEmb{} embedded and \(\feSatPre\) for the \nGrPre{} predominant systems, against \(\feSatIso\) for the \nGrIso{} isolated ones (Wilson 95\% intervals; Table~\ref{tab:zheng_shen_fe}). The isolated compact groups are compatible with the loose \RG{} groups (group-level permutation test on the per-group mean satellite \(f_{\rm E}\), \(p = \pIsoRG\)) and sit below the rest of \CG{} (\(p = \pIsoRest\)), whereas the two embedded-type classes are indistinguishable from each other (predominant-versus-embedded odds ratio at fixed stellar mass \(0.97\), \(p = 0.94\)). The host halo masses differ strongly between the classes (median \(\log M_{200c} = \lMIso\), \lMEmb, and \lMPre{} for isolated, embedded, and predominant systems), yet adding \(\log M_{200c}\) to the class model changes neither the class coefficients nor the conclusion (\(\log M_{200c}\) odds ratio \(0.80\) per standard deviation, \(p = 0.31\)); with only \nGrIso{} isolated groups the class contrast itself is exploratory, and an exploratory battery of other group-scale descriptors is given in Appendix~\ref{app:battery}. In \citet{Tricottet+25} the embedded and predominant labels were interchanged relative to the definition of \citet{Zheng&Shen21} adopted here, which affects the class counts and host masses quoted there but none of the isolated-group results.

\subsection{Local projected density}\label{sec:tidal_index}

Local projected density accounts for part, but not a well-determined part, of the excess. For each of the 6076 catalogue rows we compute a pairwise tidal index \(T_i = \sum_j M_{\star,j}/R_{ij}^3\) over the selected quartet co-members (\(R_{ij}\) in proper kpc; co-members without a stellar mass contribute zero, so \(T_i\) is a lower bound in those groups) and refit the elliptical model, adjusted for stellar mass and satellite status and clustered by physical group, with and without the index on the three control catalogues pooled \emph{without} objid deduplication (unlike the pooled models of Appendix~\ref{app:pooled_models}). The \CG{} odds ratio falls from 1.95 (\(p<10^{-4}\)) to 1.39 (\(p = 0.031\)) when the index is added, the index itself having an odds ratio of 1.42 per \(\sdlogT\)-dex standard deviation of \(\log T_i\) (\(p<10^{-4}\)). Because the index is built from the same projected separations that define a compact group, conditioning on it partly conditions on the selection, so this attenuation is not evidence that local density mediates the morphology contrast. Nor is the residual \CG{} term robust on common support: the \CG{} and control \(\log T_i\) distributions overlap with coefficient 0.54 but their medians differ by 1.1 dex, and the residual odds ratio becomes 1.40 with a spline in \(\log T_i\), 1.27 after deduplicating the control pool (\(p = 0.12\)), and 1.22 on the common-support window (\(p = 0.20\)). The index is a proxy for immediate neighbours in projection, not a measurement of tidal history; the restriction to quartet co-members is innocuous (Appendix~\ref{app:selection}).


\subsection{Other galaxy properties}\label{sec:other_properties}





None of the other galaxy properties shows an offset as robust as the morphology excess (Fig.~\ref{fig:property_trends}). Strong \(\mathrm{H}\alpha\) emission (\(\mathrm{H}\alpha\,\mathrm{EW} \leq -3\,\text{\AA}\), emission negative) is rarer in \CG{}: 32\% of members against 41\%, 43\%, and 57\% in \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{} (Fisher \(p = 0.01\), \(p = 0.002\), \(p<10^{-4}\)), but this morphology-blind fraction largely restates the morphology contrast, since LINER-like emission is common in early-type galaxies, and it is not adjusted for the samples' different morphology mixes. The AGN-like fraction among BPT-classified galaxies (above the \citealt{Kewley+01} line, or flagged as AGN without line ratios; composites between the \citealt{Kauffmann+03a} and \citealt{Kewley+01} lines) is 38\% in \CG{}, and its mass- and environment-adjusted \CG{} odds ratio is 1.17 (\(p = 0.36\)). The \(D_n4000\) index follows stellar mass in every sample; the \CG{} medians sit above the controls only in the \(10.0\)--\(10.5\) bin (\(D_n4000 = 1.77\) against 1.59, 1.68, and 1.56), where the \CG{} elliptical fraction also peaks relative to the controls, and coincide with them elsewhere; a post-starburst classification from \(D_n4000\) and H\(\delta_A\) is deferred to a follow-up paper.

\section{Interpretation and comparison with previous work}\label{sec:discussion}




\subsection{An elliptical satellite excess linked to dense group cores}\label{sec:morph_local_density}

Compact groups of four galaxies host more elliptical satellites than any of the three ordinary-group comparison samples, and the size of the excess follows the compactness of the comparison: it is largest against the \({\sim}\SepLoose\)~kpc \CB{} and \RG{} quartets and smallest against the \({\sim}\SepCC\)~kpc projected cores \CC{} (Sect.~\ref{sec:morphology}). Within their own hosts, compact-group members show the excess only when their central location is not held fixed, which is the behaviour expected if they occupy the early-type-rich centres of their groups. The within-host estimator refines the surrounding-host comparison of \citet{Montaguth2026NonIsolated} by asking whether morphology differs beyond shared host and central location; samples, morphology definitions, and structural measurements differ, so this refines rather than contradicts their result. The scale of the difference depends on what is held fixed: restricting the individually matched comparison to satellites raises the elliptical-fraction difference to \(0.19\), and present-day stellar mass may itself encode part of a galaxy's environmental history, so the fixed-mass estimates describe the remaining association rather than the total accumulated transformation.

The local projected-density experiment of Sect.~\ref{sec:tidal_index} is consistent with this picture without proving it: the tidal-index term is itself strongly predictive of elliptical morphology and attenuates the \CG{} coefficient (from \(1.95\) to \(1.39\)), as expected if compact groups select galaxies in locally dense projected configurations where repeated close companions, rather than the catalogue label, are linked to the elliptical population. Because the index is entangled with the selection and its residual term is not stable on common support, the causal ordering of selection, density, and morphology is not identified by projected snapshots. This reading is consistent with compact groups tracing the high-density, high-interaction tail of group environments---some genuinely compact, others dense cores or substructures inside larger haloes---and with the stronger structural signatures reported for non-isolated compact groups in recent S-PLUS work \citep{Montaguth2023,Montaguth2025,Montaguth2026MassSize,Montaguth2026NonIsolated}. The \nGrIso{} isolated compact groups are compatible with loose \RG{} groups in satellite elliptical fraction and lie below the rest of \CG{} (Sect.~\ref{sec:cg_classes}); this is consistent with a larger-scale host contribution, but the isolated sample is too small to separate the explanations, and the host halo mass adds nothing to the class contrast or to the adjusted models (Appendix~\ref{app:selection}).

Stellar-population diagnostics could in principle separate an accumulated enrichment history from a shorter star-formation timescale, but we defer a stellar-metallicity analysis for two reasons. No quenching excess is detected at fixed morphology (Sect.~\ref{sec:star_formation}), so a strangulation-type metallicity test of the kind proposed by \citet{Peng+15} would have little leverage here, and the single-fibre SDSS spectra sample only the central \({\sim}2\) kpc of the galaxies at the sample redshifts, where the gradients of both metallicity and age are steepest.

\subsection{Elliptical-vote morphologies and transition galaxies}\label{sec:s0_degeneracy}

Our E class is a smooth early-type proxy with a substantial S0 content (Sect.~\ref{sec:ssfr_classification}), so the excess is one of early-type-like galaxies rather than of classical ellipticals. Whether the excess is made of S0s, of ellipticals, or of both is a question that Galaxy Zoo~1 cannot answer and that we reserve for a dedicated, pre-registered analysis of the DS18 classes by environment. This caveat matters when comparing with the S-PLUS studies of compact-group transition galaxies and \(R_{\rm e}\)--S\'ersic-index trends \citep{Montaguth2023,Montaguth2025,Montaguth2026MassSize,Montaguth2026NonIsolated}: their structural measurements and transition populations probe pathways, whereas our control hierarchy tests which apparent \CG{} signatures survive across genuine four-member, luminosity-selected, and BGG-centred projected-core quartets.

\subsection{Why the star-formation and size signals are weaker}\label{sec:weaker_signals}





The star-formation contrast is what the morphology mix implies. At fixed vote-based morphology the quenched fractions of the four samples agree and the Kitagawa conditional terms are consistent with zero (Sect.~\ref{sec:star_formation}), so the quenched odds ratios of Fig.~\ref{fig:primary_contrasts} measure the different proportions of ellipticals, not an additional quenching channel; the raw satellite deficit of star formation against \RG{} survives every assignment of the missing-sSFR galaxies but is control-dependent once mass and rank are held fixed. The non-detections are also limited by the spectroscopy. \CG{} has more catalogued galaxies with a nearest neighbour below the 55-arcsec fibre-collision scale than the pooled controls (19\% versus 8\%), and \CC{}, selected as a compact-core analogue, has the highest close-pair fraction of the controls (10\%). Additional close companions may therefore be preferentially missing in \CG{}, hiding short-lived starbursts or post-starburst phases and biasing the pairwise tidal index downward, and the MPA-JHU aperture corrections \citep{Brinchmann+04,Salim+07} may be less secure for interacting or blended members. We therefore read the sSFR, main-sequence, and \(\mathrm{H}\alpha\) results as conservative non-detections rather than as proof of no star-formation effect.






The size result carries a parallel message. \citet{Coenda+12} found compact-group galaxies to be smaller at fixed luminosity than loose-group and field samples, especially early types, using SDSS Petrosian half-light radii; against three regular-group controls, with seeing-corrected model sizes, cluster-robust adjusted models, one-to-one matching, and crowding tests, we find no size offset at fixed stellar mass (satellite coefficient \(-0.010\) dex, bounding any mean \CG{} size deficit to roughly \(0.04\) dex; matched Simard difference \(-0.019\) dex). This is compatible with a compact-group versus field offset if ordinary-group cores have already undergone comparable structural processing. A negative fixed-mass size offset could reflect tidal truncation but also higher bulge dominance or residual blending in crowded fields; the morphology-stratified fits, the Petrosian cross-check, and the crowding exclusions of Appendix~\ref{app:size_details} bound both readings.


\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We have tested whether galaxies in \CG{} compact groups remain special after removing split systems, comparing them with \RG, \CB, and \CC{} through individual-galaxy, group-composition, and shared-host contrasts. The inference applies to this luminous, volume-limited, exactly four-member non-split HMCG sample rather than to all compact groups.

The main result is an elliptical satellite excess. \CG{} galaxies are more often classified elliptical than all three regular-group comparisons after accounting for galaxy and group properties; the excess is strongest against \CB{} and \RG{} and weakest against the projected cores \CC{}, matched quartets and satellite-mass-balanced variants show the same hierarchy, BGGs show no corresponding difference, and within their hosts \CG{} members show no excess beyond their central locations. Vote-threshold, continuous-vote, S\'ersic-index, close-pair, and independent DS18 checks support the result, and a projected tidal-density proxy attenuates it in the way expected if local density contributes, although projected data identify neither mediation nor a unique mechanism.

The star-formation result is the morphology mix seen through a different lens: at fixed vote-based morphology the quenched fractions agree, the matched quenched-fraction difference is small, and star-forming \CG{} galaxies show no robust main-sequence offset. At fixed stellar mass the seeing-corrected satellite size estimate is \(-0.010\) dex with an interval including zero, so we do not claim a compact-group size effect. Colours, nuclear activity, magnitude gaps, \(\mathrm{H}\alpha\) emission, \(D_n4000\), and projected phase-space trends identify no simple alternative driver.

We therefore interpret \CG{} systems as hosting a population shifted towards smooth, early-type-like morphologies, consistent with structural transformation accumulated in dense group cores and with preprocessing, and without evidence for a compact-group-specific quenching mechanism at the present epoch; the isolated subset is too small to isolate a distinct mode. Explicit E--S0--spiral and transition-galaxy classifications, bulge--disc structure, homogeneous halo masses, and stellar-population diagnostics will be needed to determine whether the satellite excess reflects disc fading, tidal heating, or merger-driven transformation.

\section{sSFR classification details}\label{app:ssfr_method}

We identify BPT-classifiable galaxies by requiring measured values of both emission-line ratios
\[
\log_{10}\!\bigl([\mathrm{N\,II}]\lambda6584/\mathrm{H}\alpha\bigr)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\log_{10}\!\bigl([\mathrm{O\,III}]\lambda5007/\mathrm{H}\beta\bigr).
\]
Among these galaxies, we flag AGN-like systems if they satisfy either
\[
\log_{10}\frac{[\mathrm{N\,II}]}{\mathrm{H}\alpha} > 0,
\]
or if they lie above the empirical demarcation of \citet{Kauffmann+03a}:
\[
\log_{10}\frac{[\mathrm{O\,III}]}{\mathrm{H}\beta}
> \frac{0.61}{\log_{10}([\mathrm{N\,II}]/\mathrm{H}\alpha)-0.05} \;+\; 1.3,
\]
in which case they are excluded from the GMM calibration sample. This calibration flag is deliberately more inclusive than the three-class BPT scheme of Sect.~\ref{sec:other_properties}, in which composite galaxies lie between the \citet{Kauffmann+03a} and \citet{Kewley+01} demarcations and only galaxies above the \citet{Kewley+01} line are labelled AGN-like, because its sole purpose is to keep possible AGN contamination out of the sSFR calibration. Galaxies with missing diagnostic line ratios are BPT-unclassified, not proven non-AGN; they are retained for the sSFR status assignment when valid SDSS stellar mass and sSFR estimates are available, while the AGN-like analysis treats them separately.
Reference-sample rows with unavailable \texttt{specsfr\_tot\_p50}, and group-sample rows with unavailable \texttt{sfr\_tot\_p50} or \texttt{lgm\_tot\_p50}, including the \(-9999\) sentinel, have no valid sSFR estimate; they are treated as missing data throughout: excluded from the GMM calibration where applicable, never assigned an sSFR class, omitted from every sSFR figure, and reported as counts only (Sect.~\ref{sec:ssfr_classification}).
Galaxies with a measured sSFR are divided into quenched and star-forming classes with a two-component Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) in the \(\log M_\star\)-\(\log\mathrm{sSFR}\) plane \citep{McLachlanPeel2000}; the boundary is the locus where the two component posterior probabilities are equal, and the resulting classification for the SDSS non-AGN reference is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:sSFR_classification}. A fixed-threshold check confirms that the qualitative hierarchy is not tied to the exact GMM split (Sect.~\ref{sec:star_formation}).

\section{Statistical conventions}\label{app:stat_conventions}

\paragraph{Independence unit.} The unit for every inferential contrast among the group catalogues is the \emph{physical} Lim group: standard errors are clustered by Lim group (unifying the overlapping control definitions, with \CG{} systems assigned to their host Lim group, or to their own identifier when they have no Lim counterpart), and resampling of grouped contrasts is blocked by physical group or by matched group/pair block. Pooled models across the three controls are secondary summaries computed after deduplicating the overlapping control samples to one row per physical galaxy, with the kept label following the priority \RG{} \(>\) \CB{} \(>\) \CC{} (Appendix~\ref{app:pooled_models}). Purely descriptive reference-sample diagnostics report their own unit where they appear.

\paragraph{Tests and p-values.} Descriptive comparisons use two-sided Fisher exact tests for \(2\times2\) tables and rank-sum or bootstrap comparisons for distributions and median differences; Spearman and Pearson coefficients are diagnostics of monotonic or linear trends only. Paired sign-flip permutation p-values use add-one tail counts, \(p = (k+1)/(B+1)\) with \(k\) the number of permutations at least as extreme as the observation; bootstrap sign-crossing p-values are \(2\min[(k_{\le 0}+1)/(B+1),(k_{\ge 0}+1)/(B+1)]\), capped at unity, where \(k_{\le 0}\) and \(k_{\ge 0}\) count resampled differences on either side of zero; displayed p-values are floored at \(10^{-4}\). Adjusted odds ratios come from binomial generalised linear models with cluster-robust covariance; continuous covariates are standardised, so coefficients of continuous terms are per standard deviation of the fitted frame. Multiplicity control is reserved for the exploratory batteries: Holm adjustment for the colour, measurement-availability, and magnitude-gap screens, and Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate control for the dominance, morphology--environment, and second-order screens; the Holm values across the three per-control matched contrasts (Table~\ref{tab:group_match_per_control}) are bookkeeping, since the controls are separate pre-specified comparisons.


\paragraph{Covariates.} The velocity dispersion \(\sigma_v\) that enters models and matching is the quartet-level gapper estimate \citep{Wainer&Thissen76} on the four selected members' redshifts, converted as \(c\,\sigma_z/(1+\bar z)\), for \emph{all} samples, including \CB{} and \CC{} quartets embedded in richer hosts (this is how the spurious Lim 3688 quartets of Sect.~\ref{sec:samples} reach \({\simeq}2000\kms\)). With four tracers the estimator is intrinsically noisy: in a Gaussian Monte Carlo of \(10^5\) quartets, \(\sigma_{\rm gapper}/\sigma_{\rm true}\) has median 0.96 with a 16--84\% range of 0.57--1.42. A candidate covariate enters an adjusted model when it is non-missing for at least 65\% of that model's frame (matching variables require 70\%); in practice \(\sigma_v\), group luminosity, stellar mass, redshift, and rank are complete for every quartet and always enter, and no harmonised group-mass covariate is available (Appendix~\ref{app:selection}).


\paragraph{Overlap coefficient.} Where two distributions are compared for common support (compactness in Sect.~\ref{sec:samples}, \(\log T_i\) in Sect.~\ref{sec:tidal_index}), the overlap coefficient is the integral of the minimum of the two kernel density estimates, between 0 (disjoint) and 1 (identical).

\section{Main-sequence and quenched-sequence details}\label{app:baseline_details}

We model the star-forming main sequence as a function of stellar mass with polynomials of order 1--4 fitted to the star-forming galaxies of the SDSS non-AGN reference sample; the diagnostic is the residual RMS scatter, not a formal reduced \(\chi^2\), because no per-galaxy uncertainty model is used. We adopt order 2, the lowest order with the smallest five-fold cross-validated RMS (0.305 dex, against 0.306 dex for order 1), and compute the residual of each star-forming galaxy with respect to it; a positive residual means a higher sSFR at fixed stellar mass than the reference relation. In Sect.~\ref{sec:star_formation}, \(\Delta\) is the \CG{}-minus-control median residual with the central 68\% group-bootstrap interval on the median difference and the two-sided sign-crossing probability (10\,000 resamples). The quenched sequence of Fig.~\ref{fig:sfms_residual_distributions}b is obtained with the same procedure on the 7705 quenched galaxies of the reference sample: order 4 has the smallest cross-validated RMS (0.331 dex), and the residuals are attached to the quenched galaxies of the four samples.


Within \CG, the star-forming main-sequence offsets of the embedded, isolated, and predominant classes are statistically consistent with one another.

\subsection{Magnitude gaps}\label{app:magnitude_gaps}
We define \(\Delta m_{12}=M_{r,2}-M_{r,1}\) and \(\Delta m_{14}=M_{r,4}-M_{r,1}\), so that a more dominant BGG has a positive, larger gap; large gaps are the classic optical signature of fossil-like assembly \citep{Ponman+94,Jones+03,Dariush+10}.


Under the Holm adjustment of the magnitude-gap battery, larger gaps appear only against \CB{}: for \(\Delta m_{12}\), the \CG{} and \CB{} medians are \(1.17\) and \(0.85\) mag (adjusted \(p<10^{-4}\)), and for \(\Delta m_{14}\) they are \(2.56\) and \(1.90\) mag (adjusted \(p<10^{-4}\)); the \CC{} and \RG{} differences are weak for both gap definitions.



A deduplicated pooled sensitivity gives control medians of \(0.86\) mag for \(\Delta m_{12}\) and \(1.91\) mag for \(\Delta m_{14}\) (adjusted \(p<10^{-4}\) and \(p<10^{-4}\)); because the deduplicated control side is dominated by \CB{}-selected systems, it is not evidence for a control-robust gap excess, and the morphology signal is not accompanied by a comparably robust excess in these fossil-like indicators.



In the 14-test gap-correlation screen over all 1519 \CG{} and control quartets (control quartets entering once per label, without deduplication), the relation between \(\Delta m_{12}\) and the quenched satellite fraction has \(\rho_{\rm S}=-0.18\) (adjusted \(p<10^{-4}\)).





\subsection{Projected phase space}\label{app:phase_space}

We ask whether the \CG{}--\RG{} satellite contrasts vary with projected position relative to the BGG, using 174 \CG{} and 166 \RG{} satellites ranked by projected BGG-centric distance and, when available, \(|\Delta v_{\rm BGG}|/\sigma_v\); such coordinates are statistical proxies for infall state, not orbital reconstructions \citep{Mahajan+11,Oman&Hudson16,Wetzel+13}.

In the innermost satellite-distance bin, the quenched fractions are 68.3\% for \CG{} and 48.2\% for \RG, a difference of \(20.1\) percentage points; the corresponding elliptical fractions are 64.7\% and 35.6\% (difference \(29.1\) points).

The quenched-fraction contrast is similar between the inner and outer distance ranks, so the data do not isolate the \CG{} offset to the immediate BGG environment.



After adjusting for stellar mass, redshift, distance bin, and available group-scale controls, the quenched-status model gives a \CG{} odds ratio of 1.18 (95\% CI 0.43--3.24; \(p = 0.742\)), and the analogous elliptical model gives 2.42 (\(p = 0.078\)).


A nearest-neighbour mass--redshift matched check on 126 \CG{}--\RG{} satellite pairs gives matched quenched- and elliptical-fraction differences of \(9.5\) and \(18.7\) percentage points.

In compact groups the member counts are small, velocity dispersions are noisy, and projection mixes recently accreted and long-resident satellites, so these bins locate where the contrast appears; they are not a dynamical clock.

\subsection{Dominance}\label{app:dominance}









We split groups into dominated systems, where the BGG contributes more than 60\% of the group luminosity, and non-dominated systems. Because this appendix screens many group properties, \(p_\mathrm{adj}\) denotes the false-discovery-rate adjustment of Appendix~\ref{app:stat_conventions}; Cliff's \(\delta\) is the rank-based effect size. The split does not produce a compact-group signal by itself: across the control samples, the clearest binary separation is in group luminosity, dominated systems having lower \(L_\mathrm{group}\) than their non-dominated counterparts (Control$_{4B}$: $9.1\times 10^{10}$ versus $1.28\times 10^{11}$ $L_\odot$, \(p_\mathrm{adj}<10^{-4}\), \(\delta=-0.55\); Control$_{4C}$: $9.66\times 10^{10}$ versus $1.07\times 10^{11}$ $L_\odot$, \(p_\mathrm{adj}<10^{-4}\), \(\delta=-0.25\); RG$_4$: $7.09\times 10^{10}$ versus $9.82\times 10^{10}$ $L_\odot$, \(p_\mathrm{adj}=1\times 10^{-4}\), \(\delta=-0.59\)), the negative \(\delta\) meaning that dominated groups occupy the lower-luminosity side of each comparison.




Splitting instead at each sample's median BGG luminosity fraction (\(f_{L,\rm BGG} = 0.63\), 0.52, 0.61, and 0.59 for \CG{}, \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{}) gives the same picture: the FDR-significant separations are \CB{} in  $L_\mathrm{group}$ ($L_\odot$) (\(\delta = -0.53\), \(p_{\rm adj} = 0.000\)), \CC{} in  $L_\mathrm{group}$ ($L_\odot$) (\(\delta = -0.24\), \(p_{\rm adj} = 0.000\)), \RG{} in  $L_\mathrm{group}$ ($L_\odot$) (\(\delta = -0.58\), \(p_{\rm adj} = 0.000\)), \CB{} in $\Delta_\mathrm{BGG-cen}/ \langle R_{ij} \rangle$ (\(\delta = -0.17\), \(p_{\rm adj} = 0.000\)), \CB{} in $\sigma_v$ (km s$^{-1}$) (\(\delta = -0.12\), \(p_{\rm adj} = 0.024\)), \CC{} in $\Delta_\mathrm{BGG-cen}/ \langle R_{ij} \rangle$ (\(\delta = -0.11\), \(p_{\rm adj} = 0.028\)), and none involves \CG{}.


The spiral-fraction correlations show a broader environmental pattern rather than a clean dominated/non-dominated dichotomy. The most coherent set appears in \CC, where \(S_\mathrm{frac}\) increases with \(\left\langle R_{ij}\right\rangle\) in both dominated and non-dominated groups (\(\rho_\mathrm{S}=0.30\), \(p<10^{-4}\) and \(\rho_\mathrm{S}=0.26\), \(p<10^{-4}\)), while weaker negative trends are found with \(\sigma_v\) and \(L_\mathrm{group}\). \CB{} contributes only weaker non-dominated-group correlations. In \CG, the reported trends occur in small subsets, with \(S_\mathrm{frac}\) decreasing with \(L_\mathrm{group}\) among dominated groups (\(\rho_\mathrm{S}=-0.40\), 36 groups) and with \(\Delta V_\mathrm{BGG}/\sigma_v\) among non-dominated groups (\(\rho_\mathrm{S}=-0.45\), 24 groups); we treat these as supporting diagnostics rather than the main dominance result. The full distributional diagnostics are provided in the online supplement.

\subsection{Morphology versus Zheng--Shen class, magnitude gap, and BGG dominance}\label{app:zheng_shen}

\subsection{Crossing time}\label{app:crossing_time}







Group radii and \(t_\mathrm{cross}\) use projected proper separations from the Planck 2015 angular-diameter distance, correcting the luminosity-distance convention used for this diagnostic in \citet{Tricottet+25}; the values decrease by 3.3--9.6\% (median 7.2\%), without changing the reported correlations at quoted precision. The crossing-time diagnostic mainly separates systems by dynamical scale rather than by luminosity: shorter \(t_\mathrm{cross}\) systems have larger virial mass-to-light ratios and virial masses, with correlations of \(\rho_\mathrm{S}=-0.39\) (\(p<10^{-4}\)) with \(\log(\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{VT}/L_r)\) and \(\rho_\mathrm{S}=-0.33\) (\(p<10^{-4}\)) with \(\log(\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{VT})\) over 816 groups, whereas the relation with total group luminosity is formally detected but very weak (\(\rho_\mathrm{S}=0.08\)). We therefore read \(t_\mathrm{cross}\) as a dynamical compactness indicator tied to virial mass and mass-to-light ratio, with little independent leverage from \(L_\mathrm{group}\).

\subsection{Optical colours}\label{sec:optical_colours}


We derive the $(u-r)$, $(u-g)$, $(g-r)$ and $(r-i)$ colours from the matched SDSS photometry and model each colour jointly with $\log(M_\star/M_\odot)$ and redshift. The retained colour-analysis matches include 110 of 248 galaxies in CG$_4$, 931 of 2792 galaxies in Control$_{4B}$, 1153 of 2812 galaxies in Control$_{4C}$, 102 of 224 galaxies in RG$_4$. This stricter subset differs from the completeness of Fig.~\ref{fig:data_availability}, whose colour row only requires the four derived SDSS colour columns to be present in the final catalogue (217 of 248 \CG{} galaxies), whereas the colour analysis also requires successful matched-photometry retention and the covariates used below; the fractions retained are 44.4\% in CG4, 33.3\% in Control4B, 41.0\% in Control4C, 45.5\% in RG4. The colour subset is not representative of the full catalogues: matched galaxies are systematically less massive, more actively star-forming, and more frequently satellites than unmatched galaxies.

The raw catalogue colour--mass fits indicate that \CG{} does not simply follow the same colour--mass relation as every comparison sample: common slopes are rejected in all four colours, with the directional slope differences having small adjusted p-values mainly in the bluer colours and depending on the adopted comparison catalogue. In the satellite-only comparison, the fitted offsets and slopes likewise depend on the control definition. We refit satellite colours with cluster-robust standard errors: in the pooled mass- and redshift-adjusted comparison, the negative \CG{} coefficients are weak, and adding morphology and sSFR class does not reveal a clear negative coefficient. These coefficients remain control-sample dependent, with non-uniform split-sample behaviour, so they do not identify a global colour shift or a simple interaction-triggered star-formation population (Fig.~\ref{fig:colour_robustness_coefficients}). Residual colour trends with projected BGG-centric distance are consistent with stronger processing near the group centre, and redder BGGs tend to have redder mean satellite populations in the combined group sample, but both use the same biased colour subset and are exploratory; the \CG{} satellite blue-fraction correlation with crossing time, \(\rho_\mathrm{S}=0.365\) (adjusted \(p=0.050\)), is a boundary result that does not change this reading. Detailed colour--mass, satellite, and radial diagnostics are provided in the online supplement.

\section{Robustness details}\label{app:robustness_details}









\paragraph{Crowding.} Galaxy Zoo morphology is image-based, so crowded closest pairs could bias the votes. After excluding galaxies whose nearest projected neighbour within the catalogue group lies within 55 arcsec (retaining 200 \CG{} and 2534 \CC{} galaxies), the \CG{} all-row elliptical fraction \emph{rises} relative to the full-sample value of Table~\ref{tab:morphologies} (52.5\% against 40.1\% for \CC{}, all retained rows including uncertain and missing classes), and the \CG{}--\CC{} contrast remains clear. In the joint model, the close-neighbour flag has OR \(1.98\) (\(p = 6.1\times 10^{-4}\)), while the \CG{} coefficient at fixed flag status is OR \(1.76\) (\(p = 2.6\times 10^{-4}\)); the former compares close with non-close galaxies, the latter \CG{} with controls at fixed flag status, so neither contradicts the rising raw \CG{} fraction after exclusion. The checks do not prove immunity to crowding, but they rule out the closest-pair artefact as the sole origin of the result.





\paragraph{Without \(\sigma_v\).} Refitting the per-control and pooled models without the \(\sigma_v\) covariate, on identical samples and with identical clustering, moves the elliptical odds ratios from 2.14 to 2.22 (\CB{}), 1.67 to 1.68 (\CC{}), and 2.81 to 2.76 (\RG{}), less than their standard errors. The galaxy-level match retains the same 234 \CG{} galaxies with an elliptical-fraction difference of \(0.16\). The per-control group-level differences become \(\Delta = 0.28\), \(0.10\), and \(0.25\) against \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{} (permutation \(p = 0.0001\), \(0.056\), \(0.0016\)). The \CB{} change reflects the composition of the matched set rather than the \CG{} side: the with- and without-\(\sigma_v\) matches share only 7 of 54 control groups, the matched-control mean elliptical-satellite fraction changes by \(-0.12\) while the \CG{} mean is unchanged, and without \(\sigma_v\) the matched controls are dynamically cooler than their \CG{} partners (residual \(\sigma_v\) standardized mean difference \(0.27\), medians 160 against 129 \(\kms\)) and less well balanced in redshift, so the with-\(\sigma_v\) match remains the primary one and the two values bracket the \CB{} contrast.







\paragraph{Within-host estimators.} The conditional-logit sample of Sect.~\ref{sec:morphology} contains 791 galaxies in 53 host strata. A binomial GLM with host fixed-effect dummies and cluster-robust standard errors gives OR \(1.57\) (95\% CI \([1.02, 2.43]\); \(p = 0.041\)); we prefer the conditional logit because it eliminates the host intercepts rather than estimating many indicators from small strata. The within-host quenched odds ratio is \(0.75\) (95\% CI \([0.45, 1.25]\); \(p = 0.269\); 915 complete cases), and \(1.22\) without the radius covariate (\(p = 0.412\)). A host-BGG alignment check supports the satellite interpretation: 56 of 62 non-split \CG{} systems have the same quartet and Lim-host BGG, the 6 misaligned systems are embedded systems whose host BGG is brighter than the compact-group BGG, and all 186 quartet-rank \CG{} satellites are Lim-host satellites; restricting \CG{} to the 56 BGG-aligned systems leaves the three satellite elliptical contrasts stable (against \CC{}, OR \(1.98\)).




\paragraph{DS18 classification.} The DS18 cross-match of Sect.~\ref{sec:ssfr_classification} covers 173/248 (70\%) of the \CG{} galaxies and 2263/3609 (63\%) of the deduplicated controls, i.e. the DR7-era population; it is not worse in \CG{}, but neither rate establishes representativeness. With the DS18 \(T\mathrm{Type}\leq0\) outcome and the per-control covariates, standardisation, and physical-group clustering of Sect.~\ref{sec:morphology}, the \CG{} coefficients are positive in all three contrasts (Table~\ref{tab:morph_robustness}); support is broad except that 83\% of \CG{} values lie within the \RG{} group-luminosity range, limiting that smallest comparison. Because DS18 \(P_{\rm S0}\) separates S0 from E only within \(T\mathrm{Type}\leq0\), the environment dependence of the E/S0 mix is left to a dedicated analysis.

\section{Extended size diagnostics}\label{app:size_details}












The size-availability counts are included in Fig.~\ref{fig:data_availability}; the descriptive mass--size relations and the per-sample sizes predicted at \(\log(M_\star/M_\odot) = 10.3\) are provided in the online supplement. The concentration index \(C = R_{90,r}/R_{50,r}\) provides a model-independent structural proxy: the adjusted satellite coefficient is \(0.068\) (\(p = 0.124\)), and the matched concentration difference is \(-0.008\) (\(p = 0.904\)), offering no evidence for a fixed-mass concentration shift.


The crowding checks mirror Appendix~\ref{app:robustness_details}: excluding galaxies with a projected neighbour within 55 arcsec leaves a satellite coefficient of \(-0.013\) dex (\(p = 0.463\)), and adding the close-neighbour flag as a covariate gives \(-0.011\) dex (\(p = 0.479\)).



The per-galaxy difference between the two measures, \(d = \log R_{\rm chl,r} - \log R_{50,r}\), averages \(0.054\) dex, and is larger for elliptical galaxies (\(0.074\) dex) than for spiral galaxies (\(0.042\) dex), as expected if the Petrosian aperture preferentially truncates concentrated profiles. Its rank correlation with the nearest-neighbour separation is \(\rho_\mathrm{S} = 0.012\) (\(p = 0.524\)), so residual blending does not drive a strong measure-dependent size systematic.



Adding the pairwise projected tidal index of Sect.~\ref{sec:tidal_index} to the all-galaxy size model changes the \CG{} coefficient from \(-0.012\) dex to \(0.005\) dex on the pairwise complete-case sample; the local projected-density proxy attenuates the size coefficient in the same way it attenuates the morphology coefficient.



The radial diagnostic is exploratory: mass- and redshift-adjusted satellite size residuals correlate with projected BGG-centric distance with \(\rho_\mathrm{S} = 0.17\) (\(p = 0.026\)) in \CG{} and \(\rho_\mathrm{S} = 0.18\) (\(p<10^{-4}\)) in the pooled controls. A more negative inner-region residual would be the tidal-truncation expectation; the observed trends are similar in \CG{} and the controls, so the data do not isolate a \CG{}-specific inner-region size suppression.





Within morphology classes, the satellite-only fits give \(0.036\) dex for elliptical satellites (\(p = 0.068\)) and \(-0.005\) dex for spiral satellites (\(p = 0.831\)), and the \CG{}\(\times\)morphology interaction is \(0.032\) dex (\(p = 0.348\)). These strata are secondary diagnostics of whether size carries information beyond the morphology shift.
