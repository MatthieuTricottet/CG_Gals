  \abstract
  {Compact groups combine the highest projected galaxy densities with low velocity dispersions, yet most of them lie inside larger groups. Whether their galaxies are special therefore depends on how they compare with the members of ordinary groups, not only with the field.}
  {We test whether the galaxies of compact groups of four members (\CG) differ from those of regular groups of four members (\RG), from the four brightest members of regular groups (\CB), and from the projected cores formed by the brightest group galaxy and its three nearest projected companions (\CC).}
  {For 62 non-split \CG{} systems we combine Galaxy Zoo~1 morphologies, stellar masses, specific star-formation rates, emission-line diagnostics, spectral indices, and seeing-corrected half-light radii. Each control is compared separately with covariate-adjusted models, galaxy- and group-level matching, and comparisons within shared host groups.}
  {The clearest difference is morphological. At fixed stellar mass, redshift, rank, and group properties, \CG{} galaxies are more often in the Galaxy Zoo~1 E class, especially their satellites, whose odds ratios are \(2.47\), \(1.86\), and \(2.80\) against \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{}. Quartets matched on our initial criteria confirm the excess against \CC{} (\(+14.9\) percentage points, \(p = 0.019\)). At comparable mean pair separation, however, the \CG{}--\CC{} difference is \(+3.4\) points (95\% CI \([-7.7, +14.4]\)) in their common range and \(-0.4\) points (\([-12.0, +11.3]\)) for compactness-matched quartets. Within shared hosts, the \CG{} coefficient depends on the radial adjustment. Morphology accounts for part of the satellite-quenching contrast. Other star-formation, colour, AGN-like, magnitude-gap, and size diagnostics show no comparably robust offsets.}
  {Compact-group satellites are more often in the Galaxy Zoo~1 E class than those of our control samples, including the full projected-core sample. At comparable compactness, the estimated difference is small, although the data still allow an appreciable residual offset. Quenching at fixed morphology shows no clear compact-group-specific signal.}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Given their high apparent densities and low velocity dispersions \citep{Hickson82,Hickson+92}, compact groups appear to be ideal sites for the frequent tidal encounters and mergers that alter galaxy masses, luminosities, sizes, morphologies, and star-formation rates (e.g., \citealt{Mamon92}). \citet{MendesdeOliveira&Hickson94} provided the classic report of an excess of early-type galaxies in compact groups relative to the field (building on the photometric morphologies of \citealt{HKA89} and the merger-remnant expectations of \citealt{ZepfWhitmore93}), in line with the broader morphology--density relation \citep{Dressler80}. Compact-group versus loose-group and field comparisons have since found broad differences in morphology and other galaxy properties, including the analysis of \citet{Coenda+12}; multi-wavelength studies likewise report altered infrared, UV, star-formation, and stellar-population properties \citep{Johnson+07,Tzanavaris+10,Bitsakis+11}, and H\,{\sc i} deficiency has been organised into an evolutionary sequence \citep{VerdesMontenegro+01}.

The interpretation of such contrasts is nevertheless ambiguous. Projected selection can include chance alignments, and many Hickson-like systems need not be isolated bound entities: apparent compact groups may be transient alignments, dense substructures, or cores embedded in looser groups and richer haloes \citep{Mamon86,DiazGimenez&Mamon10,DiazGimenez+20,Zheng&Shen21,Taverna+23}. The relevant observational question is therefore not only whether compact-group galaxies differ from field galaxies, but whether they remain different from galaxies occupying regular-group analogues selected with comparable luminosity, redshift, and membership constraints. Recent structural studies find evidence for transformation in compact groups, especially non-isolated systems \citep{Montaguth2023,Montaguth2025,Montaguth2026MassSize,Montaguth2026NonIsolated}; \citet{Montaguth2026NonIsolated} compared non-isolated compact-group galaxies with surrounding host populations, but not with ordinary-group quartets reproducing compact-group membership, luminosity-ranking, or projected-core selection.

In our previous work \citep{Tricottet+25}, we found that, once the direct consequences of the compact-group selection are accounted for, compact groups of four galaxies have group-scale properties broadly similar to those of regular groups of four galaxies and to the eligible cores of regular groups. Here we ask how the galaxy-population contrast changes across three ordinary-group comparison samples: genuine four-member groups (\RG{}), the four brightest eligible members of richer groups (\CB{}), and BGG-centred projected cores (\CC{}), the closest analogue of a compact configuration. The incremental contribution is the joint use of these explicit comparator definitions, BGG--satellite separation, adjusted regressions, individual and quartet matching, a comparison with projected cores of comparable compactness, and shared-host models with host-centric radius. This design tests how the interpretation changes with the comparator and with location inside the host; it does not propose a new evolutionary mechanism. Section~\ref{sec:data} describes the samples, the classifications, and the statistical approach; Sect.~\ref{sec:results} presents the results; Sect.~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses their physical interpretation; and Sect.~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Data and classifications}\label{sec:data}
\subsection{Samples}\label{sec:samples}
We use the compact-group and control samples constructed in our previous study \citep{Tricottet+25}. Galaxy properties are taken from the SDSS DR12 catalogue of \citet{Tempel+17}, while regular groups are drawn from the SDSS DR7 group catalogue of \citet{Lim+17}; the two catalogues were matched through SDSS object identifiers to define the Lim--Tempel working catalogue. Compact groups are selected from the modified Hickson compact-group catalogue of \citet{DiazGimenez+18}: reliable HMCGs with exactly four members, all present in the Lim--Tempel catalogue, satisfying the volume and BGG-luminosity limits $0.005 \le z_{\rm BGG} \le 0.0452$ and $M_{r,\rm BGG}\leq -21.81$. The original HMCG membership uses the Hickson three-magnitude concordance in apparent $r$ magnitude, whereas the Lim-based control eligibility below uses the catalogue absolute magnitude; these conventions are not identical (retained CGs 173, 229, and 354 span 3.070, 3.063, and 3.182 mag in absolute $r$). In contrast to our previous study, we remove CGs classified as \emph{split} following \citet{Zheng&Shen21}, which reduces the compact-group sample from 78 to 62 groups. Following \citet{Zheng&Shen21}, the remaining systems are \emph{isolated} when the four members constitute their whole Lim--Tempel group, \emph{predominant} when they contribute at least half of the host-group $r$-band luminosity, and \emph{embedded} otherwise (\nGrIso, \nGrPre, and \nGrEmb{} systems).
\footnote{The embedded and predominant assignments were interchanged in parts of \citet{Tricottet+25}, including Sect.~3.3. We correct the inherited catalogue labels to the definitions adopted here; isolated systems are unaffected.}

The three control samples are built from Lim--Tempel groups satisfying the same redshift and BGG-luminosity limits, with at least four members and at least three satellites within 3 absolute magnitudes of the BGG, and serve different purposes (Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic}). \RG{} contains regular groups with exactly four members and is the most direct equal-membership comparison. \CB{} contains the four brightest eligible members of each regular group and tests whether the \CG{} population resembles the luminous core of an ordinary group. \CC{} contains the BGG and its three closest projected companions among members within 3 magnitudes of the BGG, reconstructed here from the eligible parent groups by applying the magnitude criterion before ranking companions by projected distance; it is the most compact-core-like analogue, but not a compactness-matched sample: the \CG{}--\CC{} overlap coefficient in \(-\log \Rmed\) is 0.34, and 18\% of \CG{} groups lie above the \CC{} 95th percentile in compactness; Sect.~\ref{sec:morphology} therefore also compares the two samples at comparable compactness. A candidate control quartet is excluded when it contains a \CG{} galaxy, so no galaxy appears on both sides of a comparison; this does not exclude every other quartet drawn from a host that contains a different \CG{} member. Among the 56 non-isolated compact groups, 51 overlap their would-be host core and 46 are exact four-member identities (Appendix~\ref{app:cg_core_overlap}). We also remove the systems associated with Lim group 3688, whose single outlying-redshift member ($z=0.048$, against $0.032$--$0.034$ for the other six) enters both the \CB{} and \CC{} quartets and inflates their velocity dispersions to $\simeq 2000 \kms$. Table~\ref{tab:samples} summarises the final samples; the \CB{} and \CC{} group counts differ because the luminosity-ranked and distance-ranked definitions retain different Lim groups after the compact-group exclusion.

We use SDSS DR16 \citep{Ahumada+20} to retrieve stellar masses, star formation rates \citep[SFRs;][]{Brinchmann+04,Kauffmann+03b}, emission-line measurements, and Galaxy Zoo morphologies \citep{Lintott+08,GalaxyZoo1}; Appendix~\ref{app:sdss_query} lists the tables and joins. Throughout, `SDSS non-AGN reference' denotes the AGN-filtered reference sample used for calibration and orientation: spectroscopic galaxies with \(0.005 \le z \le 0.0452\), extinction-corrected Petrosian \(r \le 17.77\), a valid MPA-JHU stellar mass, and finite Galaxy Zoo vote fractions after the AGN filter. It is a field-dominated reference population, not a control sample. Projected physical separations are derived from angular-diameter distances in the adopted Planck 2015 cosmology \citep{Planck15}, as in Paper~I; all distances quoted below are in kpc.

\subsection{Classifications}\label{sec:ssfr_classification}
Star-formation classes are based on SDSS total specific star-formation rates. The inherited DR12 group catalogues store \(\log\mathrm{sSFR}=\texttt{sfr\_tot\_p50}-\texttt{lgm\_tot\_p50}\). In the MPA-JHU catalogues, \(-9999\) is the missing-value sentinel of the \texttt{galSpecExtra} table \citep{Stoughton02,Brinchmann+04,Kauffmann+03b}, so rows with unavailable components are treated as \emph{missing data}: they are excluded from the classification and from every sSFR-based figure and fraction, and are reported as counts only (Table~\ref{tab:sSFR_status}). All quenched and star-forming fractions are computed among classified galaxies. The missing-sSFR incidence is higher in \CG{} (7\% of members, 10\% of BGGs) than in the reference sample (1\%). Of the 394 missing rows, 332 are BOSS-instrument spectra outside the legacy-only MPA-JHU coverage, 60 are legacy spectra whose SFR fit returned the sentinel, and 2 have no spectrum identifier (Appendix~\ref{app:sdss_query}). These provenance categories relate the missingness to catalogue and spectroscopic availability; they do not establish that availability is physically independent of star formation. Galaxies with a measured sSFR are split into quenched and star-forming classes with a two-component Gaussian mixture model (GMM) in the \(\log M_\star\)--\(\log\mathrm{sSFR}\) plane; the boundary and the AGN handling are given in Appendix~\ref{app:ssfr_method} (Fig.~\ref{fig:sSFR_classification}).

Morphologies are drawn from Galaxy Zoo 1 \citep{Lintott+08,GalaxyZoo1}, whose debiased catalogue vote fractions \citep{Bamford+09} are \texttt{p\_el\_debiased}, labelled ``elliptical'' in GZ1, and \texttt{p\_cs\_debiased}, labelled combined spiral. We define the GZ1 E class by \(\texttt{p\_el\_debiased}>0.5\), the S class by \(\texttt{p\_cs\_debiased}>0.5\), and the uncertain class when both vote fractions are finite but neither threshold is exceeded; rows with a missing vote fraction are treated as missing Galaxy Zoo data. The DR16 \texttt{zooSpec} join returns no votes for part of the sample. Because the GZ1 catalogue is keyed to DR7 objects, we recover these votes through the SkyServer DR7 cross-match, accepting a counterpart only when its DR7 identifier is not shared by several DR16 objects and the archived position agrees within 1.5 arcsec. This adds votes for 17 \CG{} galaxies (6 E, 10 S, and 1 uncertain) and for 118, 116, and 13 galaxies in \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{}; wherever both sources classify a galaxy, the classes agree. Three cases therefore remain distinct: an uncertain class has finite votes below both thresholds; a missing classification has votes in neither source (14 \CG{} galaxies, and 186, 205, and 7 in the controls); and 11, 9, and 3 control galaxies (none in \CG{}) keep their DR16 class without a verified archival counterpart. GZ1 does not separate E from S0, so throughout this paper the E class is interpreted as a smooth early-type-like class, not as a physical sample of classical ellipticals. A cross-match with the deep-learning morphologies of \citet{DominguezSanchez2018} (DS18; 2436 of 3857 unique galaxies) quantifies this: of the 990 matched E-class galaxies, 92\% have DS18 \(T\mathrm{Type}\leq0\), 54\% are early with \(P_{\rm S0}>0.5\), and 28\% with \(P_{\rm S0}\geq0.8\); conversely, DS18 early types with \(P_{\rm S0}>0.5\) (\(N = 730\)) fall 73\% in our E class and 20\% in S (66\% and 27\% for \(P_{\rm S0}\geq0.8\)), with the E share dropping from 81\% for face-on to 48\% for inclined systems (DS18 \(P_{\rm edge\text{-}on}<0.1\) versus \(\geq0.5\)), as expected if inclined S0s expose their discs. We reserve ``elliptical'' for the literal GZ1 category and for external classifications that physically distinguish ellipticals. We also retrieve the MPA-JHU age-sensitive indices \(D_n4000\) and H\(\delta_A\) \citep{Kauffmann+03b} for the stored spectra of the group galaxies, for descriptive use only (Sect.~\ref{sec:other_properties}, Appendix~\ref{app:selection}).





\subsection{Galaxy sizes}\label{sec:size_data}

Our primary size measure is the circular effective (half-light) radius of the pure S\'ersic fit in the $r$ band, \(R_{e,r}\), from the structural catalogue of \citet{Simard2011}: PSF-convolved GIM2D fits with re-derived sky levels and deblending designed for crowded fields, so the sizes are corrected for seeing. The catalogue is keyed to SDSS DR7 identifiers; we bridge our DR16 objects to their DR7 counterparts through the SkyServer \texttt{SpecDR7} cross-match table. Rows whose Simard redshift differs from the catalogue redshift by more than 0.005 are treated as wrong matches and dropped (9 rows). The Simard sizes, tabulated in kpc under the catalogue's own cosmology, are converted back to angular sizes with the catalogue plate scale and then to kpc with the same Planck 2015 conversion as every other projected quantity.

As a secondary, model-independent measure we use the SDSS Petrosian radii \texttt{petroR50\_r} and \texttt{petroR90\_r}, which also define the concentration index \(C = R_{90,r}/R_{50,r}\) and provide the like-for-like bridge to \citet{Coenda+12}. Petrosian radii are not corrected for seeing, and the Petrosian aperture misses roughly 20\% of the flux of an \(n=4\) profile, which biases \(R_{50,r}\) low for concentrated galaxies \citep{Blanton2001,Graham2005}. All sizes are required to lie between 0.1 and 50 kpc after re-conversion; S\'ersic fits pegged at the GIM2D bounds (\(n_g \le 0.55\) or \(n_g \ge 7.9\); 60 galaxies) are excluded from the primary sample, a cut that removes 2.5\% of the Simard-matched \CG{} members against 1.0--1.5\% in the controls and is therefore conservative with respect to a \CG{} early-type excess; Petrosian entries must satisfy \(R_{50,r}>0\), \(R_{90,r}>R_{50,r}\), and a finite positive uncertainty. The completeness of both measures is given in Appendix~\ref{app:selection}.


\subsection{Statistical approach}\label{sec:stats}

Our main question is whether compact-group galaxies retain distinct properties once differences in stellar mass, redshift, galaxy rank, and group properties are taken into account. The three controls address physically different comparisons, so we compare \CG{} with each one separately. Galaxy-level logistic models quantify the adjusted E-versus-S and quenched-versus-star-forming contrasts; group-level matching compares the satellite mix of whole quartets; and within-host models compare compact-group members with their non-compact co-members. The covariates are stellar mass, redshift, rank, group luminosity, and the quartet velocity dispersion \(\sigma_v\); because \(\sigma_v\) from four tracers is noisy, every analysis that uses it is repeated without it (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness_details}).

The independence unit is the \emph{physical} Lim group: standard errors are clustered by physical group and resampling is blocked by physical group or matched pair, because the four galaxies of one group are not independent. We report two-sided p-values with effect sizes and confidence intervals for the primary morphology, star-formation, matching, host, and size analyses, and reserve multiplicity control for the exploratory batteries. The p-value conventions, the covariate-completeness rule, the overlap coefficient, and the treatment of the overlapping control samples in pooled summaries are collected in Appendix~\ref{app:stat_conventions}.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Morphology}\label{sec:morphology}

\CG{} galaxies are more often in the GZ1 E class than the galaxies of every control. Tables~\ref{tab:sSFR_status} and \ref{tab:morphologies} give the raw class counts; among galaxies with a usable E or S classification, the \CG{} E-class fraction is 58\% against 47\%, 48\%, and 33\% in \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{} (Fisher exact tests: \(p = 2.2\times 10^{-3}\), \(p = 4.3\times 10^{-3}\), and \(p<10^{-4}\)). The 22 \CG{} rows with uncertain or missing Galaxy Zoo classifications are excluded rather than assigned to either class. Satellites account for most of the difference: among usable BGG classifications the E-class fraction is 66.1\% in \CG{} and 46.9\% in \RG{} (Fisher \(p\) = 0.88, 0.88, and 0.07 against \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{}), and restricted to star-forming galaxies the E/S mix is compatible with every control (two-sided Fisher exact test: \(p = 0.372\), \(p = 0.607\), \(p = 0.374\)), so star-forming E-class galaxies do not produce the raw excess.

Individually matched galaxies confirm the shift. Matching 234 of the 248 \CG{} galaxies one-to-one to ordinary-group galaxies of the deduplicated control pool on stellar mass, redshift, rank, group luminosity, and \(\sigma_v\) (exact rank strata, common support, 0.2-SD caliper; maximum absolute standardized mean difference 0.08 after matching, Appendix~\ref{app:pooled_models}), the matched E-class fraction difference over the 207 pairs with a decisive class on both sides is \(0.14\) (95\% block-bootstrap interval \([0.04, 0.23]\); \(p = 0.006\)), rising to \(0.18\) for the 152 such satellite pairs (\(p = 0.003\)).







The projected cores \CC{} are the closest analogue of a compact configuration, but they are less compact than \CG{} (Table~\ref{tab:samples}). To compare quartets of similar compactness, we measure it here by the arithmetic mean of the six projected pair separations of each quartet, \(\Rmean\), in physical kpc; Table~\ref{tab:samples} uses their median, \(\Rmed\). The median \(\Rmean\) is 143~kpc for \CG{} and 296~kpc for \CC{}. Three \CG{}-minus-\CC{} differences in the E-class fraction of E/S-classified satellites must be distinguished (Fig.~\ref{fig:compactness}). In the full sample, with the satellite covariates of Table~\ref{tab:adjusted_models} and standardised to the \CG{} satellites, the difference is \(+14.4\) points (95\% CI \([+7.3, +21.5]\)). Within the range common to the central 90\% of both \(\Rmean\) distributions, 95--216~kpc (48 \CG{} and 187 \CC{} quartets), a model with separate \(\log\Rmean\) slopes for the two samples gives \(+3.4\) points (\([-7.7, +14.4]\)) at \(\Rmean = 175\)~kpc (+4.5 points with a common slope). Matching quartets one-to-one within that range, within 0.08~dex in \(\Rmean\) and within calipers in redshift, mean satellite stellar mass, and BGG luminosity, gives \(-0.4\) points (\([-12.0, +11.3]\)) over 40 pairs of quartets with classified satellites. The quartet match of Table~\ref{tab:group_match_per_control} follows the initial criteria of this paper, which do not include compactness, and gives +14.9 points against \CC{}; the second match imposes proximity in compactness. At comparable compactness the central estimates are therefore close to zero, but their intervals still allow differences of about 10 points in either direction. The restriction to the common range also changes the compared population: it excludes the most compact \CG{} quartets and most \CC{} quartets (Appendix~\ref{app:cg_core_overlap}).

Within their own host groups, the \CG{} membership coefficient is sensitive to how radial position is adjusted. For the \nGrEmb{} embedded and \nGrPre{} predominant systems, whose quartets live inside richer Lim groups (54 distinct hosts; 958 member galaxies, 224 of them in a \CG{}), we compare the \CG{} members with the non-\CG{} members of the same host by conditional logistic regression stratified by host, which removes every host-level property by design, with stellar mass, host luminosity rank, and the projected radius from the Lim BGG of the host as covariates. The E-class odds ratio for \CG{} membership is \(1.46\) (95\% CI \([0.92, 2.33]\); \(p = 0.112\); 803 galaxies in 54 informative strata), and rises to \(1.89\) (\([1.22, 2.93]\); \(p = 0.004\)) when the radius is omitted. In 46 of the 56 non-isolated systems the compact group is exactly the Lim BGG and its three nearest eligible projected companions (Sect.~\ref{sec:samples}), so its satellites and the eligible non-members of the same host occupy separate radial ranges; after the stellar-mass and E/S requirements, only 6 hosts offer radial overlap between member and non-member satellites. The change of coefficient therefore measures the sensitivity of the within-host contrast to the radial adjustment, which bridges radii that same-host comparisons rarely sample; it is neither a causal mediation fraction nor proof of environmental equivalence. A fixed-effects GLM gives the same direction (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness_details}).


\subsection{Star formation at fixed morphology}\label{sec:star_formation}

The star-formation contrast is weaker and control-dependent (Table~\ref{tab:adjusted_models}). It is clearest against \RG{} in the raw data, but the adjusted and matched results do not support a control-independent excess of quenching; assigning all missing-sSFR galaxies to either extreme class does not erase the raw \RG{} contrast (Appendix~\ref{app:ssfr_method}).





Morphology accounts for part of the satellite-quenching contrast, and no clear association remains once it is fixed. In direct satellite-only models that add the binary GZ1 E/S indicator to stellar mass, redshift, group luminosity, and velocity dispersion, the \CG{} odds ratios are \(1.56\) (95\% CI \([0.99, 2.48]\); \(p = 0.057\)), \(1.12\) (\([0.74, 1.70]\); \(p = 0.591\)), and \(1.14\) (\([0.55, 2.36]\); \(p = 0.731\)) against \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{}. The largest estimate, against \CB{}, has a 95\% interval that includes unity, and the other two are close to unity; these estimates do not identify a causal quenching mechanism. On the exact \CB{} morphology-complete sample, omitting morphology raises the odds ratio from \(1.56\) to \(2.31\), so the attenuation is not caused by changing the fitted population.

The descriptive conditional fractions are \(P(\mathrm{Q}\,|\,\mathrm{E}) = \qEcg\), \qEcb, \qEcc, and \qErg{} for \CG{}, \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{} (Table~\ref{tab:quenched_by_morphology}). Within the common morphology-available and sSFR-classified population used by the Kitagawa decomposition, each total \CG{}-minus-control difference is split exactly into a morphology-composition term and a conditional-within-morphology term (Appendix~\ref{app:ssfr_method}, Table~\ref{tab:quenching_decomposition}). The host-blocked 95\% intervals for the conditional terms are \([\condCBlo, \condCBhi]\), \([\condCClo, \condCChi]\), and \([\condRGlo, \condRGhi]\); these compatible ranges do not demonstrate equality. The individually matched quenched-fraction difference is \(0.03\) (95\% CI \([-0.06, 0.12]\); \(p = 0.526\)). With the existing fixed-threshold diagnostic, $\log_{10}(\mathrm{sSFR}/\mathrm{yr}^{-1})=-11.0$, the \CG{} and \RG{} satellite star-forming fractions are 41.4\% and 60.2\% (difference \(-18.8\) points; \(p = 5.3\times 10^{-4}\)).







No robust \CG{} displacement of the star-forming main sequence is established: the unmatched median offsets are comparison-dependent, while the matched star-forming pairs give a small mean difference whose interval includes zero. The sequence fits are described in Appendix~\ref{app:baseline_details}; the cumulative distributions and full numerical comparison are retained in the online supplement. The quenched-sequence residuals are also shown there, but are weakly informative because MPA-JHU sSFRs of quenched galaxies are largely \(D_n4000\)-calibrated.


\subsection{Compact-group classes}\label{sec:cg_classes}

The early-type-like excess is concentrated among the compact groups that live inside richer hosts. Figure~\ref{fig:cg4_classes} compares the satellite E-class fraction in two broad stellar-mass bins; the aggregate fractions \(f_{\rm E} = N_{\rm E}/(N_{\rm E}+N_{\rm S})\) for the three Zheng--Shen classes of Sect.~\ref{sec:samples}: \(f_{\rm E} = \feSatEmb\) for the \nGrEmb{} embedded and \(\feSatPre\) for the \nGrPre{} predominant systems, against \(\feSatIso\) for the \nGrIso{} isolated ones (aggregate Wilson 95\% intervals). The isolated compact groups are compatible with the loose \RG{} groups (group-level permutation test on the per-group mean satellite \(f_{\rm E}\), \(p = \pIsoRG\)) and sit below the rest of \CG{} (\(p = \pIsoRest\)), whereas the two non-isolated classes do not differ detectably (predominant-versus-embedded odds ratio at fixed stellar mass \(0.99\), \(p = 0.97\)). The host halo masses differ strongly between the classes (median \(\log M_{200c} = \lMIso\), \lMEmb, and \lMPre{} for isolated, embedded, and predominant systems), yet adding \(\log M_{200c}\) to the class model changes neither the class coefficients nor the conclusion (\(\log M_{200c}\) odds ratio \(0.85\) per standard deviation, \(p = 0.43\)). With only \nGrIso{} isolated groups, this class contrast is exploratory; other group-scale descriptors are examined in Appendix~\ref{app:env_diagnostics}.

\subsection{Local projected density}\label{sec:tidal_index}

Including local projected density attenuates the \CG{} coefficient, but the amount is not well determined. For each of the 6076 catalogue rows we compute \(T_i = \sum_j M_{\star,j}/R_{ij}^3\) over selected quartet co-members (\(R_{ij}\) in kpc; unmeasured companion masses contribute zero, making \(T_i\) a lower bound). For the pooled sensitivity fit, controls are deduplicated by objid as in Appendix~\ref{app:pooled_models}, retaining the quartet assignment in the priority order \RG{}, \CB{}, \CC{}. The E-class model adjusts for stellar mass and satellite status, with physical-group clustering. With the index, the \CG{} odds ratio is 1.19 (95\% CI \([0.89, 1.60]\); \(p = 0.24\); 3416 galaxies). Retaining all catalogue rows instead gives an attenuation from 1.83 to 1.30 (\(p = 0.083\)), with an index odds ratio of 1.43 per \(\sdlogT\)-dex standard deviation (\(p<10^{-4}\)); this check weights multiply selected controls more heavily. On that catalogue-row frame, the \(\log T_i\) overlap coefficient is 0.54 and the median gap 1.1 dex; restricting to common support gives OR 1.14 (\(p = 0.38\)). Conditioning on an index built from the separations defining compact groups partly conditions on selection, so attenuation does not establish mediation. The index traces projected neighbours, not tidal history; the host-inclusive check is given in Appendix~\ref{app:selection}.


\subsection{Other galaxy properties}\label{sec:other_properties}





None of the other galaxy properties shows an offset as robust as the morphology excess (Fig.~\ref{fig:property_trends}). Strong \(\mathrm{H}\alpha\) emission (\(\mathrm{H}\alpha\,\mathrm{EW} \leq -3\,\text{\AA}\), emission negative) is rarer in \CG{}: 70 of 217 members with measurements (32\%), against 41\%, 43\%, and 57\% in \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{} (Fisher \(p = 0.012\), \(p = 0.002\), \(p<10^{-4}\)). The deficit is not fully explained by the different morphology mix: after adjustment for stellar mass and GZ1 E/S class, the \CG{} odds ratios for strong \(\mathrm{H}\alpha\) emission are 0.55, 0.65, and 0.56 against \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{}, respectively (\(p = 0.002\), \(p = 0.027\), and \(p = 0.072\)), with the first two contrasts statistically clear. These equivalent widths are measured in the 3-arcsec SDSS fibre, whereas the quenched class of Sect.~\ref{sec:star_formation} uses the total sSFR, so a fibre deficit of strong emission is not by itself evidence of more quenching. The comparison at comparable compactness of Sect.~\ref{sec:morphology} tests the deficit more directly. Measured on the exact stored spectra, with a common compactness slope and at \(\Rmean = 175\)~kpc, the strong-\(\mathrm{H}\alpha\) difference from \CC{} is \(-6.3\) points (95\% CI \([-15.4, +2.8]\)) for all satellites. In the bin \(10 \le \log(M_\star/\msun) < 10.5\), the same contrast is \(-16.0\) points (\([-30.6, -1.5]\)) (62 \CG{} and 196 \CC{} satellites), whereas the quenched-fraction difference in the same bin is \(+3.9\) points (\([-11.8, +19.6]\)). On the subsample with a GZ1 E/S class and an aperture covariate, the logarithm of the ratio of the 1.5-arcsec fibre radius to the Petrosian half-light radius, adding that covariate to a model with the E/S class keeps the deficit (from \(-15.4\) to \(-18.2\) points; 55 and 172 satellites), whereas also excluding BPT AGN and composites gives \(-9.5\) points (\([-26.5, +7.4]\)) for 29 and 94 satellites. Because this selection changes both the estimate and the sample, we regard the intermediate-mass deficit as an interesting hint that is sensitive to the spectroscopic selection; the variants are listed in the online supplement. The AGN-like BPT line-ratio fraction (above the \citealt{Kewley+01} line, or flagged as AGN without line ratios; composites between the \citealt{Kauffmann+03a} and \citealt{Kewley+01} lines) is 38\% in \CG{}, and its mass- and environment-adjusted \CG{} odds ratio is 1.17 (\(p = 0.364\)); this diagnostic does not measure all nuclear activity. The \(D_n4000\) index follows stellar mass in every sample; the \CG{} medians sit above the controls only in the \(10.0\)--\(10.5\) bin (\(D_n4000 = 1.77\) against 1.59, 1.68, and 1.56), where the \CG{} E-class fraction also peaks relative to the controls, and coincide with them elsewhere; a post-starburst classification from \(D_n4000\) and H\(\delta_A\) is deferred to a follow-up paper.

No clear adjusted size offset is detected between \CG{} and ordinary-group galaxies. \subsection{Robustness}\label{sec:robustness}

The morphology excess does not depend on the vote threshold, on the classifier, or on the crowded closest pairs (Table~\ref{tab:morph_robustness}). Excluding every galaxy whose nearest catalogued neighbour lies within 55 arcsec, the SDSS fibre-collision scale, removes 19\% of \CG{} members against 6--10\% of the controls and moves the adjusted E-class odds ratios from 1.99 to 2.00 (\CB{}), 1.60 to 1.63 (\CC{}), and 2.52 to 2.44 (\RG{}). Across classification thresholds of 0.4--0.8 the pooled adjusted odds ratio stays between 1.65 and 1.93, the continuous debiased E vote fraction \(p_E\) itself is offset by \(0.076\) (\(p = 3.6\times 10^{-4}\)), and the Galaxy-Zoo-independent S\'ersic index (\(n_g > 2.5\); 75\% coverage) gives an odds ratio of 1.60 (\(p = 0.002\)). With the DS18 \(T\mathrm{Type}\leq0\) classification as the outcome, the per-control \CG{} odds ratios are 2.11, 1.49, and 2.22 (all \(p \leq 0.017\); \(n = 1822\), 1913, 324). The crowding, threshold, and \(\sigma_v\) reruns are detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:robustness_details}.

\section{Interpretation and comparison with previous work}\label{sec:discussion}




\subsection{An early-type-like satellite excess linked to dense group cores}\label{sec:morph_local_density}

Compact groups of four galaxies host more early-type-like satellites than any of the three ordinary-group comparison samples. The three adjusted contrasts are ordered in the same sense as the characteristic compactness of the controls, with the smallest contrast against the projected cores \CC{}; this is a descriptive ordering, not a formal between-control trend, and the matched-quartet contrasts do not follow it (Sect.~\ref{sec:morphology}). Within shared hosts, the \CG{} membership coefficient decreases when the radius from the Lim BGG is adjusted for, but few hosts contain members and non-members at the same radius, so this change is a sensitivity to the radial adjustment rather than a measured contrast at fixed radius. \citet{Montaguth2026NonIsolated} already examined quenched and early-type fractions with host-centric position and local density around non-isolated compact groups. Our narrower addition combines conditional shared-host modelling and individual covariates with the explicit quartet-control analyses; differences in samples and morphology definitions make the results complementary. Restricting the individually matched comparison to satellites raises the E-class fraction difference to \(0.18\). Present-day stellar mass may itself encode part of a galaxy's environmental history, so fixed-mass estimates describe the remaining association rather than the total accumulated transformation.

The BGG--satellite separation matters when comparing with \citet{Martinez+13}, whose brightest-galaxy selections involved group luminosity, morphology or concentration, and magnitude gap. Our lack of a consistent adjusted BGG excess need not conflict with their result given the different samples, morphology measures, and adjustments. The luminous-BGG selection concentrates our samples at high stellar mass, where most BGGs are already quenched (Table~\ref{tab:sSFR_status}), potentially limiting the observable environmental contrast.

The 46 exact host-core identities among 56 non-isolated \CG{} systems (Appendix~\ref{app:cg_core_overlap}), in which the compact group is the Lim BGG and its three nearest eligible projected companions, make \CC{} the natural comparator for a direct test at comparable compactness. At fixed stellar mass, smaller pairwise separations accompany higher satellite E-class probabilities within \CG{}, \CB{}, and \CC{} separately (Table~\ref{tab:battery}, Fig.~\ref{fig:compactness}). When \CG{} and \CC{} quartets of similar mean projected pair separation are compared, the estimated difference in satellite E-class fraction is small (\(+3.4\) points in the common range and \(-0.4\) points for matched quartets, against \(+14.4\) points in the full sample; Sect.~\ref{sec:morphology}). These estimates do not show that the two populations are equivalent or that a single compactness--morphology relation holds for all groups: their intervals still allow appreciable differences in either direction, and the restriction to the common range removes the most compact \CG{} quartets and most \CC{} quartets, so the compared populations are not the full samples. Local projected-density adjustment also attenuates the \CG{} association (Sect.~\ref{sec:tidal_index}), without identifying causal ordering. An association of early-type-like satellites with dense projected group cores accords with the structural signatures in recent S-PLUS studies \citep{Montaguth2023,Montaguth2025,Montaguth2026MassSize,Montaguth2026NonIsolated}, while allowing other host properties, selection effects, and evolutionary pathways to contribute. The \nGrIso{} isolated systems have lower satellite E-class fractions than the rest of \CG{} but cannot establish a distinct evolutionary stage (Sect.~\ref{sec:cg_classes}). Host-mass adjustment leaves the class contrasts unchanged (Appendix~\ref{app:selection}); this does not exclude a host contribution.

Stellar-population diagnostics could in principle separate an accumulated enrichment history from a shorter star-formation timescale, but a metallicity analysis requires its own treatment of selection, aperture coverage, and radial gradients. The SDSS fibres sample central regions whose physical extent varies with redshift, complicating the interpretation of age and metallicity gradients, so that analysis is outside the present scope.

\subsection{GZ1 E-class morphologies and transition galaxies}\label{sec:s0_degeneracy}

Our E class is a smooth early-type-like proxy with a substantial S0 content (Sect.~\ref{sec:ssfr_classification}). Galaxy Zoo~1 cannot determine whether the excess comprises S0s, ellipticals, or both; we reserve that question for a dedicated follow-up analysis of the DS18 classes by environment. This caveat matters when comparing with the S-PLUS studies of compact-group transition galaxies and mass--size trends \citep{Montaguth2023,Montaguth2025,Montaguth2026MassSize,Montaguth2026NonIsolated}: those studies use hybrid structural--colour classifications, identify transition populations, and adopt their own size definitions, whereas we use GZ1 E/S classes and seeing-corrected Simard radii. Their result therefore does not predict a universal size deficit for every \CG{} GZ1 E-class galaxy. Our three control samples instead test which apparent \CG{} signatures survive across genuine four-member, luminosity-selected, and BGG-centred projected-core quartets.

\subsection{Why the star-formation and size signals are weaker}\label{sec:weaker_signals}





Morphology accounts for part of the sSFR-defined quenching contrast; after adjustment for morphology, no control shows a clear residual association (Sect.~\ref{sec:star_formation}). The decomposition's conditional intervals span zero without demonstrating equality or superseding the regressions. Over the full samples, the deficit of strong \(\mathrm{H}\alpha\) emission persists at fixed broad morphology and stellar mass. At comparable compactness it is clear only in the \(10 \le \log(M_\star/\msun) < 10.5\) bin, and there it weakens and becomes uncertain once AGN and composite spectra are excluded (Sect.~\ref{sec:other_properties}); this intermediate-mass deficit is therefore a hint rather than an established compact-group signature, and it is not accompanied by a clear excess of sSFR-defined quenching. The EW measures line emission relative to stellar continuum within the 3-arcsec legacy SDSS fibre and can include non-star-forming excitation from AGN, shocks, or old stars. MPA-JHU total sSFRs instead incorporate photometric aperture corrections and statistical estimates for weak-line systems \citep{Brinchmann+04,Salim+07}. These measurement differences may contribute to the differing contrasts without establishing central quenching. Spectroscopic incompleteness also matters: 19\% of catalogued \CG{} galaxies have a neighbour below the 55-arcsec fibre-collision scale, against 8\% of pooled controls (10\% in \CC{}). Missing companions could hide brief starbursts or post-starburst phases and bias the tidal index downward; aperture corrections may also be less secure for interacting or blended members. The comparison-dependent sSFR and main-sequence estimates therefore remain uncertain.






The size comparison is sensitive to methodology. \citet{Coenda+12} reported a tendency towards smaller compact-group galaxies at fixed luminosity with Petrosian radii within concentration-selected morphological subsamples, although their more restrictive \(C>3\) selection showed no clear environmental trend. Our seeing-corrected Simard radii give no clear fixed-mass offset, with a 95\% interval still permitting a deficit of roughly \(0.04\) dex. The matched Simard and Petrosian differences also use unequal available-pair subsets (Sect.~\ref{sec:other_properties}). The larger Simard-minus-Petrosian offsets for E-class galaxies (Appendix~\ref{app:size_details}) make morphology mix relevant, without measuring its contribution to the environmental contrast. Profile-dependent size estimates offer a possible methodological contribution to differences between studies, but sample selection, luminosity-versus-mass conditioning, and structural classification also differ. Modest intrinsic size differences remain allowed.


\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We have tested whether galaxies in \CG{} compact groups remain special after removing split systems and comparing them with \RG, \CB, and \CC{}. The conclusions apply to low-redshift, luminous, exactly four-member non-split HMCGs present in the required parent catalogues and satisfying the BGG and group-luminosity constraints. Morphology-based results additionally apply only where GZ1 classifications are available, and the sample is not universally stellar-mass complete for satellites. \CC{} is a BGG-centred projected-core comparator rather than a compactness-matched sample, so the full-sample coefficients do not isolate an ``effect of compactness alone.''

The main result is an early-type-like satellite excess. \CG{} galaxies are more often in the GZ1 E class than all three regular-group comparisons after accounting for galaxy and group properties; in the adjusted models the difference is strongest against \CB{} and \RG{} and weakest against the projected cores \CC{}. \CG{} satellites have a higher GZ1 E-class fraction than satellites in the full projected-core control sample. At comparable mean projected pair separation, the estimated difference is small, although the data still allow an appreciable residual offset. BGGs show no consistent adjusted excess across the controls, and within their hosts the \CG{} membership coefficient depends on how the radius from the Lim BGG is adjusted, because members and non-members rarely share the same radius. A projected tidal-density proxy attenuates the \CG{} coefficient, but its entanglement with compact-group selection and imperfect common support preclude a causal interpretation.

Morphology accounts for part of the satellite-quenching contrast. After simultaneous adjustment for morphology and the principal covariates, no control shows a clear residual association, and these estimates do not identify a causal mechanism. Star-forming \CG{} galaxies show no robust main-sequence offset. The secondary deficit of strong \(\mathrm{H}\alpha\) emission in the SDSS fibre persists after adjustment for broad morphology and stellar mass; at comparable compactness it remains only as an intermediate-mass hint that is sensitive to the spectroscopic selection, and it is distinct from quenching defined by the total sSFR. At fixed stellar mass the seeing-corrected satellite size estimate is \(-0.010\) dex with a 95\% interval spanning zero, so no clear adjusted size offset is detected. Colours, AGN-like line ratios, magnitude gaps, \(D_n4000\), and projected phase-space trends identify no simple alternative driver.

We therefore interpret this selected luminous, low-redshift, four-member, non-split catalogue population as shifted towards early-type-like satellite morphologies relative to ordinary groups and their projected cores taken as a whole; whether an offset remains at the same compactness is not resolved by the present sample. Neither the compactness comparison nor the within-host models identify a unique mechanism. The isolated subset is too small to establish a distinct mode. Explicit E--S0--spiral classifications, bulge--disc structure, and stellar-population diagnostics will be needed to distinguish possible pathways.

\section{sSFR classification details}\label{app:ssfr_method}

We identify BPT-classifiable galaxies by requiring measured values of both emission-line ratios
\[
\log_{10}\!\bigl([\mathrm{N\,II}]\lambda6584/\mathrm{H}\alpha\bigr)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\log_{10}\!\bigl([\mathrm{O\,III}]\lambda5007/\mathrm{H}\beta\bigr).
\]
Among these galaxies, we flag AGN-like systems if they satisfy either
\[
\log_{10}\frac{[\mathrm{N\,II}]}{\mathrm{H}\alpha} > 0,
\]
or if they lie above the empirical demarcation of \citet{Kauffmann+03a}:
\[
\log_{10}\frac{[\mathrm{O\,III}]}{\mathrm{H}\beta}
> \frac{0.61}{\log_{10}([\mathrm{N\,II}]/\mathrm{H}\alpha)-0.05} \;+\; 1.3,
\]
in which case they are excluded from the GMM calibration sample. This calibration flag is deliberately more inclusive than the three-class BPT scheme of Sect.~\ref{sec:other_properties}, in which composite galaxies lie between the \citet{Kauffmann+03a} and \citet{Kewley+01} demarcations and only galaxies above the \citet{Kewley+01} line are labelled AGN-like, because its sole purpose is to keep possible AGN contamination out of the sSFR calibration. Galaxies with missing diagnostic line ratios are BPT-unclassified, not proven non-AGN; they are retained for the sSFR status assignment when valid SDSS stellar mass and sSFR estimates are available, while the AGN-like analysis treats them separately.
Reference-sample rows with unavailable \texttt{specsfr\_tot\_p50}, and group-sample rows with unavailable \texttt{sfr\_tot\_p50} or \texttt{lgm\_tot\_p50}, including the \(-9999\) sentinel, have no valid sSFR estimate; they are treated as missing data throughout: excluded from the GMM calibration where applicable, never assigned an sSFR class, omitted from every sSFR figure, and reported as counts only (Sect.~\ref{sec:ssfr_classification}).
Galaxies with a measured sSFR are divided into quenched and star-forming classes with a two-component Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) in the \(\log M_\star\)-\(\log\mathrm{sSFR}\) plane \citep{McLachlanPeel2000}; the boundary is the locus where the two component posterior probabilities are equal, and the resulting classification for the SDSS non-AGN reference is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:sSFR_classification}. A fixed-threshold check confirms that the qualitative hierarchy is not tied to the exact GMM split (Sect.~\ref{sec:star_formation}).

\section{Statistical conventions}\label{app:stat_conventions}

\paragraph{Independence unit.} The unit for every inferential contrast among the group catalogues is the \emph{physical} Lim group: standard errors are clustered by Lim group (unifying the overlapping control definitions, with \CG{} systems assigned to their host Lim group, or to their own identifier when they have no Lim counterpart). The decomposition resamples physical hosts jointly across comparison sides, and matched-quartet inference resamples connected components of the bipartite treated-quartet/control-host graph. Pooled models across the three controls are secondary summaries computed after deduplicating the overlapping control samples to one row per physical galaxy, with the kept label following the priority \RG{} \(>\) \CB{} \(>\) \CC{} (Appendix~\ref{app:pooled_models}). Purely descriptive reference-sample diagnostics report their own unit where they appear.

\paragraph{Tests and p-values.} Descriptive comparisons use two-sided Fisher exact tests for \(2\times2\) tables and rank-sum or bootstrap comparisons for distributions and median differences; Spearman and Pearson coefficients are diagnostics of monotonic or linear trends only. Paired sign-flip permutation p-values use add-one tail counts, \(p = (k+1)/(B+1)\) with \(k\) the number of permutations at least as extreme as the observation; bootstrap sign-crossing p-values are \(2\min[(k_{\le 0}+1)/(B+1),(k_{\ge 0}+1)/(B+1)]\), capped at unity, where \(k_{\le 0}\) and \(k_{\ge 0}\) count resampled differences on either side of zero; displayed p-values are floored at \(10^{-4}\). Adjusted odds ratios come from binomial generalised linear models with cluster-robust covariance; continuous covariates are standardised, so coefficients of continuous terms are per standard deviation of the fitted frame. Multiplicity control is reserved for exploratory screens: Holm adjustment for the colour, measurement-availability, and magnitude-gap screens, and Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate control for the dominance, morphology--environment, and second-order screens.


\paragraph{Covariates.} The velocity dispersion \(\sigma_v\) that enters models and matching is the quartet-level gapper estimate \citep{Wainer&Thissen76} on the four selected members' redshifts, converted as \(c\,\sigma_z/(1+\bar z)\), for \emph{all} samples, including \CB{} and \CC{} quartets embedded in richer hosts (this is how the spurious Lim 3688 quartets of Sect.~\ref{sec:samples} reach \({\simeq}2000\kms\)). With four tracers the estimator is intrinsically noisy: in a Gaussian Monte Carlo of \(10^5\) quartets, \(\sigma_{\rm gapper}/\sigma_{\rm true}\) has median 0.96 with a 16--84\% range of 0.57--1.42. A candidate covariate enters an adjusted model when it is non-missing for at least 65\% of that model's frame (matching variables require 70\%); in practice \(\sigma_v\), group luminosity, stellar mass, redshift, and rank are complete for every quartet and always enter, and no harmonised group-mass covariate is available (Appendix~\ref{app:selection}).


\paragraph{Overlap coefficient.} Where two distributions are compared for common support (compactness in Sect.~\ref{sec:samples}, \(\log T_i\) in Sect.~\ref{sec:tidal_index}), the overlap coefficient is the integral of the minimum of the two normalised histograms on common bins, between 0 (disjoint) and 1 (identical).

\section{Main-sequence and quenched-sequence details}\label{app:baseline_details}

We model the star-forming main sequence as a function of stellar mass with polynomials of order 1--4 fitted to the star-forming galaxies of the SDSS non-AGN reference sample; the diagnostic is the residual RMS scatter, not a formal reduced \(\chi^2\), because no per-galaxy uncertainty model is used. We adopt order 2, the lowest order with the smallest five-fold cross-validated RMS (0.305 dex, against 0.306 dex for order 1), and compute the residual of each star-forming galaxy with respect to it; a positive residual means a higher sSFR at fixed stellar mass than the reference relation. In Sect.~\ref{sec:star_formation}, \(\Delta\) is the \CG{}-minus-control median residual with the central 68\% group-bootstrap interval on the median difference and the two-sided sign-crossing probability (10\,000 resamples). The quenched sequence shown in the online supplement is obtained with the same procedure on the 7705 quenched galaxies of the reference sample: order 4 has the smallest cross-validated RMS (0.331 dex), and the residuals are attached to the quenched galaxies of the four samples.


Within \CG, the star-forming main-sequence offsets of the embedded, isolated, and predominant classes are statistically consistent with one another.

We define \(\Delta m_{12}=M_{r,2}-M_{r,1}\) and \(\Delta m_{14}=M_{r,4}-M_{r,1}\), so that a more dominant BGG has a positive, larger gap; large gaps are the classic optical signature of fossil-like assembly \citep{Ponman+94,Jones+03,Dariush+10}.

\subsection{Projected phase space}\label{app:phase_space}

We ask whether the \CG{}--\RG{} satellite contrasts vary with projected position relative to the BGG, using 174 \CG{} and 166 \RG{} satellites ranked by projected BGG-centric distance and, when available, \(|\Delta v_{\rm BGG}|/\sigma_v\); such coordinates are statistical proxies for infall state, not orbital reconstructions \citep{Mahajan+11,Oman&Hudson16,Wetzel+13}.

In the innermost satellite-distance bin, the quenched fractions are 68.3\% for \CG{} and 48.2\% for \RG, a difference of \(20.1\) percentage points; the corresponding GZ1 E-class fractions are 63.8\% and 37.5\% (difference \(26.3\) points).

The quenched-fraction contrast is similar between the inner and outer distance ranks, so the data do not isolate the \CG{} offset to the immediate BGG environment.



After adjusting for stellar mass, redshift, distance bin, and available group-scale controls, the quenched-status model gives a \CG{} odds ratio of 1.18 (95\% CI 0.43--3.24; \(p = 0.742\)), and the analogous E-class model gives 2.17 (\(p = 0.092\)).


A nearest-neighbour mass--redshift matched check on 126 \CG{}--\RG{} satellite pairs gives matched quenched- and E-class-fraction differences of \(9.5\) and \(19.3\) percentage points.

In compact groups the member counts are small, velocity dispersions are noisy, and projection mixes recently accreted and long-resident satellites, so these bins locate where the contrast appears; they are not a dynamical clock.


Group radii and crossing times use projected physical separations from the adopted Planck 2015 angular-diameter distance. This corrects the luminosity-distance convention used for that diagnostic in Paper~I, decreasing the values by 3.3--9.6\% (median 7.2\%) without changing the reported correlations at quoted precision. The optical-colour offsets remain control dependent; full diagnostics are in the online supplement.

\subsection{Dominance}\label{app:dominance}









We split groups into dominated systems, where the BGG contributes more than 60\% of the group luminosity, and non-dominated systems. Because this appendix screens many group properties, \(p_\mathrm{adj}\) denotes the false-discovery-rate adjustment of Appendix~\ref{app:stat_conventions}; Cliff's \(\delta\) is the rank-based effect size. The split does not produce a compact-group signal by itself: across the control samples, the clearest binary separation is in group luminosity, dominated systems having lower \(L_\mathrm{group}\) than their non-dominated counterparts (Control$_{4B}$: $9.1\times 10^{10}$ versus $1.28\times 10^{11}$ $L_\odot$, \(p_\mathrm{adj}<10^{-4}\), \(\delta=-0.55\); Control$_{4C}$: $9.66\times 10^{10}$ versus $1.07\times 10^{11}$ $L_\odot$, \(p_\mathrm{adj}<10^{-4}\), \(\delta=-0.25\); RG$_4$: $7.09\times 10^{10}$ versus $9.82\times 10^{10}$ $L_\odot$, \(p_\mathrm{adj}=1\times 10^{-4}\), \(\delta=-0.59\)), the negative \(\delta\) meaning that dominated groups occupy the lower-luminosity side of each comparison.

\subsection{Crossing time}\label{app:crossing_time}







Group radii and \(t_\mathrm{cross}\) use projected physical separations from the Planck 2015 angular-diameter distance, correcting the luminosity-distance convention used for this diagnostic in \citet{Tricottet+25}; the values decrease by 3.3--9.6\% (median 7.2\%), without changing the reported correlations at quoted precision. The crossing-time diagnostic mainly separates systems by dynamical scale rather than by luminosity: shorter \(t_\mathrm{cross}\) systems have larger virial mass-to-light ratios and virial masses, with correlations of \(\rho_\mathrm{S}=-0.39\) (\(p<10^{-4}\)) with \(\log(\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{VT}/L_r)\) and \(\rho_\mathrm{S}=-0.33\) (\(p<10^{-4}\)) with \(\log(\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{VT})\) over 816 groups, whereas the relation with total group luminosity is formally detected but very weak (\(\rho_\mathrm{S}=0.08\)). We therefore read \(t_\mathrm{cross}\) as a dynamical compactness indicator tied to virial mass and mass-to-light ratio, with little independent leverage from \(L_\mathrm{group}\).

\section{Robustness details}\label{app:robustness_details}


\paragraph{Sample-definition sensitivities.} Restoring the 16 Zheng--Shen split systems while retaining the published model definitions leaves the morphology result qualitatively unchanged: the adjusted all-galaxy E-class odds ratios against \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{} are 1.85 (95\% CI \([1.39, 2.46]\)), 1.44 (\([1.10, 1.88]\)), and 2.38 (\([1.58, 3.58]\)), and the corresponding satellite odds ratios are 2.42 (\([1.80, 3.25]\)), 1.78 (\([1.35, 2.35]\)), and 2.75 (\([1.69, 4.49]\)). Retaining the anomalous Lim 3688 quartets likewise leaves the coefficients essentially unchanged: the all-galaxy and satellite odds ratios are 2.03 and 2.48 against \CB{}, and 1.60 and 1.86 against \CC{}.









\paragraph{Crowding.} Galaxy Zoo morphology is image-based, so crowded closest pairs could bias the votes. After excluding galaxies whose nearest projected neighbour within the catalogue group lies within 55 arcsec (retaining 200 \CG{} and 2534 \CC{} galaxies), the \CG{} all-row E-class fraction \emph{rises} relative to the full-sample value of Table~\ref{tab:morphologies} (55.0\% against 41.9\% for \CC{}, all retained rows including uncertain and missing classes), and the \CG{}--\CC{} contrast remains clear. In the joint model, the close-neighbour flag has OR \(2.00\) (\(p = 4.4\times 10^{-4}\)), while the \CG{} coefficient at fixed flag status is OR \(1.66\) (\(p = 6.6\times 10^{-4}\)); the former compares close with non-close galaxies, the latter \CG{} with controls at fixed flag status, so neither contradicts the rising raw \CG{} fraction after exclusion. The checks do not prove immunity to crowding, but they rule out the closest-pair artefact as the sole origin of the result.





\paragraph{Without \(\sigma_v\).} Refitting the per-control and pooled models without the \(\sigma_v\) covariate, on identical samples and with identical clustering, moves the E-class odds ratios from 1.99 to 2.06 (\CB{}), 1.60 to 1.59 (\CC{}), and 2.52 to 2.58 (\RG{}), less than their standard errors. The galaxy-level match retains the same 234 \CG{} galaxies with an E-class fraction difference of \(0.14\). The per-control group-level differences become \(\Delta = 0.22\), \(0.14\), and \(0.22\) against \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{} (permutation \(p = 0.0002\), \(0.011\), \(0.0013\)). The \CB{} change reflects the composition of the matched set rather than the \CG{} side: the with- and without-\(\sigma_v\) matches share only 9 of 57 control groups, the matched-control mean E-class satellite fraction changes by \(-0.09\) while the \CG{} mean is unchanged, and without \(\sigma_v\) the matched controls are dynamically cooler than their \CG{} partners (residual \(\sigma_v\) standardized mean difference \(0.49\), medians 158 against 123 \(\kms\)), so the with-\(\sigma_v\) match remains the primary one and the two values bracket the \CB{} contrast.







\paragraph{Within-host estimators.} The conditional-logit sample of Sect.~\ref{sec:morphology} contains 803 galaxies in 54 host strata. A binomial GLM with host fixed-effect dummies and cluster-robust standard errors gives OR \(1.56\) (95\% CI \([1.00, 2.42]\); \(p = 0.049\)); we prefer the conditional logit because it eliminates the host intercepts rather than estimating many indicators from small strata. The within-host quenched odds ratio is \(0.75\) (95\% CI \([0.45, 1.25]\); \(p = 0.269\); 915 complete cases), and \(1.22\) without the radius covariate (\(p = 0.412\)). A host-BGG alignment check supports the satellite interpretation: 56 of 62 non-split \CG{} systems have the same quartet and Lim-host BGG, the 6 misaligned systems are embedded systems whose host BGG is brighter than the compact-group BGG, and all 186 quartet-rank \CG{} satellites are Lim-host satellites; restricting \CG{} to the 56 BGG-aligned systems leaves the three satellite E-class contrasts stable (against \CC{}, OR \(1.94\)).




\paragraph{DS18 classification.} The DS18 cross-match of Sect.~\ref{sec:ssfr_classification} covers 173/248 (70\%) of the \CG{} galaxies and 2263/3609 (63\%) of the deduplicated controls, i.e. the DR7-era population; it is not worse in \CG{}, but neither rate establishes representativeness. With the DS18 \(T\mathrm{Type}\leq0\) outcome and the per-control covariates, standardisation, and physical-group clustering of Sect.~\ref{sec:morphology}, the \CG{} coefficients are positive in all three contrasts (Table~\ref{tab:morph_robustness}); support is broad except that 83\% of \CG{} values lie within the \RG{} group-luminosity range, limiting that smallest comparison. Because DS18 \(P_{\rm S0}\) separates S0 from E only within \(T\mathrm{Type}\leq0\), the environment dependence of the E/S0 mix is left to a dedicated analysis.

\section{Extended size diagnostics}\label{app:size_details}












The size-availability counts are included in Fig.~\ref{fig:data_availability}; the descriptive mass--size relations and the per-sample sizes predicted at \(\log(M_\star/M_\odot) = 10.3\) are provided in the online supplement. The concentration index \(C = R_{90,r}/R_{50,r}\) provides a model-independent structural proxy: the adjusted satellite coefficient is \(0.068\) (\(p = 0.124\)), and the matched concentration difference is \(-0.008\) (\(p = 0.904\)), offering no evidence for a fixed-mass concentration shift.


The crowding checks mirror Appendix~\ref{app:robustness_details}: excluding galaxies with a projected neighbour within 55 arcsec leaves a satellite coefficient of \(-0.013\) dex (\(p = 0.463\)), and adding the close-neighbour flag as a covariate gives \(-0.011\) dex (\(p = 0.479\)).



The per-galaxy difference between the two measures, \(d = \log R_{e,r} - \log R_{50,r}\), averages \(0.054\) dex, and is larger for GZ1 E-class galaxies (\(0.076\) dex) than for S-class galaxies (\(0.041\) dex), as expected if the Petrosian aperture preferentially truncates concentrated profiles. Its rank correlation with the nearest-neighbour separation is \(\rho_\mathrm{S} = 0.012\) (\(p = 0.524\)), with no detected monotonic relation to neighbour separation.



Adding the pairwise projected tidal index of Sect.~\ref{sec:tidal_index} to the all-galaxy size model changes the \CG{} coefficient from \(-0.012\) dex to \(0.005\) dex on the pairwise complete-case sample; the local projected-density proxy attenuates the size coefficient in the same way it attenuates the morphology coefficient.



The radial diagnostic is exploratory: mass- and redshift-adjusted satellite size residuals correlate with projected BGG-centric distance with \(\rho_\mathrm{S} = 0.17\) (\(p = 0.026\)) in \CG{} and \(\rho_\mathrm{S} = 0.18\) (\(p<10^{-4}\)) in the pooled controls. A more negative inner-region residual would be the tidal-truncation expectation; the observed trends are similar in \CG{} and the controls, so the data do not isolate a \CG{}-specific inner-region size suppression.





Within morphology classes, the satellite-only fits give \(0.031\) dex for GZ1 E-class satellites (\(p = 0.091\)) and \(0.000\) dex for S-class satellites (\(p = 0.998\)), and the \CG{}\(\times\)morphology interaction is \(0.022\) dex (\(p = 0.491\)). These strata are secondary diagnostics of whether size carries information beyond the morphology shift.
