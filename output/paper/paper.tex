  \abstract
  {Compact groups are dense in projection, but most lie inside larger groups, so their galaxies must be compared with those of ordinary groups as well as the field.}
  {We ask whether compact-group satellites have distinctive morphologies and whether any difference persists against ordinary-group cores of similar compactness.}
  {We compare 62 compact groups of four galaxies whose members all belong to one host group (non-split; \CG) with three ordinary-group controls: groups with exactly four members, quartets of the four brightest eligible members, and projected cores of the brightest group galaxy and its three nearest eligible companions (\CC). We measure the fraction of satellites in the Galaxy Zoo~1 E class (an early-type proxy including S0s) among those classified E or spiral, with covariate-adjusted models, matched quartets, and host-group bootstraps.}
  {After adjustment for stellar mass, redshift, and group properties, \CG{} satellite E-class odds ratios against the three full controls are 1.9--2.8. Against \CC{}, the adjusted difference is \(+14.4\) points (95\% CI \([+7.3, +21.5]\)). Within the shared range of median projected pair separation (94--219~kpc), it is \(+4.8\) points (\([-4.6, +14.1]\)) without a separation term and \(+4.0\) points (\([-5.4, +13.3]\)) at 150~kpc with separate slopes; separation-matched quartets give \(+5\) to \(+8\) points. The reduction, \(+10.4\) points (\([+4.4, +16.4]\); \(p = 8\times 10^{-4}\) in a paired host bootstrap), is statistically established and arises mainly from the widely separated \CC{} cores. Quenching shows no clear excess at fixed morphology, and satellite sizes show no offset at fixed stellar mass (\(-0.010\)~dex).}
  {Compact-group satellites are more often early-type than those of ordinary groups as a whole, but most of this excess is shared by ordinary-group cores of similar compactness. At matched separation, no compact-group-specific excess is detected; the residual remains uncertain (\([-5, +13]\) points), and the 11 compact groups below 94~kpc are too few to constrain the densest regime.}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Given their high apparent densities and low velocity dispersions \citep{Hickson82,Hickson+92}, compact groups appear to be ideal sites for the frequent tidal encounters and mergers that alter galaxy masses, luminosities, sizes, morphologies, and star-formation rates (e.g., \citealt{Mamon92}). \citet{MendesdeOliveira&Hickson94} provided the classic report of an excess of early-type galaxies in compact groups relative to the field (building on the photometric morphologies of \citealt{HKA89} and the merger-remnant expectations of \citealt{ZepfWhitmore93}), in line with the broader morphology--density relation \citep{Dressler80}. Comparisons with loose groups and the field have since found broad differences: \citet{Coenda+12} found higher fractions of red and early-type galaxies in SDSS compact groups than in loose groups and the field, with smaller, more concentrated galaxies of higher surface brightness; multi-wavelength studies likewise report altered infrared, UV, star-formation, and stellar-population properties \citep{Johnson+07,Tzanavaris+10,Bitsakis+11}, and H\,{\sc i} deficiency has been organised into an evolutionary sequence \citep{VerdesMontenegro+01}.

The interpretation of such contrasts is nevertheless ambiguous. Projected selection can include chance alignments, and many Hickson-like systems need not be isolated bound entities: apparent compact groups may be transient alignments, dense substructures, or cores embedded in looser groups and richer haloes \citep{Mamon86,DiazGimenez&Mamon10,DiazGimenez+20,Zheng&Shen21,Taverna+23}. The relevant observational question is therefore not only whether compact-group galaxies differ from field galaxies, but whether compact-group satellites differ from those of ordinary-group cores of similar compactness, selected with comparable luminosity, redshift, and membership constraints. Structural studies with the Southern Photometric Local Universe Survey (S-PLUS) find evidence for transformation in compact groups, especially non-isolated systems \citep{Montaguth2023,Montaguth2025,Montaguth2026MassSize,Montaguth2026NonIsolated}; \citet{Montaguth2026NonIsolated} compared non-isolated compact-group galaxies with the other galaxies of their host structures, but not with ordinary-group quartets reproducing compact-group membership, luminosity ranking, or projected-core selection.

In \citet[hereafter Paper~I]{Tricottet+25}, we found that, once the direct consequences of the compact-group selection are accounted for, compact groups of four galaxies (\CG) have group-scale properties broadly similar to those of ordinary groups of four galaxies and to the eligible cores of ordinary groups. Here we ask how the galaxy-population contrast changes across three ordinary-group comparison samples: regular (ordinary) groups with exactly four members (\RG{}), the four brightest eligible members of groups with at least four members (\CB{}), and projected cores centred on the brightest group galaxy (BGG; \CC{}), the closest analogue of a compact configuration. By separating satellites from BGGs and comparing both full samples and quartets of similar projected separation, we test whether compact-group satellites differ from those of ordinary-group cores of similar compactness. Section~\ref{sec:data} describes the samples, the classifications, and the statistical approach; Sect.~\ref{sec:results} presents the results; Sect.~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses their physical interpretation; and Sect.~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Data and classifications}\label{sec:data}
\subsection{Samples}\label{sec:samples}
We use the compact-group and control samples constructed in Paper~I. Galaxy properties are taken from the catalogue of \citet{Tempel+17}, based on SDSS DR12 \citep{York+00,Alam+15}, while ordinary groups are drawn from the catalogue of \citet{Lim+17}, built on SDSS DR7 \citep{Abazajian+09} with the halo-based group finder of \citet{Yang+07}; the two catalogues were matched through SDSS object identifiers to define the Lim--Tempel working catalogue. Compact groups are selected from the modified Hickson compact-group (HMCG) catalogue of \citet{DiazGimenez+18}: reliable HMCGs with exactly four members, all present in the Lim--Tempel catalogue, satisfying the volume and BGG-luminosity limits $0.005 \le z_{\rm BGG} \le 0.0452$ and $M_{r,\rm BGG}\leq -21.81$. HMCG membership uses the Hickson three-magnitude concordance in apparent $r$ magnitude, while control eligibility uses catalogue absolute magnitudes (Appendix~\ref{app:cg_core_overlap}). Following \citet{Zheng&Shen21}, a compact group is \emph{split} if its four members do not all belong to a single Lim--Tempel group; removing split systems leaves 62 of the initial 78 compact groups, which are \emph{isolated} when the four members constitute their whole Lim--Tempel group, \emph{predominant} when they contribute at least half of the host-group $r$-band luminosity, and \emph{embedded} otherwise (\nGrIso, \nGrPre, and \nGrEmb{} systems).
\footnote{The embedded and predominant assignments were interchanged in parts of Paper~I, including its Sect.~3.3. We correct the inherited catalogue labels to the definitions adopted here. Statements about embedded systems in Paper~I apply to predominant systems and vice versa; its results for isolated systems and for the full sample, which do not use these two labels, are unaffected.}

The three control samples are built from Lim--Tempel groups satisfying the same redshift and BGG-luminosity limits, with at least four members and at least three satellites within 3 absolute magnitudes of the BGG, and serve different purposes (Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic}). \RG{} contains the regular (ordinary) groups with exactly four members and is the most direct equal-membership comparison. \CB{} contains the four brightest eligible members of ordinary groups with at least four members and tests whether the \CG{} population resembles the luminous core of an ordinary group. \CC{} contains the BGG and its three closest projected companions among members within 3 magnitudes of the BGG; as in the Paper~I definition, the magnitude criterion is applied before companions are ranked by projected distance. Selecting the nearest companions makes \CC{} a projected-core sample, without imposing the surface-density requirement of the compact-group catalogue. In 46 of 56 non-isolated compact groups, all four members coincide with the candidate \CC{} core of the same host before exclusion (Appendix~\ref{app:cg_core_overlap}). The exclusion acts quartet by quartet: a candidate quartet is removed if it contains any galaxy of the initial 78 compact groups (split systems included), while another quartet of the same host remains eligible if it contains none (Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic}b). We also exclude Lim group 3688 because one redshift outlier enters both candidate quartets and inflates their velocity dispersions to $\simeq 2000\kms$ (Appendix~\ref{app:cg_core_overlap}). Table~\ref{tab:samples} summarises the final samples, which overlap; the \CB{} and \CC{} group counts differ because the luminosity-ranked and distance-ranked definitions retain different Lim groups after the compact-group exclusion.

We use SDSS DR16 \citep{Ahumada+20} to retrieve stellar masses, star formation rates \citep[SFRs;][]{Brinchmann+04,Kauffmann+03b}, emission-line measurements, and Galaxy Zoo morphologies \citep{Lintott+08,GalaxyZoo1}; Appendix~\ref{app:sdss_query} lists the tables and joins. Throughout, `SDSS non-AGN reference' denotes the AGN-filtered reference sample used for calibration and orientation: spectroscopic galaxies with \(0.005 \le z \le 0.0452\), extinction-corrected Petrosian \(r \le 17.77\), a valid MPA-JHU stellar mass, and finite Galaxy Zoo vote fractions after the AGN filter. It is a field-dominated reference population, not a control sample. Projected physical separations are derived from angular-diameter distances in the adopted Planck 2015 cosmology \citep{Planck15}, as in Paper~I; all distances quoted below are in kpc.

\subsection{Classifications}\label{sec:ssfr_classification}
Star-formation classes are based on the MPA-JHU total specific star-formation rates \citep{Brinchmann+04,Kauffmann+03b} stored in the DR12 group catalogues. Rows without a valid estimate are treated as \emph{missing data}: they are excluded from every sSFR-based fraction and figure and reported as counts only (Table~\ref{tab:sSFR_status}), so all quenched and star-forming fractions are computed among classified galaxies. The missing-sSFR incidence is higher in \CG{} (7\% of members, 10\% of BGGs) than in the reference sample (1\%), mostly because BOSS spectra lie outside the MPA-JHU coverage (Appendix~\ref{app:sdss_query}). Galaxies with a measured sSFR are split into quenched and star-forming classes with a two-component Gaussian mixture model (GMM) in the \(\log M_\star\)--\(\log\mathrm{sSFR}\) plane (Appendix~\ref{app:ssfr_method}, Fig.~\ref{fig:sSFR_classification}).

Morphologies are drawn from Galaxy Zoo 1 \citep{Lintott+08,GalaxyZoo1}, whose debiased catalogue vote fractions \citep{Bamford+09} are \texttt{p\_el\_debiased}, labelled ``elliptical'' in GZ1, and \texttt{p\_cs\_debiased}, labelled combined spiral. We define the GZ1 E class by \(\texttt{p\_el\_debiased}>0.5\), the S class by \(\texttt{p\_cs\_debiased}>0.5\), and the uncertain class when both vote fractions are finite but neither threshold is exceeded; rows with a missing vote fraction are treated as missing Galaxy Zoo data. GZ1 does not separate E from S0, so throughout this paper the E class is interpreted as a smooth early-type-like class, not as a physical sample of classical ellipticals. A cross-match with the deep-learning morphologies of \citet{DominguezSanchez2018} (DS18; 2436 of 3857 unique galaxies) quantifies this: 92\% of the 990 matched E-class galaxies have DS18 \(T\mathrm{Type}\leq0\) and 54\% are early types with \(P_{\rm S0}>0.5\); conversely, 73\% of the 730 DS18 early types with \(P_{\rm S0}>0.5\) fall in our E class, a share that drops from 81\% for face-on to 48\% for inclined systems (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness_details}), as expected if inclined S0s expose their discs.


The DR16 \texttt{zooSpec} join returns no votes for part of the sample. Since GZ1 is keyed to DR7 objects, we recover votes through the SkyServer DR7 cross-match only when the DR7 identifier is unique among the DR16 objects and the positions agree within 1.5 arcsec. This recovers 17 \CG{} galaxies; the classifications agree wherever both sources classify a galaxy. Finite votes below both thresholds remain uncertain, while votes missing from both sources remain missing. Existing DR16 classes without a verified DR7 counterpart are kept; they are not missing votes (Table~\ref{tab:morphologies}). We also retrieve the age-sensitive \(D_n4000\) index \citep{Kauffmann+03b} from the stored group-galaxy spectra for descriptive use (Sect.~\ref{sec:other_properties}, Appendix~\ref{app:selection}).





\subsection{Galaxy sizes}\label{sec:size_data}

Our primary size measure is the circular effective (half-light) radius of the pure S\'ersic fit in the $r$ band, \(R_{e,r}\), from the structural catalogue of \citet{Simard2011}: PSF-convolved fits with the GIM2D software (Galaxy IMage 2D; \citealt{Simard+02}) with re-derived sky levels and deblending designed for crowded fields, so the sizes are corrected for seeing. The catalogue is keyed to SDSS DR7 identifiers; we bridge our DR16 objects to their DR7 counterparts through the SkyServer \texttt{SpecDR7} cross-match table. Rows whose Simard redshift differs from the catalogue redshift by more than 0.005 are treated as wrong matches and dropped (9 rows). The Simard sizes, tabulated in kpc under the catalogue's own cosmology, are converted back to angular sizes with the catalogue plate scale and then to kpc with the same Planck 2015 conversion as every other projected quantity.

As a secondary, model-independent measure we use the SDSS Petrosian radii \texttt{petroR50\_r} and \texttt{petroR90\_r}, which also define the concentration index \(C = R_{90,r}/R_{50,r}\) and provide the like-for-like bridge to \citet{Coenda+12}. Petrosian radii are not corrected for seeing, and the Petrosian aperture misses roughly 20\% of the flux of an \(n=4\) profile, which biases \(R_{50,r}\) low for concentrated galaxies \citep{Blanton2001,Graham2005}. All sizes are required to lie between 0.1 and 50 kpc after re-conversion; S\'ersic fits pegged at the GIM2D bounds (\(n_g \le 0.55\) or \(n_g \ge 7.9\)) are excluded from the primary sample: 44 unique galaxies (60 catalogue rows), including 5 of the 199 Simard-matched \CG{} members (4 with \(n_g \le 0.55\) and 1 with \(n_g \ge 7.9\)), against 1.0--1.5\% of the matched controls; Petrosian entries must satisfy \(R_{50,r}>0\), \(R_{90,r}>R_{50,r}\), and a finite positive uncertainty. The completeness of both measures is given in Appendix~\ref{app:selection}.


\subsection{Statistical approach}\label{sec:stats}

Our main question is whether compact-group galaxies retain distinct properties once differences in stellar mass, redshift, BGG/satellite status, and group properties are taken into account. The three controls address physically different comparisons, so we compare \CG{} with each one separately. Galaxy-level logistic models quantify the adjusted E-versus-S and quenched-versus-star-forming contrasts; group-level matching compares the satellite mix of whole quartets; and within-host models compare compact-group members with their non-compact co-members. The covariates are stellar mass, redshift, BGG/satellite status, quartet luminosity (the summed \(r\)-band luminosity of the four members), and the quartet velocity dispersion \(\sigma_v\); because \(\sigma_v\) from four tracers is noisy, every analysis that uses it is repeated without it (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness_details}).

We account for dependence among galaxies in the same group by clustering standard errors at the level of the \emph{physical} Lim group and resampling by physical group or matched pair, as appropriate. We report two-sided p-values with effect sizes and confidence intervals for the primary morphology, star-formation, matching, within-host, and size analyses, and reserve multiple-testing correction for the secondary diagnostics. The p-value conventions, the completeness requirement for the control variables, the overlap coefficient, and the treatment of the overlapping control samples in pooled summaries are collected in Appendix~\ref{app:stat_conventions}.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Morphology}\label{sec:morphology}

\CG{} galaxies are more often in the GZ1 E class than the galaxies of every control. Tables~\ref{tab:sSFR_status} and \ref{tab:morphologies} give the raw class counts; among galaxies with a usable E or S classification, the \CG{} E-class fraction is 58\% against 47\%, 48\%, and 33\% in \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{} (Fisher exact tests: \(p = 2.2\times 10^{-3}\), \(p = 4.3\times 10^{-3}\), and \(p<10^{-4}\)). The 22 \CG{} rows with uncertain or missing Galaxy Zoo classifications are excluded rather than assigned to either class. Satellites account for most of the difference: their E-class fraction is 55\% in \CG{} against 40\%, 41\%, and 28\% (\(p = 2.3\times 10^{-4}\), \(p = 6.1\times 10^{-4}\), and \(p<10^{-4}\)), whereas among usable BGG classifications the E-class fraction is 66\% in \CG{} against 67\%, 67\%, and 47\% (Fisher \(p\) = 0.88, 0.88, and 0.07). Restricted to star-forming galaxies, the E/S mix is compatible with every control (two-sided Fisher exact test: \(p = 0.372\), \(p = 0.607\), \(p = 0.374\)), so star-forming E-class galaxies do not produce the raw excess.

\subsection{Star formation at fixed morphology}\label{sec:star_formation}

The star-formation contrast is weaker and control-dependent (Table~\ref{tab:adjusted_models}). It is clearest against \RG{} in the raw data, but the adjusted and matched results do not support a control-independent excess of quenching; assigning all missing-sSFR galaxies to either extreme class does not erase the raw \RG{} contrast (Appendix~\ref{app:ssfr_method}).





Morphology accounts for part of the satellite-quenching contrast, and no clear association remains once it is fixed. Without morphology, the adjusted satellite odds ratio against \CB{} is \(2.16\) (95\% CI \([1.51, 3.09]\); \(p<10^{-4}\)) (Table~\ref{tab:adjusted_models}). In direct satellite-only models that add the binary GZ1 E/S indicator to stellar mass, redshift, quartet luminosity, and velocity dispersion, the \CG{} odds ratios are \(1.56\) (95\% CI \([0.99, 2.48]\); \(p = 0.057\)), \(1.12\) (\([0.74, 1.70]\); \(p = 0.591\)), and \(1.14\) (\([0.55, 2.36]\); \(p = 0.731\)) against \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{}. The 95\% interval for the largest estimate, against \CB{}, includes unity, and the other two estimates lie close to unity. On the same \CB{} comparison sample with usable morphologies, omitting the morphological term raises the odds ratio from \(1.56\) to \(2.31\), showing that the attenuation also occurs when the fitted population is held fixed.

The descriptive conditional fractions are \(P(\mathrm{Q}\,|\,\mathrm{E}) = \qEcg\), \qEcb, \qEcc, and \qErg{} for \CG{}, \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{} (Table~\ref{tab:quenched_by_morphology}). Among galaxies with both a morphology class and an sSFR classification, we decompose each total \CG{}-minus-control quenched-fraction difference algebraically \citep{Kitagawa1955} into a term for the different morphological mix and a conditional term for quenching differences within morphological classes (Appendix~\ref{app:ssfr_method}, Table~\ref{tab:quenching_decomposition}). The 95\% intervals for the conditional terms, obtained by resampling physical hosts, are \([\condloCBPp, \condhiCBPp]\), \([\condloCCPp, \condhiCCPp]\), and \([\condloRGPp, \condhiRGPp]\) points. All include zero and still allow differences of several percentage points. The individually matched quenched-fraction difference is \(+3.1\) points (95\% CI \([-5.6, +12.2]\); \(p = 0.526\)). With the fixed threshold $\log_{10}(\mathrm{sSFR}/\mathrm{yr}^{-1})=-11.0$, the \CG{} satellite star-forming fraction is 41.4\%, against 43.2\%, 47.0\%, and 60.2\% in \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{} (Fisher \(p = 0.690\), \(p = 0.155\), and \(p = 5.3\times 10^{-4}\)), so only the \RG{} difference is clear.







No robust \CG{} displacement of the star-forming main sequence is established: the unmatched median offsets are comparison-dependent, while the matched star-forming pairs give a small mean difference whose interval includes zero. The sequence fits are described in Appendix~\ref{app:baseline_details}; the cumulative distributions, the full numerical comparison, and the weakly informative quenched-sequence residuals (MPA-JHU sSFRs of quenched galaxies are largely \(D_n4000\)-calibrated) are deposited on Zenodo.


\subsection{Compact-group classes}\label{sec:cg_classes}

Within \CG{}, the satellite E-class fraction is lower in isolated systems than in predominant and embedded systems (Fig.~\ref{fig:cg4_classes}). The aggregate fractions \(f_{\rm E}=N_{\rm E}/(N_{\rm E}+N_{\rm S})\) are \(\feSatIso\) for the \nGrIso{} isolated, \(\feSatPre\) for the \nGrPre{} predominant, and \(\feSatEmb\) for the \nGrEmb{} embedded systems (95\% Wilson intervals). The isolated compact groups are compatible with the \RG{} groups (group-level permutation test on the per-group mean satellite \(f_{\rm E}\), \(p = \pIsoRG\)) and sit below the rest of \CG{} (\(p = \pIsoRest\)), whereas the two non-isolated classes do not differ detectably (predominant-versus-embedded odds ratio at fixed stellar mass \(0.99\), \(p = 0.97\)). The host halo masses differ strongly between the classes (median \(\log M_{200c} = \lMIso\), \lMPre, and \lMEmb{} for isolated, predominant, and embedded systems), yet adding \(\log M_{200c}\) to the class model changes neither the class coefficients nor the conclusion (\(\log M_{200c}\) odds ratio \(0.85\) per standard deviation, \(p = 0.43\)). The contrast is consistent with the distinct host environments of isolated and embedded compact groups found by \citet{Mendel+11}, but with only \nGrIso{} isolated groups it is exploratory; other group-scale descriptors are examined in Appendix~\ref{app:env_diagnostics}.

\subsection{Local projected density}\label{sec:tidal_index}

Including local projected density attenuates the \CG{} coefficient, but the amount is not well determined. For each of the 6076 catalogue rows we compute \(T_i = \sum_j M_{\star,j}/R_{ij}^3\) over selected quartet co-members (\(R_{ij}\) in kpc; unmeasured companion masses contribute zero, making \(T_i\) a lower bound). For the main pooled fit, controls are deduplicated by objid as in Appendix~\ref{app:pooled_models}, retaining the quartet assignment in the priority order \RG{}, \CB{}, \CC{}. The E-class model adjusts for stellar mass and satellite status, with physical-group clustering. In this main model, the index lowers the \CG{} odds ratio from 1.75 (95\% CI \([1.33, 2.30]\); \(p<10^{-4}\)) to 1.19 (\([0.89, 1.60]\); \(p = 0.24\)) on the same 3416 galaxies. Retaining all catalogue rows instead (a sensitivity variant) gives an attenuation from 1.83 to 1.30 (\(p = 0.083\)), with an index odds ratio of 1.43 per \(\sdlogT\)-dex standard deviation (\(p<10^{-4}\)); this check weights multiply selected controls more heavily. In this sample, the \(\log T_i\) distributions have an overlap coefficient of 0.54 and their medians differ by 1.1 dex. Restricting the fit to their overlapping range gives an odds ratio of 1.14 (\(p = 0.38\)). Because \(T_i\) depends on the same projected configuration used to select compact groups, adjustment for the index also accounts for some of that selection. The attenuation alone leaves the physical role of local density in the morphological difference unresolved. The index traces projected neighbours, not tidal history; the host-inclusive check is given in Appendix~\ref{app:selection}.

\subsection{Other galaxy properties}\label{sec:other_properties}





None of the other galaxy properties shows an offset as robust as the morphology excess (Fig.~\ref{fig:property_trends}). Strong \(\mathrm{H}\alpha\) emission (\(\mathrm{H}\alpha\,\mathrm{EW} \leq -3\,\text{\AA}\), emission negative) is rarer in \CG{}: 70 of 217 members with measurements (32\%), against 41\%, 43\%, and 57\% in \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{} (Fisher \(p = 0.012\), \(p = 0.002\), \(p<10^{-4}\)). The deficit is not fully explained by the different morphology mix: after adjustment for stellar mass and GZ1 E/S class, the \CG{} odds ratios for strong \(\mathrm{H}\alpha\) emission are 0.55, 0.65, and 0.56 against \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{}, respectively (\(p = 0.002\), \(p = 0.027\), and \(p = 0.072\)). Adding redshift and the fibre-to-galaxy aperture ratio \(\log_{10}(1.5''/R_{50,r})\) leaves them at 0.53, 0.62, and 0.53, and adding satellite status and the group covariates as well gives 0.52, 0.62, and 0.64. These lines are joined by photometric \texttt{objid} through a query that requires a DR16 Galaxy Zoo match, which drops 342 spectra (17 in \CG{}) available under the stored spectrum identifier (Appendix~\ref{app:sdss_query}). With that complete join, the mass- and morphology-adjusted odds ratios are 0.62 (\(p = 0.010\)), 0.73 (\(p = 0.088\)), and 0.62 (\(p = 0.124\)).

These equivalent widths sample the 3-arcsec SDSS fibre, whereas the quenched class uses the total sSFR. For satellites against \CC{} with the stored-spectrum join, the E/S class absorbs about half of the deficit in the full samples, from \(-10.5\) to \(-4.3\) points, whereas restricting to the common range and fitting a common separation slope at 150~kpc brings no further reduction (\(-4.8\) points; paired difference \(+0.5\) points (\([-4.1, +5.5]\))); with the aperture term as well, the common-slope contrast is \(-7.5\) points (\([-14.5, -0.3]\)). Without morphology, the common-slope contrast is \(-6.0\) points (95\% CI \([-14.9, +2.8]\)) for all satellites and \(-14.7\) points (\([-28.3, -1.2]\)) in the mass bin \(10 \le \log(M_\star/\msun) < 10.5\) (61 \CG{} and 214 \CC{} satellites), a bin examined post hoc because the \CG{} E-class fraction and \(D_n4000\) peak there relative to the controls. After adjustment for E/S morphology and aperture and exclusion of AGN and composites on the Baldwin--Phillips--Terlevich (BPT) diagram \citep{BPT81}, the contrast in this bin is \(-9.5\) points (\([-26.1, +7.1]\)) on a smaller sample (29/97 satellites). In the same bin, the total-sSFR quenched-fraction contrast is \(+3.9\) points (\([-10.6, +18.4]\)) with a common slope and \(+2.6\) points (\([-11.9, +17.0]\)) with separate slopes (58/211 satellites); both intervals include zero. Table~\ref{tab:spectral_variants} gives the full spectral comparisons.

Among BPT-classified galaxies, the AGN-like fraction (above the \citealt{Kewley+01} line; composites lie between the \citealt{Kauffmann+03a} and \citealt{Kewley+01} lines) is 38\% in \CG{}, against 40\%, 35\%, and 30\% in \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{}. Adjusted for stellar mass, satellite status, BGG distance, and dominance, the \CG{} odds ratio is 1.17 (\(p = 0.364\)) in a model pooling all control rows, 1.17 on the deduplicated pool, and 1.15, 1.17, and 0.96 against each control; this diagnostic does not measure all nuclear activity. In Fig.~\ref{fig:property_trends}d, the \CG{} satellite fraction exceeds all three controls only in the 10.0--10.5 bin (40\% against 22\%, 24\%, and 27\% in \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{}) and is lower or similar elsewhere. The \(D_n4000\) index follows stellar mass in every sample; the \CG{} medians sit above the controls only in the \(10.0\)--\(10.5\) bin (\(D_n4000 = 1.77\) against 1.59, 1.68, and 1.56), where the \CG{} E-class fraction also peaks relative to the controls, and coincide with them elsewhere.










No clear adjusted size offset is detected between \CG{} and ordinary-group galaxies. In the pooled model with the seeing-corrected Simard radii, the satellite size offset at fixed stellar mass, redshift, and group-scale covariates is \(-0.010\) dex (\(-2\)\%; 95\% CI \([-0.039, 0.019]\); \(p = 0.507\); 2575 satellites in 754 physical groups), and \(-0.006\) dex for all galaxies (\(p = 0.653\)); the per-control estimates are \(-0.031\) dex against \CB{}, \(-0.001\) dex against \CC{}, \(-0.021\) dex against \RG{} (Appendix~\ref{app:size_details}, Fig.~\ref{fig:size_per_control}). The one-to-one matched difference is \(-0.019\) dex for the 166 pairs with Simard sizes (95\% CI \([-0.064, 0.026]\); \(p = 0.400\)), and \(-0.033\) dex for the 234 pairs with uncorrected Petrosian radii (\(p = 0.030\)). The Simard catalogue provides usable fits for 77\% of \CG{} galaxies and 71\% of the pooled controls. Petrosian sizes are available for 6073 of the 6076 catalogue rows (Fig.~\ref{fig:data_availability}), with an adjusted satellite offset of \(-0.019\) dex (\(p = 0.111\)). Colours, magnitude gaps, and projected phase-space trends are likewise control-dependent or weak; they are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:env_diagnostics}.












\subsection{Robustness}\label{sec:robustness}







The morphology excess does not depend on the vote threshold, on the classifier, or on the crowded closest pairs (Table~\ref{tab:morph_robustness}). Excluding every galaxy whose nearest catalogued neighbour lies within 55 arcsec, the SDSS fibre-collision scale, removes 19\% of \CG{} members against 6--10\% of the controls and moves the adjusted E-class odds ratios from 1.99 to 2.00 (\CB{}), 1.60 to 1.63 (\CC{}), and 2.52 to 2.44 (\RG{}). Across classification thresholds of 0.4--0.8 the pooled adjusted odds ratio stays between 1.65 and 1.93, the continuous debiased E vote fraction \(p_E\) itself is offset by \(0.076\) (\(p = 3.6\times 10^{-4}\)), and the Galaxy-Zoo-independent S\'ersic index (\(n_g > 2.5\); 75\% coverage) gives an odds ratio of 1.60 (\(p = 0.002\)). With the DS18 \(T\mathrm{Type}\leq0\) classification as the outcome, the per-control \CG{} odds ratios are 2.11, 1.49, and 2.22 (all \(p \leq 0.018\); \(n = 1822\), 1913, 324). Satellite-only versions of the odds-ratio checks give 1.65--2.97, all with \(p\leq 0.002\) (Table~\ref{tab:morph_robustness}). The crowding, threshold, and \(\sigma_v\) reruns are detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:robustness_details}.

\section{Interpretation and comparison with previous work}\label{sec:discussion}




\subsection{An early-type-like satellite excess linked to dense group cores}\label{sec:morph_local_density}


The morphology signature lies primarily in the satellites. Its persistence after stellar-mass adjustment indicates differences beyond the present-day mass distributions, although mass itself may record earlier environmental evolution. BGGs offer less contrast: the luminous-BGG selection places them at high masses where most are already early-type and quenched (Tables~\ref{tab:sSFR_status} and \ref{tab:morphologies}).

\citet{Martinez+13} compared compact and loose groups of similar total luminosity. Compact-group BGGs were brighter and more often Galaxy Zoo ellipticals overall, but at fixed BGG magnitude the elliptical fractions were comparable within errors, whereas colour- and concentration-based early-type fractions remained higher in compact groups. Our weak adjusted BGG contrast resembles their fixed-magnitude Galaxy Zoo result; their colour and concentration comparisons show how much depends on the early-type definition.

Most of the satellite excess over \CC{} reflects the larger separations of the ordinary-group cores (Fig.~\ref{fig:compactness}, Table~\ref{tab:group_match_per_control}). Restricting \CC{} to the separation range of \CG{} removes about 70\% of it (paired reduction \(+10.4\) points, \(p = 8\times 10^{-4}\)), and neither host richness nor host luminosity accounts for the remainder. At matched separation, the contrast is not distinguishable from zero, although its interval still admits an excess of 13 points, and the 11 most compact \CG{} quartets lie below the common range, where both samples have high E-class fractions but few hosts. At fixed stellar mass, smaller separations accompany higher E-class probabilities within \CG{}, \CB{}, and \CC{} (Table~\ref{tab:battery}), and the per-dex slopes of \CG{} and \CC{} are compatible (\(p \approx 0.46\)). Pre-processing in the host halo may build up an early-type satellite population before the compact configuration is observed. Radial segregation could raise the fraction of these satellites in dense cores, where dynamical friction favours the inward migration of more massive satellites and earlier arrivals have had more time to orbit and interact. Repeated tidal encounters, galaxy harassment, and minor mergers may heat or restructure discs and contribute to the S0 population, consistent with the structural trends reported in S-PLUS \citep{Montaguth2023,Montaguth2025,Montaguth2026MassSize,Montaguth2026NonIsolated}. As discussed in Sect.~5.2 of Paper~I, chance alignments of host members can give a small projected separation even for galaxies far apart in three dimensions; they can dilute a signal produced by close encounters, while their shared host can still contribute a morphological excess.

The shared-host models address location inside the same halo. \citet{Montaguth2026NonIsolated} found that non-isolated compact groups host more early-type and quenched galaxies than the other galaxies of their host structures, especially above \(\log(M_\star/\msun)=10.2\). Our within-host comparison agrees when radius is ignored (odds ratio \(1.89\), \([1.22, 2.93]\)), but adjusting for BGG-centric radius lowers the estimate to \(1.46\) (\([0.92, 2.33]\)), which rests on only 6 hosts with radial overlap between member and non-member satellites (Sect.~\ref{sec:morphology}); position within the host may therefore account for part of that contrast. Our explicit quartet controls complement their approach by showing how the comparison itself changes the inferred signature.

\subsection{GZ1 E-class morphologies and transition galaxies}\label{sec:s0_degeneracy}


Our E class is a smooth early-type-like proxy with a substantial S0 contribution (Sect.~\ref{sec:ssfr_classification}). The DS18 cross-match covers 63--70\% of the sample, and \(P_{\rm S0}\) distinguishes S0s from ellipticals only for matched DR7 galaxies with \(T\mathrm{Type}\leq0\). Within that early-type subset, the GZ1 classification of S0s depends strongly on inclination, and splitting the sample by control and environment leaves too few galaxies to estimate the elliptical and S0 contributions reliably (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness_details}). \citet{Montaguth2026MassSize} found in S-PLUS that early-type galaxies follow environment-independent mass--size relations, that late types in compact groups are only modestly smaller at fixed mass, and that transition galaxies (red discs) are smaller in groups than in the field. Our size offsets by morphology agree: at fixed stellar mass, \CG{} E-class satellites differ from the controls by \(+0.031\)~dex and S-class satellites by \(0.000\)~dex, neither significantly (Appendix~\ref{app:size_details}). Their hybrid structural--colour classes and size definitions differ from our GZ1 E/S classes and seeing-corrected Simard radii, and our E class mixes ellipticals with S0s, so a transition-galaxy size deficit need not appear in our classes.

\subsection{Star formation and sizes follow morphology}\label{sec:weaker_signals}





Morphology accounts for part of the full-sample quenching contrast, and the residual at fixed morphology is poorly constrained (Sect.~\ref{sec:star_formation}). A clear morphological excess with a weak residual quenching signal is compatible with earlier processing in the host. If disc transformation and the decline in star formation occurred on overlapping timescales, the present-day fractions can retain a shift in the mix of morphological classes even when the quenching fractions within each class are similar. Constraining the relative timescales would require spectroscopic reconstructions of individual star-formation histories.

Fibre H\(\alpha\) equivalent width measures emission relative to the central stellar continuum, with possible contributions from AGN, shocks, and old stars, whereas MPA-JHU total sSFRs use photometric aperture corrections and statistical estimates for weak-line systems \citep{Brinchmann+04,Salim+07}. The full-sample H\(\alpha\) deficit is not an aperture or redshift effect, but its significance against \CC{} depends on the emission-line join, morphology removes about half of it for satellites, and fixing the separation removes no more (Sect.~\ref{sec:other_properties}). In the intermediate-mass bin, excluding AGN and composites weakens the deficit, and both separation models give total-sSFR quenched contrasts spanning zero. These diagnostics therefore provide no clear evidence for centrally concentrated quenching beyond the morphology shift. Spectroscopic incompleteness also matters: 19\% of catalogued \CG{} galaxies have a neighbour below the 55-arcsec fibre-collision scale, against 8\% of pooled controls (10\% in \CC{}). Missing companions could hide brief episodes of enhanced or recently suppressed star formation and bias the tidal index downward.





The size comparison is sensitive to methodology. \citet{Coenda+12} reported smaller compact-group galaxies at fixed luminosity with Petrosian radii, although their more restrictive \(C>3\) selection showed no clear environmental trend. Our seeing-corrected Simard radii give no clear fixed-mass offset, with a 95\% interval still permitting a deficit of roughly \(0.04\)~dex, and the matched Simard and Petrosian differences use unequal available-pair subsets (Sect.~\ref{sec:other_properties}). The larger Simard-minus-Petrosian offsets for E-class galaxies (Appendix~\ref{app:size_details}) make morphology mix relevant, so differences between studies can reflect profile-dependent size estimates, sample selection, adjustment for luminosity or mass, and structural classification.


\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}


We have compared 62 low-redshift, luminous, non-split compact groups of four galaxies with three ordinary-group controls: groups with exactly four members, quartets of the four brightest eligible members, and BGG-centred projected cores. Our main results are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Morphology: \CG{} satellites are more often in the GZ1 E class than the satellites of all three full controls, with adjusted odds ratios of 1.86--2.80 (Table~\ref{tab:adjusted_models}). The excess persists across vote thresholds, an independent deep-learning classification, S\'ersic indices, crowding cuts, and the omission of \(\sigma_v\), for all galaxies and for satellites alone (Table~\ref{tab:morph_robustness}).
\item Compactness: against projected cores, the full-sample excess of \(+14.4\) points falls to \(+4.8\) points in the common separation range and to \(+4.0\) points (\([-5.4, +13.3]\)) at 150~kpc. The reduction, \(+10.4\) points (\(p = 8\times 10^{-4}\)), comes mainly from the widely separated \CC{} cores and is not explained by host richness or luminosity; separation-matched quartets give \(+5\) to \(+8\) points. The 11 compact groups below 94~kpc show no detected excess but are too few to constrain the densest regime.
\item Within hosts: compact-group members of predominant and embedded systems are more often in the E class than the other members of their hosts when radius is ignored (odds ratio 1.89), but only 6 hosts offer radial overlap, and the radius-adjusted estimate (1.46) is not significant.
\item Star formation and sizes: quenching shows no clear excess at fixed morphology. The fibre H\(\alpha\) deficit is halved by morphology, does not decrease further at fixed separation, and its significance depends on the emission-line join. Satellite sizes show no offset at fixed stellar mass (\(-0.010\)~dex).
\end{enumerate}
These results answer the question posed in the Introduction. Paper~I found that, once the selection is accounted for, the group-scale properties of compact groups resemble those of ordinary groups and of their cores; their satellite populations behave alike. Compact groups are special mainly in the way dense group cores are special: their satellites are more often early-type than those of ordinary groups as a whole, but most of that difference is shared by ordinary-group cores of similar compactness. A compact-group-specific residual of up to 13 points at fixed separation, or an excess confined to the densest systems, cannot be excluded with the present samples.

\section{SDSS data query}\label{app:sdss_query}

For the SDSS reference sample, we extract \texttt{sfr\_tot\_p50}, \texttt{specsfr\_tot\_p50}, and \texttt{lgm\_tot\_p50} from \texttt{galSpecExtra}, \texttt{h\_alpha\_eqw} and the [N\,{\sc ii}], H\(\alpha\), [O\,{\sc iii}], and H\(\beta\) fluxes from \texttt{galSpecLine}, and \texttt{p\_el\_debiased} and \texttt{p\_cs\_debiased} from \texttt{zooSpec}. The strong-emission flags from the two joins agree for all 5400 rows measured by both.





We audited all 6076 final sample rows against \texttt{SpecObjAll} in DR12, retaining the stored \texttt{specObjID} even when the same photometric \texttt{objid} has several spectra (looking these DR12 identifiers up in DR16 fails for reprocessed BOSS observations, whose identifier encodes the reduction version). \section{sSFR classification details}\label{app:ssfr_method}

We identify BPT-classifiable galaxies by requiring measured values of both emission-line ratios
\[
\log_{10}\bigl([\mathrm{N\,II}]\lambda6584/\mathrm{H}\alpha\bigr)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\log_{10}\bigl([\mathrm{O\,III}]\lambda5007/\mathrm{H}\beta\bigr).
\]
Among these galaxies, we flag AGN-like systems if they satisfy either
\[
\log_{10}\frac{[\mathrm{N\,II}]}{\mathrm{H}\alpha} > 0,
\]
or if they lie above the empirical demarcation of \citet{Kauffmann+03a}:
\[
\log_{10}\frac{[\mathrm{O\,III}]}{\mathrm{H}\beta}
> \frac{0.61}{\log_{10}([\mathrm{N\,II}]/\mathrm{H}\alpha)-0.05} \;+\; 1.3,
\]
in which case they are excluded from the GMM calibration sample. This calibration flag is deliberately more inclusive than the three-class BPT scheme of Sect.~\ref{sec:other_properties}, in which composite galaxies lie between the \citet{Kauffmann+03a} and \citet{Kewley+01} demarcations and only galaxies above the \citet{Kewley+01} line are labelled AGN-like, because its sole purpose is to keep possible AGN contamination out of the sSFR calibration. Galaxies with missing diagnostic line ratios are BPT-unclassified, not proven non-AGN; they are retained for the sSFR status assignment when valid SDSS stellar mass and sSFR estimates are available, while the AGN-like analysis treats them separately.
Reference-sample rows with unavailable \texttt{specsfr\_tot\_p50}, and group-sample rows with unavailable \texttt{sfr\_tot\_p50} or \texttt{lgm\_tot\_p50}, including the \(-9999\) sentinel, have no valid sSFR estimate; they are treated as missing data throughout: excluded from the GMM calibration where applicable, never assigned an sSFR class, omitted from every sSFR figure, and reported as counts only (Sect.~\ref{sec:ssfr_classification}).
Galaxies with a measured sSFR are divided into quenched and star-forming classes with a two-component Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) in the \(\log M_\star\)-\(\log\mathrm{sSFR}\) plane \citep{McLachlanPeel2000}; the boundary is the locus where the two component posterior probabilities are equal, and the resulting classification for the SDSS non-AGN reference is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:sSFR_classification}. A fixed-threshold check confirms that the qualitative hierarchy is not tied to the exact GMM split (Sect.~\ref{sec:star_formation}).

\section{Statistical conventions}\label{app:stat_conventions}

\paragraph{Independence unit.} The unit for every inferential contrast among the group catalogues is the \emph{physical} Lim group: standard errors are clustered by Lim group (unifying the overlapping control definitions, with \CG{} systems assigned to their host Lim group, or to their own identifier when they have no Lim counterpart). The decomposition resamples physical hosts jointly across comparison sides, and matched-quartet inference resamples connected components of the bipartite graph linking matched \CG{} quartets to their control hosts. Pooled models across the three controls are secondary summaries computed after deduplicating the overlapping control samples to one row per physical galaxy, with the kept label following the priority \RG{} \(>\) \CB{} \(>\) \CC{} (Appendix~\ref{app:pooled_models}). Purely descriptive reference-sample diagnostics report their own unit where they appear.

\paragraph{Tests and p-values.} Descriptive comparisons use two-sided Fisher exact tests for \(2\times2\) tables and rank-sum or bootstrap comparisons for distributions and median differences; Spearman and Pearson coefficients are diagnostics of monotonic or linear trends only. Paired sign-flip permutation p-values use add-one tail counts, \(p = (k+1)/(B+1)\) with \(k\) the number of permutations at least as extreme as the observation; bootstrap sign-crossing p-values are \(2\min[(k_{\le 0}+1)/(B+1),(k_{\ge 0}+1)/(B+1)]\), capped at unity, where \(k_{\le 0}\) and \(k_{\ge 0}\) count resampled differences on either side of zero; displayed p-values are floored at \(10^{-4}\). Adjusted odds ratios come from binomial generalised linear models with cluster-robust covariance; continuous covariates are standardised, so coefficients of continuous terms are per standard deviation of the fitted frame. Multiple-testing correction is reserved for exploratory tests. We apply Holm adjustment \citep{Holm1979} to the colour, measurement-availability, and magnitude-gap tests and Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate control \citep{BenjaminiHochberg1995} to the dominance, morphology--environment, and other group-scale tests. Paired comparisons between model designs (Sect.~\ref{sec:morphology}) refit every model on the same resampled physical hosts and evaluate it on the same \CG{} reference satellites; their intervals and sign-crossing p-values come from the resampled differences.


\paragraph{Covariates.} The velocity dispersion \(\sigma_v\) that enters models and matching is the quartet-level gapper estimate \citep{Wainer&Thissen76} on the four selected members' redshifts, converted as \(c\,\sigma_z/(1+\bar z)\), for \emph{all} samples, including \CB{} and \CC{} quartets embedded in richer hosts (this is how the spurious Lim 3688 quartets of Sect.~\ref{sec:samples} reach \({\simeq}2000\kms\)). With four tracers the estimator is intrinsically noisy: in a Gaussian Monte Carlo of \(10^5\) quartets, \(\sigma_{\rm gapper}/\sigma_{\rm true}\) has median 0.96 with a 16--84\% range of 0.57--1.42. A candidate covariate enters an adjusted model when it is non-missing for at least 65\% of that model's frame (matching variables require 70\%); in practice, quartet luminosity and velocity dispersion are complete. Individual stellar masses can be missing (Fig.~\ref{fig:data_availability}). The 14 \CG{} galaxies omitted from individual matching lack a stellar mass (Appendix~\ref{app:matching_details}). No harmonised group-mass covariate is available (Appendix~\ref{app:selection}).


\paragraph{Overlap coefficient.} For the \(\log T_i\) distributions in Sect.~\ref{sec:tidal_index}, the overlap coefficient is the integral of the minimum of the two normalised histograms on common bins, between 0 (disjoint) and 1 (identical).

\paragraph{Morphology and pair separation.} The fixed-mass points of Fig.~\ref{fig:compactness} use bin intercepts and a common stellar-mass slope within each sample. Points lie at the median satellite \(\log\Rmed\) in each bin, and bins with fewer than three hosts are omitted. The outermost \CC{} bin has 10 classified satellites in 4 hosts. Its rise over the preceding bin is \(+20.1\) points (95\% CI \([+5.4, +34.9]\)) and is based on few hosts. For the local reference in panel (b) and the model comparisons of Sect.~\ref{sec:morphology}, we select satellites from the shared range within 0.1~dex of the evaluation separation and require a counterpart within tolerances of 0.006 in redshift, 0.25~dex in stellar mass, 0.35~dex in quartet luminosity, and \(120\kms\) in velocity dispersion. We average both predicted probabilities over the observed covariates of the resulting \CG{} satellites, setting separation to the same value in models that include it. At least three physical hosts per sample are required. Pointwise intervals use the delta method with physical-host-clustered covariance.

\section{Main-sequence and quenched-sequence details}\label{app:baseline_details}

We model the star-forming main sequence as a function of stellar mass with polynomials of order 1--4 fitted to the star-forming galaxies of the SDSS non-AGN reference sample; the diagnostic is the residual RMS scatter, not a formal reduced \(\chi^2\), because no per-galaxy uncertainty model is used. We adopt order 2, the lowest order with the smallest five-fold cross-validated RMS (0.305 dex, against 0.306 dex for order 1), and compute the residual of each star-forming galaxy with respect to it; a positive residual means a higher sSFR at fixed stellar mass than the reference relation. In Sect.~\ref{sec:star_formation}, \(\Delta\) is the \CG{}-minus-control median residual with the central 68\% group-bootstrap interval on the median difference and the two-sided sign-crossing probability (10\,000 resamples). The quenched sequence deposited on Zenodo is obtained with the same procedure on the 7705 quenched galaxies of the reference sample: order 4 has the smallest cross-validated RMS (0.331 dex), and the residuals are attached to the quenched galaxies of the four samples.


Within \CG, the star-forming main-sequence offsets of the isolated, predominant, and embedded classes are statistically consistent with one another.

\subsection{Projected phase space}\label{app:phase_space}

We ask whether the \CG{}--\RG{} satellite contrasts vary with projected position relative to the BGG, using 174 \CG{} and 166 \RG{} satellites ranked by projected BGG-centric distance and, when available, \(|\Delta v_{\rm BGG}|/\sigma_v\); such coordinates are statistical proxies for infall state, not orbital reconstructions \citep{Mahajan+11,Oman&Hudson16,Wetzel+13}.

Satellites are ranked within each quartet by projected BGG distance into three bins (nearest, intermediate, and farthest satellite). In the innermost bin, the quenched fractions are 68.3\% for \CG{} and 48.2\% for \RG, a difference of \(20.1\) percentage points; the corresponding GZ1 E-class fractions are 63.8\% and 37.5\% (difference \(26.3\) points).

The quenched-fraction contrast is similar between the inner and outer distance ranks, so the data do not isolate the \CG{} offset to the immediate BGG environment.



After adjusting for stellar mass, redshift, distance bin, and available group-scale controls, the quenched-status model gives a \CG{} odds ratio of 1.18 (95\% CI \([0.43, 3.24]\); \(p = 0.742\)), and the analogous E-class model gives 2.17 (\(p = 0.092\)).


A nearest-neighbour mass--redshift matched check on 126 \CG{}--\RG{} satellite pairs gives matched quenched- and E-class-fraction differences of \(9.5\) and \(19.3\) percentage points.

In compact groups the member counts are small, velocity dispersions are noisy, and projection mixes recently accreted and long-resident satellites, so these bins locate where the contrast appears; they are not a dynamical clock.


Group radii and crossing times use projected physical separations from the adopted Planck 2015 angular-diameter distance. This corrects the luminosity-distance convention used for that diagnostic in Paper~I, decreasing the values by 3.2--8.7\% (median 6.7\%; the group redshifts reach 0.0468 because the redshift limit applies to the BGG) without changing the reported correlations at quoted precision, so the conclusions of Paper~I are unaffected. The optical-colour offsets remain control dependent; full diagnostics are deposited on Zenodo.

We split groups into dominated systems, where the BGG contributes more than 60\% of the group luminosity, and non-dominated systems. Because this appendix compares many group properties, \(p_\mathrm{adj}\) denotes the false-discovery-rate adjustment of Appendix~\ref{app:stat_conventions}; Cliff's \(\delta\) is the rank-based effect size. The split does not produce a compact-group signal by itself: across the control samples, the clearest binary separation is in group luminosity, dominated systems having lower \(L_\mathrm{group}\) than their non-dominated counterparts (Control$_{4B}$: $9.1\times 10^{10}$ versus $1.28\times 10^{11}$ $L_\odot$, \(p_\mathrm{adj}<10^{-4}\), \(\delta=-0.55\); Control$_{4C}$: $9.66\times 10^{10}$ versus $1.07\times 10^{11}$ $L_\odot$, \(p_\mathrm{adj}<10^{-4}\), \(\delta=-0.25\); RG$_4$: $7.09\times 10^{10}$ versus $9.82\times 10^{10}$ $L_\odot$, \(p_\mathrm{adj}=1\times 10^{-4}\), \(\delta=-0.59\)), the negative \(\delta\) meaning that dominated groups occupy the lower-luminosity side of each comparison.




Splitting instead at each sample's median BGG luminosity fraction (\(f_{L,\rm BGG} = 0.63\), 0.52, 0.61, and 0.59 for \CG{}, \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{}) gives the same picture: the FDR-significant separations are \CB{} in  $L_\mathrm{group}$ ($L_\odot$) (\(\delta = -0.53\), \(p_{\rm adj} = 0.000\)), \CC{} in  $L_\mathrm{group}$ ($L_\odot$) (\(\delta = -0.24\), \(p_{\rm adj} = 0.000\)), \RG{} in  $L_\mathrm{group}$ ($L_\odot$) (\(\delta = -0.58\), \(p_{\rm adj} = 0.000\)), \CB{} in $\Delta_\mathrm{BGG-cen}/ \langle R_{ij} \rangle$ (\(\delta = -0.17\), \(p_{\rm adj} = 0.000\)), \CB{} in $\sigma_v$ (km s$^{-1}$) (\(\delta = -0.12\), \(p_{\rm adj} = 0.024\)), \CC{} in $\Delta_\mathrm{BGG-cen}/ \langle R_{ij} \rangle$ (\(\delta = -0.11\), \(p_{\rm adj} = 0.028\)), and none involves \CG{}.


The spiral-fraction correlations show a broader environmental pattern rather than a clean dominated/non-dominated dichotomy. The most coherent set appears in \CC, where \(S_\mathrm{frac}\) increases with \(\left\langle R_{ij}\right\rangle\) in both dominated and non-dominated groups (\(\rho_\mathrm{S}=0.29\), \(p<10^{-4}\) and \(\rho_\mathrm{S}=0.27\), \(p<10^{-4}\)), while weaker negative trends are found with \(\sigma_v\) and \(L_\mathrm{group}\). \CB{} contributes only weaker non-dominated-group correlations. In \CG, the reported trends occur in small subsets, with \(S_\mathrm{frac}\) decreasing with \(L_\mathrm{group}\) among dominated groups (\(\rho_\mathrm{S}=-0.37\), 36 groups); we treat these as supporting diagnostics rather than the main dominance result. The full distributional diagnostics are deposited on Zenodo.

\subsection{Morphology versus Zheng--Shen class, magnitude gap, and BGG dominance}\label{app:zheng_shen}

We test whether the GZ1 E/S morphology mix is associated with three group-level descriptors: the Zheng--Shen compact-group class, the first--second magnitude gap \(\Delta m_{12}=M_{r,2}-M_{r,1}\), and the continuous BGG luminosity fraction \(f_{L,\mathrm{BGG}}=L_\mathrm{BGG}/L_\mathrm{group}\). The magnitude-gap and BGG-luminosity-fraction quantities are available for all 118 \CG{}+\RG{} groups used in this test; the Zheng--Shen class is defined only for compact groups, so the \RG{} class test is unavailable while \RG{} is retained in the \(\Delta m_{12}\) and \(f_{L,\mathrm{BGG}}\) comparisons. \subsection{Crossing time}\label{app:crossing_time}







Group radii and \(t_\mathrm{cross}\) use projected physical separations from the Planck 2015 angular-diameter distance, correcting the luminosity-distance convention used for this diagnostic in Paper~I; the values decrease by 3.2--8.7\% (median 6.7\%), without changing the reported correlations at quoted precision. The crossing-time diagnostic mainly separates systems by dynamical scale rather than by luminosity: shorter \(t_\mathrm{cross}\) systems have larger virial mass-to-light ratios and virial masses, with correlations of \(\rho_\mathrm{S}=-0.39\) (\(p<10^{-4}\)) with \(\log(\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{VT}/L_r)\) and \(\rho_\mathrm{S}=-0.33\) (\(p<10^{-4}\)) with \(\log(\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{VT})\) over 816 groups, whereas the relation with total group luminosity is formally detected but very weak (\(\rho_\mathrm{S}=0.08\)). We therefore read \(t_\mathrm{cross}\) as a dynamical compactness indicator tied to virial mass and mass-to-light ratio, with little independent leverage from \(L_\mathrm{group}\).

\subsection{Pooled adjusted models}\label{app:pooled_adjusted}

As a compact summary across the heterogeneous control definitions we also fit binomial models to the pooled catalogue. Because the control samples overlap heavily, the pooled control side is first deduplicated by SDSS objid and standard errors are clustered by physical Lim group; the all-galaxy adjustment set includes stellar mass, redshift, BGG/satellite status, quartet luminosity, and velocity dispersion. This pooled result does not replace the per-control contrasts. For quenched status, the \CG{} odds ratio is \(1.35\) (95\% CI \([0.98, 1.86]\); \(p = 0.068\)). For quenched satellite status, the \CG{} odds ratio is \(1.53\) (95\% CI \([1.10, 2.11]\); \(p = 0.011\)). For GZ1 E-class morphology, the \CG{} odds ratio is \(1.68\) (95\% CI \([1.27, 2.22]\); \(p = 3.2\times 10^{-4}\)). For GZ1 E-class satellite morphology, the \CG{} odds ratio is \(1.99\) (95\% CI \([1.47, 2.71]\); \(p<10^{-4}\)). For GZ1 E-class BGG morphology, the \CG{} odds ratio is \(0.72\) (95\% CI \([0.38, 1.37]\); \(p = 0.314\)). The pooled coefficients reinforce the satellite morphology shift; the corresponding forest plot is deposited on Zenodo. The quenched estimate changes across the separate controls and is not reproduced by the matched contrast.


\subsection{Matching details}\label{app:matching_details}
For each quartet comparison in Table~\ref{tab:group_match_per_control}, the maximum permitted difference is 0.2 times the population standard deviation of the logit of the predicted \CG{} membership probability (the propensity score; \citealt{RosenbaumRubin1983,Austin2011}) in the complete matching frame. The match is one-to-one without replacement. The pooled match selects 49 \CB{}, 2 \CC{}, and 6 \RG{} quartets from a pool with one quartet per physical Lim group.


The galaxy-level control pool of Sect.~\ref{sec:morphology} is deduplicated by SDSS objid before matching: 5828 control rows collapse to 3609 unique galaxies. The propensity model uses \(\log M_\star\), redshift, quartet luminosity rank, quartet luminosity, and \(\sigma_v\), and the match is one-to-one without replacement within exact quartet-rank strata. The 14 unmatched \CG{} galaxies have no stellar mass and leave the sample before the propensity model; every \CG{} galaxy with complete covariates is matched. Of the 234 pairs, 207 have a decisive E or S class on both sides; of the 177 satellite pairs, 152 do. The maximum absolute covariate standardised mean difference falls from 0.37 to 0.08 (Fig.~\ref{fig:matched_control_balance}). By kept label, the matched controls comprise 159 \CB{}, 57 \CC{}, 18 \RG{}; physically, 18 of the 234 matched controls are \RG{} galaxies (which also appear in \CB{} through the sample overlap), and the per-galaxy origin of every matched control is released with the derived tables. Confidence intervals and p-values come from a bootstrap blocked by the compact group of the matched \CG{} galaxy.











The 234 matched controls occupy 204 physical Lim groups, and 26 control hosts link more than one \CG{} group. Resampling the 33 connected components of the graph linking matched \CG{} groups to their control hosts instead, the E-class fraction difference remains \(+13.5\) points (95\% interval \([+4.9, +21.9]\); \(p = 0.006\)), and the quenched-fraction difference is \(+3.1\) points (\([-9.0, +12.0]\); \(p = 0.649\)). The analogous matched-size sensitivities are \(-0.019\) dex for the Simard radius (\(p = 0.310\)), \(-0.033\) dex for the Petrosian radius (\(p = 0.016\)), and \(-0.008\) for the concentration (\(p = 0.896\)).




\subsection{Selection diagnostics}\label{app:selection}

The fractions with complete measurements for the major derived quantities are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:data_availability}, including the Simard and Petrosian size availability used in Sect.~\ref{sec:other_properties} and the availability of \(D_n4000\). The median nearest-neighbour angular separation is 100.9 arcsec in \CG{} and 255.9 arcsec in the controls; the fractions below the 55-arcsec SDSS fibre-collision scale are 19.4\% and 7.7\%. No availability difference between \CG{} and the pooled controls is both larger than 10 percentage points and significant after Holm adjustment (\(p<0.05\)).






The group-scale covariates are radius scale, velocity dispersion, quartet luminosity, and BGG luminosity dominance. The tables do carry an \(M_{200c}\) estimate, but it is the Yang--Lim abundance-matching \(M_{180m}\) of the identified \emph{host} halo (converted as in Paper I, Appendix A), not a quartet property: for embedded quartets it describes the richer host while for isolated systems it describes the quartet itself, it derives from the host-group luminosity by abundance matching and is therefore nearly collinear with the quartet-luminosity covariate, and it is entangled with the compact-group selection. We therefore exclude it from the harmonised covariate set. A sensitivity fit adding \(\log M_{200c}\) to the published covariates leaves every E-class conclusion unchanged: the pooled odds ratio moves from 1.68 to 1.55 (\(p = 0.0035\)), and the per-control odds ratios become 1.89, 1.49, and 2.43 against \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{} (all \(p \le 0.007\)).


In the sensitivity fit that retains all catalogue rows, restricting the pairwise tidal index of Sect.~\ref{sec:tidal_index} to quartet co-members is innocuous: recomputing \(T_i\) with all spectroscopic Lim members of the identified host group included (positions from the group catalogue; stellar masses from MPA--JHU where available, so the host-inclusive index is itself a lower bound) shifts \(\log T_i\) by a median of \dTall~dex (sample medians \(\le \dTmaxSamp\)~dex, and identically zero for \RG{}, whose groups have exactly four members), with rank correlation \(\rhoTT\) between the two definitions, and refitting the with-index E-class model on the host-inclusive index moves the attenuated \CG{} coefficient only from \ORresid{} to \ORresidAll{}. \(T_i\) is thus the local, galaxy-scale tidal density traced by catalogued galaxies; any smooth host-halo contribution is probed separately through the host-mass sensitivity fit above and the within-host analysis of Sect.~\ref{sec:morphology}. The colour-selection mass diagnostics are deposited on Zenodo.

\paragraph{Sample-definition sensitivities.} Restoring the 16 Zheng--Shen split systems while retaining the published model definitions leaves the morphology result qualitatively unchanged: the adjusted all-galaxy E-class odds ratios against \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{} are 1.85 (95\% CI \([1.39, 2.46]\)), 1.44 (\([1.10, 1.88]\)), and 2.38 (\([1.58, 3.58]\)), and the corresponding satellite odds ratios are 2.42 (\([1.80, 3.25]\)), 1.78 (\([1.35, 2.35]\)), and 2.75 (\([1.69, 4.49]\)). Retaining the anomalous Lim 3688 quartets likewise leaves the coefficients essentially unchanged: the all-galaxy and satellite odds ratios are 2.03 and 2.48 against \CB{}, and 1.60 and 1.86 against \CC{}.









\paragraph{Crowding.} Galaxy Zoo morphology is image-based, so crowded closest pairs could bias the votes. After excluding galaxies whose nearest projected neighbour within the catalogue group lies within 55 arcsec (retaining 200 \CG{} and 2534 \CC{} galaxies), the \CG{} all-row E-class fraction \emph{rises} relative to the full-sample value of Table~\ref{tab:morphologies} (55.0\% against 41.9\% for \CC{}, all retained rows including uncertain and missing classes), and the \CG{}--\CC{} contrast remains clear. Adding the close-neighbour flag to the joint model leaves the \CG{} coefficient almost unchanged (OR \(1.66\), \(p = 6.6\times 10^{-4}\)); the flag itself (OR \(2.00\), \(p = 4.4\times 10^{-4}\)) may reflect blending or the physical association of close pairs with early types. The checks do not prove immunity to crowding, but they rule out the closest-pair artefact as the sole origin of the result.





\paragraph{Without \(\sigma_v\).} Refitting the per-control and pooled models without the \(\sigma_v\) covariate, on identical samples and with identical clustering, moves the E-class odds ratios from 1.99 to 2.06 (\CB{}), 1.60 to 1.59 (\CC{}), and 2.52 to 2.58 (\RG{}), less than their standard errors. The galaxy-level match retains the same 234 \CG{} galaxies with an E-class fraction difference of \(+14.2\) points. The per-control group-level differences become \(\Delta = +21.6\), \(+14.0\), and \(+22.2\) points against \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{} (permutation \(p = 0.0002\), \(0.011\), \(0.0013\)). The \CB{} change reflects the composition of the matched set rather than the \CG{} side: the with- and without-\(\sigma_v\) matches share only 9 of 57 control groups, the matched-control mean E-class satellite fraction changes by \(-9.4\) points while the \CG{} mean is unchanged, and without \(\sigma_v\) the matched controls are dynamically cooler than their \CG{} partners (residual \(\sigma_v\) standardised mean difference \(0.49\), medians 158 against 123 \(\kms\)), so the with-\(\sigma_v\) match remains the primary one and the two values bracket the \CB{} contrast.







\paragraph{Within-host estimators.} The conditional-logit sample of Sect.~\ref{sec:morphology} contains 803 galaxies in 54 host strata. A binomial GLM with host fixed-effect dummies and cluster-robust standard errors gives OR \(1.56\) (95\% CI \([1.00, 2.42]\); \(p = 0.049\)); we prefer the conditional logit because it eliminates the host intercepts rather than estimating many indicators from small strata. The within-host quenched odds ratio is \(0.75\) (95\% CI \([0.45, 1.25]\); \(p = 0.269\); 915 complete cases), and \(1.22\) without the radius covariate (\(p = 0.412\)). A host-BGG alignment check supports the satellite interpretation: 56 of 62 non-split \CG{} systems have the same quartet and Lim-host BGG, the 6 misaligned systems are embedded systems whose host BGG is brighter than the compact-group BGG, and all 186 quartet-rank \CG{} satellites are Lim-host satellites; restricting \CG{} to the 56 BGG-aligned systems leaves the three satellite E-class contrasts stable (against \CC{}, OR \(1.94\)).



\paragraph{DS18 classification.} The DS18 cross-match of Sect.~\ref{sec:ssfr_classification} covers 173/248 (70\%) of the \CG{} galaxies and 2263/3609 (63\%) of the deduplicated controls, i.e. the DR7-era population; it is not worse in \CG{}, but neither rate establishes representativeness. With the DS18 \(T\mathrm{Type}\leq0\) outcome and the per-control covariates, standardisation, and physical-group clustering of Sect.~\ref{sec:morphology}, the \CG{} coefficients are positive in all three contrasts (Table~\ref{tab:morph_robustness}); support is broad except that 83\% of \CG{} values lie within the \RG{} group-luminosity range, limiting that smallest comparison. We use the DS18 \(P_{\rm S0}\) to distinguish S0s from ellipticals within the matched DR7 subset with \(T\mathrm{Type}\leq0\): with the stricter \(P_{\rm S0}\geq0.8\), 28\% of the matched E-class galaxies are such S0s, and 66\% (27\%) of these S0s fall in our E (S) class. The correspondence of DS18 S0s with GZ1 E and S depends strongly on inclination (Sect.~\ref{sec:ssfr_classification}). Restricting the sample to matched DS18 early types and dividing it by control and environment leaves too few galaxies for a reliable E/S0 comparison.

Within morphology classes, the satellite-only fits give \(0.031\) dex for GZ1 E-class satellites (\(p = 0.091\)) and \(0.000\) dex for S-class satellites (\(p = 0.998\)), and the \CG{}\(\times\)morphology interaction is \(0.022\) dex (\(p = 0.491\)). These strata are secondary diagnostics of whether size carries information beyond the morphology shift.
