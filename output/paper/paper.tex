  \abstract
  {Compact groups have high projected galaxy densities, but most lie inside larger groups. Their galaxy populations must therefore be compared with those of ordinary groups as well as the field.}
  {We ask whether compact-group satellites have distinctive morphologies and how the choice of ordinary-group comparison changes the answer.}
  {We compare 62 non-split compact groups of four galaxies (\CG) with three controls drawn from ordinary groups: four-member groups, quartets of the four brightest eligible members, and projected cores comprising the brightest group galaxy and its three nearest eligible companions (\CC). We measure the Galaxy Zoo~1 E class among satellites classified E or spiral; this early-type proxy includes ellipticals and S0s. We use covariate-adjusted models and matched galaxies and quartets.}
  {\CG{} satellites have higher early-type fractions than all three full control samples after adjustment for stellar mass, redshift, and group properties. Against the full projected-core sample, the adjusted difference is \(+14.4\) percentage points. These cores are typically more extended than \CG{}. Within the common range of median projected pair separation (\(\Rmed\), 94--219~kpc), a model with separate separation slopes gives \(+4.0\) points (95\% CI \([-5.4, +13.3]\)) at 150~kpc. The intervals for the compactness-matched contrasts also span zero. This restriction excludes the most compact \CG{} systems. Quenching at fixed morphology shows no clear excess over the full controls.}
  {Compact-group satellites more often have early-type morphologies than those of ordinary groups taken as a whole. At similar projected separations, the estimated residual difference is small but remains uncertain; this comparison cannot determine whether the most compact systems retain an excess or identify a causal role for compactness.}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Given their high apparent densities and low velocity dispersions \citep{Hickson82,Hickson+92}, compact groups appear to be ideal sites for the frequent tidal encounters and mergers that alter galaxy masses, luminosities, sizes, morphologies, and star-formation rates (e.g., \citealt{Mamon92}). \citet{MendesdeOliveira&Hickson94} provided the classic report of an excess of early-type galaxies in compact groups relative to the field (building on the photometric morphologies of \citealt{HKA89} and the merger-remnant expectations of \citealt{ZepfWhitmore93}), in line with the broader morphology--density relation \citep{Dressler80}. Compact-group versus loose-group and field comparisons have since found broad differences in morphology and other galaxy properties, including the analysis of \citet{Coenda+12}; multi-wavelength studies likewise report altered infrared, UV, star-formation, and stellar-population properties \citep{Johnson+07,Tzanavaris+10,Bitsakis+11}, and H\,{\sc i} deficiency has been organised into an evolutionary sequence \citep{VerdesMontenegro+01}.

The interpretation of such contrasts is nevertheless ambiguous. Projected selection can include chance alignments, and many Hickson-like systems need not be isolated bound entities: apparent compact groups may be transient alignments, dense substructures, or cores embedded in looser groups and richer haloes \citep{Mamon86,DiazGimenez&Mamon10,DiazGimenez+20,Zheng&Shen21,Taverna+23}. The relevant observational question is therefore not only whether compact-group galaxies differ from field galaxies, but whether they remain different from galaxies occupying regular-group analogues selected with comparable luminosity, redshift, and membership constraints. Recent structural studies find evidence for transformation in compact groups, especially non-isolated systems \citep{Montaguth2023,Montaguth2025,Montaguth2026MassSize,Montaguth2026NonIsolated}; \citet{Montaguth2026NonIsolated} compared non-isolated compact-group galaxies with surrounding host populations, but not with ordinary-group quartets reproducing compact-group membership, luminosity-ranking, or projected-core selection.

In our previous work \citep{Tricottet+25}, we found that, once the direct consequences of the compact-group selection are accounted for, compact groups of four galaxies have group-scale properties broadly similar to those of regular groups of four galaxies and to the eligible cores of regular groups. Here we ask how the galaxy-population contrast changes across three ordinary-group comparison samples: genuine four-member groups (\RG{}), the four brightest eligible members of richer groups (\CB{}), and BGG-centred projected cores (\CC{}), the closest analogue of a compact configuration. By separating satellites from BGGs and comparing both full samples and quartets of similar projected separation, we test whether the morphological signature extends beyond the dense cores of ordinary groups. Section~\ref{sec:data} describes the samples, the classifications, and the statistical approach; Sect.~\ref{sec:results} presents the results; Sect.~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses their physical interpretation; and Sect.~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Data and classifications}\label{sec:data}
\subsection{Samples}\label{sec:samples}
We use the compact-group and control samples constructed in our previous study \citep{Tricottet+25}. Galaxy properties are taken from the SDSS DR12 catalogue of \citet{Tempel+17}, while regular groups are drawn from the SDSS DR7 group catalogue of \citet{Lim+17}; the two catalogues were matched through SDSS object identifiers to define the Lim--Tempel working catalogue. Compact groups are selected from the modified Hickson compact-group catalogue of \citet{DiazGimenez+18}: reliable HMCGs with exactly four members, all present in the Lim--Tempel catalogue, satisfying the volume and BGG-luminosity limits $0.005 \le z_{\rm BGG} \le 0.0452$ and $M_{r,\rm BGG}\leq -21.81$. The original HMCG membership uses the Hickson three-magnitude concordance in apparent $r$ magnitude, whereas the Lim-based control eligibility below uses the catalogue absolute magnitude; these conventions are not identical (retained CGs 173, 229, and 354 span 3.070, 3.063, and 3.182 mag in absolute $r$). In contrast to our previous study, we remove CGs classified as \emph{split} following \citet{Zheng&Shen21}, which reduces the compact-group sample from 78 to 62 groups. Following \citet{Zheng&Shen21}, the remaining systems are \emph{isolated} when the four members constitute their whole Lim--Tempel group, \emph{predominant} when they contribute at least half of the host-group $r$-band luminosity, and \emph{embedded} otherwise (\nGrIso, \nGrPre, and \nGrEmb{} systems).
\footnote{The embedded and predominant assignments were interchanged in parts of \citet{Tricottet+25}, including Sect.~3.3. We correct the inherited catalogue labels to the definitions adopted here; isolated systems are unaffected.}

The three control samples are built from Lim--Tempel groups satisfying the same redshift and BGG-luminosity limits, with at least four members and at least three satellites within 3 absolute magnitudes of the BGG, and serve different purposes (Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic}). \RG{} contains regular groups with exactly four members and is the most direct equal-membership comparison. \CB{} contains the four brightest eligible members of each regular group and tests whether the \CG{} population resembles the luminous core of an ordinary group. \CC{} contains the BGG and its three closest projected companions among members within 3 magnitudes of the BGG, reconstructed here from the eligible parent groups by applying the magnitude criterion before ranking companions by projected distance. Selecting the nearest companions makes \CC{} a projected-core sample, without imposing the surface-density requirement of the compact-group catalogue. A candidate control quartet is excluded when it contains a \CG{} galaxy, so no galaxy appears on both sides of a comparison; this does not exclude every other quartet drawn from a host that contains a different \CG{} member. We also remove the systems associated with Lim group 3688, whose single outlying-redshift member ($z=0.048$, against $0.032$--$0.034$ for the other six) enters both the \CB{} and \CC{} quartets and inflates their velocity dispersions to $\simeq 2000 \kms$. Table~\ref{tab:samples} summarises the final samples; the \CB{} and \CC{} group counts differ because the luminosity-ranked and distance-ranked definitions retain different Lim groups after the compact-group exclusion.

We use SDSS DR16 \citep{Ahumada+20} to retrieve stellar masses, star formation rates \citep[SFRs;][]{Brinchmann+04,Kauffmann+03b}, emission-line measurements, and Galaxy Zoo morphologies \citep{Lintott+08,GalaxyZoo1}; Appendix~\ref{app:sdss_query} lists the tables and joins. Throughout, `SDSS non-AGN reference' denotes the AGN-filtered reference sample used for calibration and orientation: spectroscopic galaxies with \(0.005 \le z \le 0.0452\), extinction-corrected Petrosian \(r \le 17.77\), a valid MPA-JHU stellar mass, and finite Galaxy Zoo vote fractions after the AGN filter. It is a field-dominated reference population, not a control sample. Projected physical separations are derived from angular-diameter distances in the adopted Planck 2015 cosmology \citep{Planck15}, as in Paper~I; all distances quoted below are in kpc.

\subsection{Classifications}\label{sec:ssfr_classification}
Star-formation classes are based on SDSS total specific star-formation rates. The inherited DR12 group catalogues store \(\log\mathrm{sSFR}=\texttt{sfr\_tot\_p50}-\texttt{lgm\_tot\_p50}\). In the MPA-JHU catalogues, \(-9999\) is the missing-value sentinel of the \texttt{galSpecExtra} table \citep{Stoughton02,Brinchmann+04,Kauffmann+03b}, so rows with unavailable components are treated as \emph{missing data}: they are excluded from the classification and from every sSFR-based figure and fraction, and are reported as counts only (Table~\ref{tab:sSFR_status}). All quenched and star-forming fractions are computed among classified galaxies. The missing-sSFR incidence is higher in \CG{} (7\% of members, 10\% of BGGs) than in the reference sample (1\%). Of the 394 missing rows, 332 are BOSS-instrument spectra outside the legacy-only MPA-JHU coverage, 60 are legacy spectra whose SFR fit returned the sentinel, and 2 have no spectrum identifier (Appendix~\ref{app:sdss_query}). Catalogue coverage and failed fits explain the unavailable values, but do not establish that availability is physically independent of star formation. Galaxies with a measured sSFR are split into quenched and star-forming classes with a two-component Gaussian mixture model (GMM) in the \(\log M_\star\)--\(\log\mathrm{sSFR}\) plane; the boundary and the AGN handling are given in Appendix~\ref{app:ssfr_method} (Fig.~\ref{fig:sSFR_classification}).

Morphologies are drawn from Galaxy Zoo 1 \citep{Lintott+08,GalaxyZoo1}, whose debiased catalogue vote fractions \citep{Bamford+09} are \texttt{p\_el\_debiased}, labelled ``elliptical'' in GZ1, and \texttt{p\_cs\_debiased}, labelled combined spiral. We define the GZ1 E class by \(\texttt{p\_el\_debiased}>0.5\), the S class by \(\texttt{p\_cs\_debiased}>0.5\), and the uncertain class when both vote fractions are finite but neither threshold is exceeded; rows with a missing vote fraction are treated as missing Galaxy Zoo data. GZ1 does not separate E from S0, so throughout this paper the E class is interpreted as a smooth early-type-like class, not as a physical sample of classical ellipticals. A cross-match with the deep-learning morphologies of \citet{DominguezSanchez2018} (DS18; 2436 of 3857 unique galaxies) quantifies this: of the 990 matched E-class galaxies, 92\% have DS18 \(T\mathrm{Type}\leq0\), 54\% are early with \(P_{\rm S0}>0.5\), and 28\% with \(P_{\rm S0}\geq0.8\); conversely, DS18 early types with \(P_{\rm S0}>0.5\) (\(N = 730\)) fall 73\% in our E class and 20\% in S (66\% and 27\% for \(P_{\rm S0}\geq0.8\)), with the E share dropping from 81\% for face-on to 48\% for inclined systems (DS18 \(P_{\rm edge\text{-}on}<0.1\) versus \(\geq0.5\)), as expected if inclined S0s expose their discs.


The DR16 \texttt{zooSpec} join returns no votes for part of the sample. Since GZ1 is keyed to DR7 objects, we recover votes through the SkyServer DR7 cross-match only when the DR7 identifier is unique among the DR16 objects and the positions agree within 1.5 arcsec. This recovers 17 \CG{} galaxies; the classifications agree wherever both sources classify a galaxy. Finite votes below both thresholds remain uncertain, while votes missing from both sources remain missing. Existing DR16 classes without a verified DR7 counterpart are retained separately (Table~\ref{tab:morphologies}). We also retrieve the age-sensitive \(D_n4000\) index \citep{Kauffmann+03b} from the stored group-galaxy spectra for descriptive use (Sect.~\ref{sec:other_properties}, Appendix~\ref{app:selection}).





\subsection{Galaxy sizes}\label{sec:size_data}

Our primary size measure is the circular effective (half-light) radius of the pure S\'ersic fit in the $r$ band, \(R_{e,r}\), from the structural catalogue of \citet{Simard2011}: PSF-convolved GIM2D fits with re-derived sky levels and deblending designed for crowded fields, so the sizes are corrected for seeing. The catalogue is keyed to SDSS DR7 identifiers; we bridge our DR16 objects to their DR7 counterparts through the SkyServer \texttt{SpecDR7} cross-match table. Rows whose Simard redshift differs from the catalogue redshift by more than 0.005 are treated as wrong matches and dropped (9 rows). The Simard sizes, tabulated in kpc under the catalogue's own cosmology, are converted back to angular sizes with the catalogue plate scale and then to kpc with the same Planck 2015 conversion as every other projected quantity.

As a secondary, model-independent measure we use the SDSS Petrosian radii \texttt{petroR50\_r} and \texttt{petroR90\_r}, which also define the concentration index \(C = R_{90,r}/R_{50,r}\) and provide the like-for-like bridge to \citet{Coenda+12}. Petrosian radii are not corrected for seeing, and the Petrosian aperture misses roughly 20\% of the flux of an \(n=4\) profile, which biases \(R_{50,r}\) low for concentrated galaxies \citep{Blanton2001,Graham2005}. All sizes are required to lie between 0.1 and 50 kpc after re-conversion; S\'ersic fits pegged at the GIM2D bounds (\(n_g \le 0.55\) or \(n_g \ge 7.9\); 60 galaxies) are excluded from the primary sample, a cut that removes 2.5\% of the Simard-matched \CG{} members against 1.0--1.5\% in the controls and is therefore conservative with respect to a \CG{} early-type excess; Petrosian entries must satisfy \(R_{50,r}>0\), \(R_{90,r}>R_{50,r}\), and a finite positive uncertainty. The completeness of both measures is given in Appendix~\ref{app:selection}.


\subsection{Statistical approach}\label{sec:stats}

Our main question is whether compact-group galaxies retain distinct properties once differences in stellar mass, redshift, galaxy rank, and group properties are taken into account. The three controls address physically different comparisons, so we compare \CG{} with each one separately. Galaxy-level logistic models quantify the adjusted E-versus-S and quenched-versus-star-forming contrasts; group-level matching compares the satellite mix of whole quartets; and within-host models compare compact-group members with their non-compact co-members. The covariates are stellar mass, redshift, rank, group luminosity, and the quartet velocity dispersion \(\sigma_v\); because \(\sigma_v\) from four tracers is noisy, every analysis that uses it is repeated without it (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness_details}).

We account for dependence among galaxies in the same group by clustering standard errors at the level of the \emph{physical} Lim group and resampling by physical group or matched pair, as appropriate. We report two-sided p-values with effect sizes and confidence intervals for the primary morphology, star-formation, matching, within-host, and size analyses, and reserve multiple-testing correction for the secondary diagnostics. The p-value conventions, the completeness requirement for the control variables, the overlap coefficient, and the treatment of the overlapping control samples in pooled summaries are collected in Appendix~\ref{app:stat_conventions}.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Morphology}\label{sec:morphology}

\CG{} galaxies are more often in the GZ1 E class than the galaxies of every control. Tables~\ref{tab:sSFR_status} and \ref{tab:morphologies} give the raw class counts; among galaxies with a usable E or S classification, the \CG{} E-class fraction is 58\% against 47\%, 48\%, and 33\% in \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{} (Fisher exact tests: \(p = 2.2\times 10^{-3}\), \(p = 4.3\times 10^{-3}\), and \(p<10^{-4}\)). The 22 \CG{} rows with uncertain or missing Galaxy Zoo classifications are excluded rather than assigned to either class. Satellites account for most of the difference: among usable BGG classifications the E-class fraction is 66.1\% in \CG{} and 46.9\% in \RG{} (Fisher \(p\) = 0.88, 0.88, and 0.07 against \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{}), and restricted to star-forming galaxies the E/S mix is compatible with every control (two-sided Fisher exact test: \(p = 0.372\), \(p = 0.607\), \(p = 0.374\)), so star-forming E-class galaxies do not produce the raw excess.

Within their own host groups, the \CG{} membership coefficient is sensitive to how radial position is adjusted. For the \nGrEmb{} embedded and \nGrPre{} predominant systems, whose quartets live inside richer Lim groups (54 distinct hosts; 958 member galaxies, 224 of them in a \CG{}), we compare the \CG{} members with the non-\CG{} members of the same host by conditional logistic regression stratified by host, which removes every host-level property by design, with stellar mass, host luminosity rank, and the projected radius from the Lim BGG of the host as covariates. The E-class odds ratio for \CG{} membership is \(1.46\) (95\% CI \([0.92, 2.33]\); \(p = 0.112\); 803 galaxies in 54 host groups containing both \CG{} and non-\CG{} members), and rises to \(1.89\) (\([1.22, 2.93]\); \(p = 0.004\)) when the radius is omitted. After the stellar-mass and E/S requirements, only 6 hosts offer radial overlap between member and non-member satellites. The radius-adjusted coefficient thus relies largely on comparisons across separate radial ranges. A logistic model with host-group fixed effects gives a positive association as well (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness_details}).


\subsection{Star formation at fixed morphology}\label{sec:star_formation}

The star-formation contrast is weaker and control-dependent (Table~\ref{tab:adjusted_models}). It is clearest against \RG{} in the raw data, but the adjusted and matched results do not support a control-independent excess of quenching; assigning all missing-sSFR galaxies to either extreme class does not erase the raw \RG{} contrast (Appendix~\ref{app:ssfr_method}).





Morphology accounts for part of the satellite-quenching contrast, and no clear association remains once it is fixed. In direct satellite-only models that add the binary GZ1 E/S indicator to stellar mass, redshift, group luminosity, and velocity dispersion, the \CG{} odds ratios are \(1.56\) (95\% CI \([0.99, 2.48]\); \(p = 0.057\)), \(1.12\) (\([0.74, 1.70]\); \(p = 0.591\)), and \(1.14\) (\([0.55, 2.36]\); \(p = 0.731\)) against \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{}. The 95\% interval for the largest estimate, against \CB{}, includes unity, and the other two estimates lie close to unity. On the same \CB{} comparison sample with usable morphologies, omitting the morphological term raises the odds ratio from \(1.56\) to \(2.31\), showing that the attenuation also occurs when the fitted population is held fixed.

The descriptive conditional fractions are \(P(\mathrm{Q}\,|\,\mathrm{E}) = \qEcg\), \qEcb, \qEcc, and \qErg{} for \CG{}, \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{} (Table~\ref{tab:quenched_by_morphology}). Among galaxies with both a morphology class and an sSFR classification, we decompose each total \CG{}-minus-control quenched-fraction difference algebraically into a term for the different morphological mix and a conditional term for quenching differences within morphological classes (Appendix~\ref{app:ssfr_method}, Table~\ref{tab:quenching_decomposition}). The 95\% intervals for the conditional terms, obtained by resampling physical hosts, are \([\condCBlo, \condCBhi]\), \([\condCClo, \condCChi]\), and \([\condRGlo, \condRGhi]\). All include zero and still allow differences of several percentage points. The individually matched quenched-fraction difference is \(0.03\) (95\% CI \([-0.06, 0.12]\); \(p = 0.526\)). Using the fixed threshold $\log_{10}(\mathrm{sSFR}/\mathrm{yr}^{-1})=-11.0$, the \CG{} and \RG{} satellite star-forming fractions are 41.4\% and 60.2\% (difference \(-18.8\) points; \(p = 5.3\times 10^{-4}\)).







No robust \CG{} displacement of the star-forming main sequence is established: the unmatched median offsets are comparison-dependent, while the matched star-forming pairs give a small mean difference whose interval includes zero. The sequence fits are described in Appendix~\ref{app:baseline_details}; the cumulative distributions and full numerical comparison are retained in the online supplement. The quenched-sequence residuals are also shown there, but are weakly informative because MPA-JHU sSFRs of quenched galaxies are largely \(D_n4000\)-calibrated.

The early-type-like excess is concentrated among the compact groups that live inside richer hosts. \subsection{Local projected density}\label{sec:tidal_index}

Including local projected density attenuates the \CG{} coefficient, but the amount is not well determined. For each of the 6076 catalogue rows we compute \(T_i = \sum_j M_{\star,j}/R_{ij}^3\) over selected quartet co-members (\(R_{ij}\) in kpc; unmeasured companion masses contribute zero, making \(T_i\) a lower bound). For the pooled sensitivity fit, controls are deduplicated by objid as in Appendix~\ref{app:pooled_models}, retaining the quartet assignment in the priority order \RG{}, \CB{}, \CC{}. The E-class model adjusts for stellar mass and satellite status, with physical-group clustering. With the index, the \CG{} odds ratio is 1.19 (95\% CI \([0.89, 1.60]\); \(p = 0.24\); 3416 galaxies). Retaining all catalogue rows instead gives an attenuation from 1.83 to 1.30 (\(p = 0.083\)), with an index odds ratio of 1.43 per \(\sdlogT\)-dex standard deviation (\(p<10^{-4}\)); this check weights multiply selected controls more heavily. In this sample, the \(\log T_i\) distributions have an overlap coefficient of 0.54 and their medians differ by 1.1 dex. Restricting the fit to their overlapping range gives an odds ratio of 1.14 (\(p = 0.38\)). Because \(T_i\) depends on the same projected configuration used to select compact groups, adjustment for the index also accounts for some of that selection. The attenuation alone leaves the physical role of local density in the morphological difference unresolved. The index traces projected neighbours, not tidal history; the host-inclusive check is given in Appendix~\ref{app:selection}.


\subsection{Other galaxy properties}\label{sec:other_properties}





None of the other galaxy properties shows an offset as robust as the morphology excess (Fig.~\ref{fig:property_trends}). Strong \(\mathrm{H}\alpha\) emission (\(\mathrm{H}\alpha\,\mathrm{EW} \leq -3\,\text{\AA}\), emission negative) is rarer in \CG{}: 70 of 217 members with measurements (32\%), against 41\%, 43\%, and 57\% in \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{} (Fisher \(p = 0.012\), \(p = 0.002\), \(p<10^{-4}\)). The deficit is not fully explained by the different morphology mix: after adjustment for stellar mass and GZ1 E/S class, the \CG{} odds ratios for strong \(\mathrm{H}\alpha\) emission are 0.55, 0.65, and 0.56 against \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{}, respectively (\(p = 0.002\), \(p = 0.027\), and \(p = 0.072\)), with the first two contrasts statistically clear.

The morphology excess does not depend on the vote threshold, on the classifier, or on the crowded closest pairs (Table~\ref{tab:morph_robustness}). \section{Interpretation and comparison with previous work}\label{sec:discussion}




\subsection{An early-type-like satellite excess linked to dense group cores}\label{sec:morph_local_density}

The morphology signature lies primarily in the satellites. Its persistence after stellar-mass adjustment indicates differences beyond the present-day mass distributions, although mass itself may record earlier environmental evolution. BGGs offer less contrast: the luminous-BGG selection places them at high masses where most are already quenched (Table~\ref{tab:sSFR_status}).

\citet{Martinez+13} compared compact and loose groups of similar total luminosity. Compact-group BGGs were brighter and more often Galaxy Zoo ellipticals overall, but at fixed BGG magnitude the elliptical fractions were comparable within errors, whereas colour- and concentration-based early-type fractions remained higher in compact groups. Our weak adjusted BGG contrast resembles their fixed-magnitude Galaxy Zoo result; their colour and concentration comparisons show how much depends on the early-type definition.

The 46 exact host-core identities among 56 non-isolated \CG{} systems explain why \CC{} is a useful comparator: many compact groups are themselves the BGG and its three nearest eligible companions (Appendix~\ref{app:cg_core_overlap}). The remaining ordinary-group cores span larger separations. Comparing the full populations therefore combines their different compactness distributions with any differences at a given separation. The estimated contrasts are smaller within the shared range, and the intervals admit either sign for the residual association. The most compact \CG{} systems fall outside this comparison.

At fixed stellar mass, smaller projected separations accompany higher E-class probabilities within \CG{}, \CB{}, and \CC{} (Table~\ref{tab:battery}, Fig.~\ref{fig:compactness}). Pre-processing in the host halo may build up an early-type satellite population before the compact configuration is observed. Radial segregation could raise the fraction of these satellites in dense cores, where dynamical friction favours the inward migration of more massive satellites and earlier arrivals have had more time to orbit and interact. Repeated tidal encounters, galaxy harassment, and minor mergers may heat or restructure discs and contribute to the S0 population, consistent with the structural trends reported in S-PLUS \citep{Montaguth2023,Montaguth2025,Montaguth2026MassSize,Montaguth2026NonIsolated}.

As discussed in Sect.~5.2 of \citet{Tricottet+25}, chance alignments of host members along the line of sight can give a small projected separation even for galaxies far apart in three dimensions. Their inclusion can dilute a signal produced by close encounters, while their shared host can still contribute a morphological excess. The \nGrIso{} isolated systems have lower satellite E-class fractions than the other \CG{} classes, with too few groups to identify a distinct evolutionary stage (Sect.~\ref{sec:cg_classes}).

The shared-host models address location inside the same halo. Because selected compact-group members and eligible non-members usually occupy different radial ranges, radius adjustment changes the inferred association without providing many direct comparisons at fixed radius (Sect.~\ref{sec:morphology}). \citet{Montaguth2026NonIsolated} examined early-type and quenched fractions with host-centric position and density using different samples and morphology definitions. Our explicit quartet controls complement that approach by showing how the comparison itself changes the inferred signature.

\subsection{GZ1 E-class morphologies and transition galaxies}\label{sec:s0_degeneracy}

Our E class is a smooth early-type-like proxy with a substantial S0 contribution (Sect.~\ref{sec:ssfr_classification}). The DS18 cross-match covers 63--70\% of the sample, and \(P_{\rm S0}\) distinguishes S0s from ellipticals only for matched DR7 galaxies with \(T\mathrm{Type}\leq0\). Within that early-type subset, the GZ1 classification of S0s depends strongly on inclination, and splitting the sample by control and environment leaves too few galaxies to estimate the elliptical and S0 contributions reliably (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness_details}). This distinction matters when comparing with the S-PLUS studies of compact-group transition galaxies and mass--size trends \citep{Montaguth2023,Montaguth2025,Montaguth2026MassSize,Montaguth2026NonIsolated}: those studies use hybrid structural--colour classifications, identify transition populations, and adopt their own size definitions, whereas we use GZ1 E/S classes and seeing-corrected Simard radii. Their result therefore does not predict a universal size deficit for every \CG{} GZ1 E-class galaxy. Our three control samples instead test which apparent \CG{} signatures survive across genuine four-member, luminosity-selected, and BGG-centred projected-core quartets.

\subsection{Why the star-formation and size signals are weaker}\label{sec:weaker_signals}





Morphology accounts for part of the full-sample quenching contrast, and the residual at fixed morphology is poorly constrained (Sect.~\ref{sec:star_formation}). A clear morphological excess with a weak residual quenching signal is compatible with earlier processing in the host. If disc transformation and the decline in star formation occurred on overlapping timescales, the present-day fractions can retain a shift in the mix of morphological classes even when the quenching fractions within each class are similar. The SDSS diagnostics measure the present-day populations, so constraining the relative timescales of morphological transformation and quenching would require detailed spectroscopic reconstructions of individual star-formation histories.

Fibre H\(\alpha\) equivalent width measures emission relative to the central stellar continuum, with possible contributions from AGN, shocks, and old stars. MPA-JHU total sSFRs use photometric aperture corrections and statistical estimates for weak-line systems \citep{Brinchmann+04,Salim+07}. In the intermediate-mass bin at similar separation, excluding AGN and composites weakens the H\(\alpha\) deficit and reduces the sample. Both separation models give total-sSFR quenched contrasts with intervals spanning zero (Sect.~\ref{sec:other_properties}). Taken together, these diagnostics provide no clear evidence for centrally concentrated quenching. Spectroscopic incompleteness also matters: 19\% of catalogued \CG{} galaxies have a neighbour below the 55-arcsec fibre-collision scale, against 8\% of pooled controls (10\% in \CC{}). Missing companions could hide brief episodes of enhanced or recently suppressed star formation and bias the tidal index downward. Aperture corrections may also be less secure for interacting or blended members, adding uncertainty to the comparison-dependent sSFR and main-sequence estimates.






The size comparison is sensitive to methodology. \citet{Coenda+12} reported a tendency towards smaller compact-group galaxies at fixed luminosity with Petrosian radii within concentration-selected morphological subsamples, although their more restrictive \(C>3\) selection showed no clear environmental trend. Our seeing-corrected Simard radii give no clear fixed-mass offset, with a 95\% interval still permitting a deficit of roughly \(0.04\) dex. The matched Simard and Petrosian differences also use unequal available-pair subsets (Sect.~\ref{sec:other_properties}). The larger Simard-minus-Petrosian offsets for E-class galaxies (Appendix~\ref{app:size_details}) make morphology mix relevant, without measuring its contribution to the environmental contrast. Differences between studies can reflect profile-dependent size estimates, sample selection, adjustment for luminosity or mass, and structural classification.


The optical SDSS comparisons locate the strongest morphological signal among satellites in non-isolated dense projected cores. Projection and the limited radial overlap within hosts leave the relative contributions of earlier pre-processing, radial segregation, and interactions in the core unresolved.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We compare low-redshift, luminous, non-split compact groups of four galaxies with three controls drawn from ordinary groups: groups with exactly four members, quartets of the four brightest eligible members, and BGG-centred projected cores. Among E/S-classified satellites, compact groups show a clear excess of early-type morphologies over all three full control samples after adjustment for stellar mass, redshift, and group properties. At similar median projected pair separation, the estimated difference from ordinary-group cores is small, but the intervals still allow a residual excess or deficit. This restricted comparison excludes the most compact groups and cannot determine their residual signature or a causal role for compactness.

Morphology accounts for part of the full-sample quenching contrast, with no clear residual association at fixed morphology. The intermediate-mass fibre H\(\alpha\) deficit weakens after excluding AGN and composites. At similar separation, both fitted models of total-sSFR quenching have intervals that include zero. The morphological signature is consistent with several environmental histories, whose relative contributions remain unresolved.

\section{sSFR classification details}\label{app:ssfr_method}

We identify BPT-classifiable galaxies by requiring measured values of both emission-line ratios
\[
\log_{10}\!\bigl([\mathrm{N\,II}]\lambda6584/\mathrm{H}\alpha\bigr)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\log_{10}\!\bigl([\mathrm{O\,III}]\lambda5007/\mathrm{H}\beta\bigr).
\]
Among these galaxies, we flag AGN-like systems if they satisfy either
\[
\log_{10}\frac{[\mathrm{N\,II}]}{\mathrm{H}\alpha} > 0,
\]
or if they lie above the empirical demarcation of \citet{Kauffmann+03a}:
\[
\log_{10}\frac{[\mathrm{O\,III}]}{\mathrm{H}\beta}
> \frac{0.61}{\log_{10}([\mathrm{N\,II}]/\mathrm{H}\alpha)-0.05} \;+\; 1.3,
\]
in which case they are excluded from the GMM calibration sample. This calibration flag is deliberately more inclusive than the three-class BPT scheme of Sect.~\ref{sec:other_properties}, in which composite galaxies lie between the \citet{Kauffmann+03a} and \citet{Kewley+01} demarcations and only galaxies above the \citet{Kewley+01} line are labelled AGN-like, because its sole purpose is to keep possible AGN contamination out of the sSFR calibration. Galaxies with missing diagnostic line ratios are BPT-unclassified, not proven non-AGN; they are retained for the sSFR status assignment when valid SDSS stellar mass and sSFR estimates are available, while the AGN-like analysis treats them separately.
Reference-sample rows with unavailable \texttt{specsfr\_tot\_p50}, and group-sample rows with unavailable \texttt{sfr\_tot\_p50} or \texttt{lgm\_tot\_p50}, including the \(-9999\) sentinel, have no valid sSFR estimate; they are treated as missing data throughout: excluded from the GMM calibration where applicable, never assigned an sSFR class, omitted from every sSFR figure, and reported as counts only (Sect.~\ref{sec:ssfr_classification}).
Galaxies with a measured sSFR are divided into quenched and star-forming classes with a two-component Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) in the \(\log M_\star\)-\(\log\mathrm{sSFR}\) plane \citep{McLachlanPeel2000}; the boundary is the locus where the two component posterior probabilities are equal, and the resulting classification for the SDSS non-AGN reference is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:sSFR_classification}. A fixed-threshold check confirms that the qualitative hierarchy is not tied to the exact GMM split (Sect.~\ref{sec:star_formation}).

\section{Statistical conventions}\label{app:stat_conventions}

\paragraph{Independence unit.} The unit for every inferential contrast among the group catalogues is the \emph{physical} Lim group: standard errors are clustered by Lim group (unifying the overlapping control definitions, with \CG{} systems assigned to their host Lim group, or to their own identifier when they have no Lim counterpart). The decomposition resamples physical hosts jointly across comparison sides, and matched-quartet inference resamples connected components of the bipartite graph linking matched \CG{} quartets to their control hosts. Pooled models across the three controls are secondary summaries computed after deduplicating the overlapping control samples to one row per physical galaxy, with the kept label following the priority \RG{} \(>\) \CB{} \(>\) \CC{} (Appendix~\ref{app:pooled_models}). Purely descriptive reference-sample diagnostics report their own unit where they appear.

\paragraph{Tests and p-values.} Descriptive comparisons use two-sided Fisher exact tests for \(2\times2\) tables and rank-sum or bootstrap comparisons for distributions and median differences; Spearman and Pearson coefficients are diagnostics of monotonic or linear trends only. Paired sign-flip permutation p-values use add-one tail counts, \(p = (k+1)/(B+1)\) with \(k\) the number of permutations at least as extreme as the observation; bootstrap sign-crossing p-values are \(2\min[(k_{\le 0}+1)/(B+1),(k_{\ge 0}+1)/(B+1)]\), capped at unity, where \(k_{\le 0}\) and \(k_{\ge 0}\) count resampled differences on either side of zero; displayed p-values are floored at \(10^{-4}\). Adjusted odds ratios come from binomial generalised linear models with cluster-robust covariance; continuous covariates are standardised, so coefficients of continuous terms are per standard deviation of the fitted frame. Multiple-testing correction is reserved for exploratory tests. We apply Holm adjustment to the colour, measurement-availability, and magnitude-gap tests and Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate control to the dominance, morphology--environment, and other group-scale tests.


\paragraph{Covariates.} The velocity dispersion \(\sigma_v\) that enters models and matching is the quartet-level gapper estimate \citep{Wainer&Thissen76} on the four selected members' redshifts, converted as \(c\,\sigma_z/(1+\bar z)\), for \emph{all} samples, including \CB{} and \CC{} quartets embedded in richer hosts (this is how the spurious Lim 3688 quartets of Sect.~\ref{sec:samples} reach \({\simeq}2000\kms\)). With four tracers the estimator is intrinsically noisy: in a Gaussian Monte Carlo of \(10^5\) quartets, \(\sigma_{\rm gapper}/\sigma_{\rm true}\) has median 0.96 with a 16--84\% range of 0.57--1.42. A candidate covariate enters an adjusted model when it is non-missing for at least 65\% of that model's frame (matching variables require 70\%); in practice \(\sigma_v\), group luminosity, stellar mass, redshift, and rank are complete for every quartet and always enter, and no harmonised group-mass covariate is available (Appendix~\ref{app:selection}).


\paragraph{Overlap coefficient.} For the \(\log T_i\) distributions in Sect.~\ref{sec:tidal_index}, the overlap coefficient is the integral of the minimum of the two normalised histograms on common bins, between 0 (disjoint) and 1 (identical).

\paragraph{Morphology and pair separation.} The fixed-mass points of Fig.~\ref{fig:compactness} use bin intercepts and a common stellar-mass slope within each sample. Points lie at the median satellite \(\log\Rmed\) in each bin, and bins with fewer than three hosts are omitted. The outermost \CC{} bin has 10 classified satellites in 4 hosts. Its rise over the preceding bin is \(+20.9\) points (95\% CI \([+5.8, +36.1]\)) and is based on few hosts. For panel (b) we restrict both samples to within 0.1~dex of the evaluation separation and require a counterpart within tolerances of 0.006 in redshift, 0.25~dex in stellar mass, 0.35~dex in quartet luminosity, and \(120\kms\) in velocity dispersion. We evaluate both predicted probabilities at the same separation using the observed covariates of the resulting \CG{} satellites. At least three physical hosts per sample are required.

\section{Main-sequence and quenched-sequence details}\label{app:baseline_details}

We model the star-forming main sequence as a function of stellar mass with polynomials of order 1--4 fitted to the star-forming galaxies of the SDSS non-AGN reference sample; the diagnostic is the residual RMS scatter, not a formal reduced \(\chi^2\), because no per-galaxy uncertainty model is used. We adopt order 2, the lowest order with the smallest five-fold cross-validated RMS (0.305 dex, against 0.306 dex for order 1), and compute the residual of each star-forming galaxy with respect to it; a positive residual means a higher sSFR at fixed stellar mass than the reference relation. In Sect.~\ref{sec:star_formation}, \(\Delta\) is the \CG{}-minus-control median residual with the central 68\% group-bootstrap interval on the median difference and the two-sided sign-crossing probability (10\,000 resamples). The quenched sequence shown in the online supplement is obtained with the same procedure on the 7705 quenched galaxies of the reference sample: order 4 has the smallest cross-validated RMS (0.331 dex), and the residuals are attached to the quenched galaxies of the four samples.


Within \CG, the star-forming main-sequence offsets of the embedded, isolated, and predominant classes are statistically consistent with one another.

\subsection{Dominance}\label{app:dominance}









We split groups into dominated systems, where the BGG contributes more than 60\% of the group luminosity, and non-dominated systems. Because this appendix compares many group properties, \(p_\mathrm{adj}\) denotes the false-discovery-rate adjustment of Appendix~\ref{app:stat_conventions}; Cliff's \(\delta\) is the rank-based effect size. The split does not produce a compact-group signal by itself: across the control samples, the clearest binary separation is in group luminosity, dominated systems having lower \(L_\mathrm{group}\) than their non-dominated counterparts (Control$_{4B}$: $9.1\times 10^{10}$ versus $1.28\times 10^{11}$ $L_\odot$, \(p_\mathrm{adj}<10^{-4}\), \(\delta=-0.55\); Control$_{4C}$: $9.66\times 10^{10}$ versus $1.07\times 10^{11}$ $L_\odot$, \(p_\mathrm{adj}<10^{-4}\), \(\delta=-0.25\); RG$_4$: $7.09\times 10^{10}$ versus $9.82\times 10^{10}$ $L_\odot$, \(p_\mathrm{adj}=1\times 10^{-4}\), \(\delta=-0.59\)), the negative \(\delta\) meaning that dominated groups occupy the lower-luminosity side of each comparison.




Splitting instead at each sample's median BGG luminosity fraction (\(f_{L,\rm BGG} = 0.63\), 0.52, 0.61, and 0.59 for \CG{}, \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{}) gives the same picture: the FDR-significant separations are \CB{} in  $L_\mathrm{group}$ ($L_\odot$) (\(\delta = -0.53\), \(p_{\rm adj} = 0.000\)), \CC{} in  $L_\mathrm{group}$ ($L_\odot$) (\(\delta = -0.24\), \(p_{\rm adj} = 0.000\)), \RG{} in  $L_\mathrm{group}$ ($L_\odot$) (\(\delta = -0.58\), \(p_{\rm adj} = 0.000\)), \CB{} in $\Delta_\mathrm{BGG-cen}/ \langle R_{ij} \rangle$ (\(\delta = -0.17\), \(p_{\rm adj} = 0.000\)), \CB{} in $\sigma_v$ (km s$^{-1}$) (\(\delta = -0.12\), \(p_{\rm adj} = 0.024\)), \CC{} in $\Delta_\mathrm{BGG-cen}/ \langle R_{ij} \rangle$ (\(\delta = -0.11\), \(p_{\rm adj} = 0.028\)), and none involves \CG{}.


The spiral-fraction correlations show a broader environmental pattern rather than a clean dominated/non-dominated dichotomy. The most coherent set appears in \CC, where \(S_\mathrm{frac}\) increases with \(\left\langle R_{ij}\right\rangle\) in both dominated and non-dominated groups (\(\rho_\mathrm{S}=0.29\), \(p<10^{-4}\) and \(\rho_\mathrm{S}=0.27\), \(p<10^{-4}\)), while weaker negative trends are found with \(\sigma_v\) and \(L_\mathrm{group}\). \CB{} contributes only weaker non-dominated-group correlations. In \CG, the reported trends occur in small subsets, with \(S_\mathrm{frac}\) decreasing with \(L_\mathrm{group}\) among dominated groups (\(\rho_\mathrm{S}=-0.37\), 36 groups); we treat these as supporting diagnostics rather than the main dominance result. The full distributional diagnostics are provided in the online supplement.

\subsection{Morphology versus Zheng--Shen class, magnitude gap, and BGG dominance}\label{app:zheng_shen}

\subsection{Crossing time}\label{app:crossing_time}







Group radii and \(t_\mathrm{cross}\) use projected physical separations from the Planck 2015 angular-diameter distance, correcting the luminosity-distance convention used for this diagnostic in \citet{Tricottet+25}; the values decrease by 3.3--9.6\% (median 7.2\%), without changing the reported correlations at quoted precision. The crossing-time diagnostic mainly separates systems by dynamical scale rather than by luminosity: shorter \(t_\mathrm{cross}\) systems have larger virial mass-to-light ratios and virial masses, with correlations of \(\rho_\mathrm{S}=-0.39\) (\(p<10^{-4}\)) with \(\log(\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{VT}/L_r)\) and \(\rho_\mathrm{S}=-0.33\) (\(p<10^{-4}\)) with \(\log(\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{VT})\) over 816 groups, whereas the relation with total group luminosity is formally detected but very weak (\(\rho_\mathrm{S}=0.08\)). We therefore read \(t_\mathrm{cross}\) as a dynamical compactness indicator tied to virial mass and mass-to-light ratio, with little independent leverage from \(L_\mathrm{group}\).

\subsection{Matching details}\label{app:matching_details}

The galaxy-level control pool of Sect.~\ref{sec:morphology} is deduplicated by SDSS objid before matching: 5828 control rows collapse to 3609 unique galaxies. The propensity model uses \(\log M_\star\), redshift, rank, \(\log L_\mathrm{group}\), and \(\sigma_v\), and the match is one-to-one without replacement within exact rank strata. \section{Robustness details}\label{app:robustness_details}


\paragraph{Sample-definition sensitivities.} Restoring the 16 Zheng--Shen split systems while retaining the published model definitions leaves the morphology result qualitatively unchanged: the adjusted all-galaxy E-class odds ratios against \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{} are 1.85 (95\% CI \([1.39, 2.46]\)), 1.44 (\([1.10, 1.88]\)), and 2.38 (\([1.58, 3.58]\)), and the corresponding satellite odds ratios are 2.42 (\([1.80, 3.25]\)), 1.78 (\([1.35, 2.35]\)), and 2.75 (\([1.69, 4.49]\)). Retaining the anomalous Lim 3688 quartets likewise leaves the coefficients essentially unchanged: the all-galaxy and satellite odds ratios are 2.03 and 2.48 against \CB{}, and 1.60 and 1.86 against \CC{}.









\paragraph{Crowding.} Galaxy Zoo morphology is image-based, so crowded closest pairs could bias the votes. After excluding galaxies whose nearest projected neighbour within the catalogue group lies within 55 arcsec (retaining 200 \CG{} and 2534 \CC{} galaxies), the \CG{} all-row E-class fraction \emph{rises} relative to the full-sample value of Table~\ref{tab:morphologies} (55.0\% against 41.9\% for \CC{}, all retained rows including uncertain and missing classes), and the \CG{}--\CC{} contrast remains clear. In the joint model, the close-neighbour flag has OR \(2.00\) (\(p = 4.4\times 10^{-4}\)), while the \CG{} coefficient at fixed flag status is OR \(1.66\) (\(p = 6.6\times 10^{-4}\)); the former compares close with non-close galaxies, the latter \CG{} with controls at fixed flag status, so neither contradicts the rising raw \CG{} fraction after exclusion. The checks do not prove immunity to crowding, but they rule out the closest-pair artefact as the sole origin of the result.





\paragraph{Without \(\sigma_v\).} Refitting the per-control and pooled models without the \(\sigma_v\) covariate, on identical samples and with identical clustering, moves the E-class odds ratios from 1.99 to 2.06 (\CB{}), 1.60 to 1.59 (\CC{}), and 2.52 to 2.58 (\RG{}), less than their standard errors. The galaxy-level match retains the same 234 \CG{} galaxies with an E-class fraction difference of \(0.14\). The per-control group-level differences become \(\Delta = 0.22\), \(0.14\), and \(0.22\) against \CB{}, \CC{}, and \RG{} (permutation \(p = 0.0002\), \(0.011\), \(0.0013\)). The \CB{} change reflects the composition of the matched set rather than the \CG{} side: the with- and without-\(\sigma_v\) matches share only 9 of 57 control groups, the matched-control mean E-class satellite fraction changes by \(-0.09\) while the \CG{} mean is unchanged, and without \(\sigma_v\) the matched controls are dynamically cooler than their \CG{} partners (residual \(\sigma_v\) standardized mean difference \(0.49\), medians 158 against 123 \(\kms\)), so the with-\(\sigma_v\) match remains the primary one and the two values bracket the \CB{} contrast.







\paragraph{Within-host estimators.} The conditional-logit sample of Sect.~\ref{sec:morphology} contains 803 galaxies in 54 host strata. A binomial GLM with host fixed-effect dummies and cluster-robust standard errors gives OR \(1.56\) (95\% CI \([1.00, 2.42]\); \(p = 0.049\)); we prefer the conditional logit because it eliminates the host intercepts rather than estimating many indicators from small strata. The within-host quenched odds ratio is \(0.75\) (95\% CI \([0.45, 1.25]\); \(p = 0.269\); 915 complete cases), and \(1.22\) without the radius covariate (\(p = 0.412\)). A host-BGG alignment check supports the satellite interpretation: 56 of 62 non-split \CG{} systems have the same quartet and Lim-host BGG, the 6 misaligned systems are embedded systems whose host BGG is brighter than the compact-group BGG, and all 186 quartet-rank \CG{} satellites are Lim-host satellites; restricting \CG{} to the 56 BGG-aligned systems leaves the three satellite E-class contrasts stable (against \CC{}, OR \(1.94\)).



\paragraph{DS18 classification.} The DS18 cross-match of Sect.~\ref{sec:ssfr_classification} covers 173/248 (70\%) of the \CG{} galaxies and 2263/3609 (63\%) of the deduplicated controls, i.e. the DR7-era population; it is not worse in \CG{}, but neither rate establishes representativeness. With the DS18 \(T\mathrm{Type}\leq0\) outcome and the per-control covariates, standardisation, and physical-group clustering of Sect.~\ref{sec:morphology}, the \CG{} coefficients are positive in all three contrasts (Table~\ref{tab:morph_robustness}); support is broad except that 83\% of \CG{} values lie within the \RG{} group-luminosity range, limiting that smallest comparison. We use the DS18 \(P_{\rm S0}\) to distinguish S0s from ellipticals within the matched DR7 subset with \(T\mathrm{Type}\leq0\). The correspondence of DS18 S0s with GZ1 E and S depends strongly on inclination (Sect.~\ref{sec:ssfr_classification}). Restricting the sample to matched DS18 early types and dividing it by control and environment leaves too few galaxies for a reliable E/S0 comparison.

\section{Extended size diagnostics}\label{app:size_details}












Figure~\ref{fig:data_availability} shows the availability of the size measurements. The adjusted size coefficients for each control are shown in Fig.~S.6 of the online supplement. The concentration index \(C = R_{90,r}/R_{50,r}\) provides a model-independent structural proxy: the adjusted satellite coefficient is \(0.068\) (\(p = 0.124\)), and the matched concentration difference is \(-0.008\) (\(p = 0.904\)), offering no evidence for a fixed-mass concentration shift.


The crowding checks mirror Appendix~\ref{app:robustness_details}: excluding galaxies with a projected neighbour within 55 arcsec leaves a satellite coefficient of \(-0.013\) dex (\(p = 0.463\)), and adding the close-neighbour flag as a covariate gives \(-0.011\) dex (\(p = 0.479\)).



The per-galaxy difference between the two measures, \(d = \log R_{e,r} - \log R_{50,r}\), averages \(0.054\) dex, and is larger for GZ1 E-class galaxies (\(0.076\) dex) than for S-class galaxies (\(0.041\) dex), as expected if the Petrosian aperture preferentially truncates concentrated profiles. Its rank correlation with the nearest-neighbour separation is \(\rho_\mathrm{S} = 0.012\) (\(p = 0.524\)), with no detected monotonic relation to neighbour separation.



Adding the pairwise projected tidal index of Sect.~\ref{sec:tidal_index} to the all-galaxy size model changes the \CG{} coefficient from \(-0.012\) dex to \(0.005\) dex on the pairwise complete-case sample; the local projected-density proxy attenuates the size coefficient in the same way it attenuates the morphology coefficient.



The radial diagnostic is exploratory: mass- and redshift-adjusted satellite size residuals correlate with projected BGG-centric distance with \(\rho_\mathrm{S} = 0.17\) (\(p = 0.026\)) in \CG{} and \(\rho_\mathrm{S} = 0.18\) (\(p<10^{-4}\)) in the pooled controls. A more negative inner-region residual would be the tidal-truncation expectation; the observed trends are similar in \CG{} and the controls, so the data do not isolate a \CG{}-specific inner-region size suppression.





Within morphology classes, the satellite-only fits give \(0.031\) dex for GZ1 E-class satellites (\(p = 0.091\)) and \(0.000\) dex for S-class satellites (\(p = 0.998\)), and the \CG{}\(\times\)morphology interaction is \(0.022\) dex (\(p = 0.491\)). These strata are secondary diagnostics of whether size carries information beyond the morphology shift.
