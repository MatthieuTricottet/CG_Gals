# T0 summary — Control4C construction audit and repair

Prompted by the referee's question, we audited the Control4C sample against the full parent catalogue and found that the CSV inherited from our Paper I data release selected the three nearest projected companions *regardless of relative magnitude*: 457 of 705 groups (31.7% of companions) contained a companion with Δm > 3 (up to 5.76 mag), violating the intended construction. The sample behind Paper I's *published* statistics did apply the Δm ≤ 3 filter — re-deriving Control4C with the documented "filter first, then rank" construction reproduces that paper's published counts (61 exclusions → 704 groups) and every published Control4C statistic (Table 2 medians; Table 3 T₁/T₂) exactly, so the defect lay in the distributed data file, not in the published analysis. We have therefore regenerated Control4C with the Δm ≤ 3-restricted construction (`src/sample_construction.py`), which changes the quartet membership of 457 groups, and re-run the entire analysis pipeline on the corrected sample; machine-verified assertions (`referee/T0_control4c_audit.py`: every companion within 3 magnitudes, every quartet exactly the three nearest such members, zero violations) now gate the sample, and Sect. 2.1 states the eligibility filter explicitly. Control4B and RG4 pass the same assertions with zero violations by construction. The audit of the defective sample is preserved in `referee/T0_control4c_audit_shipped_FAIL.md`, and the resolved off-by-one (60 vs 61 exclusions) is documented in OPEN_QUESTIONS.md #1.

# T6 summary — origin of the Control4C satellite-mass offset, and rewording

The +0.18 dex CG4−Control4C satellite-mass offset the referee asked about was, we now find, dominated by the Control4C construction defect uncovered in T0: on the defective (unrestricted) sample the raw offset is +0.27 dex, of which the Δm > 3 interlopers (mean log M⋆ = 9.42) account for essentially all — on the corrected sample the offset collapses to +0.01 dex raw and +0.03 dex (SMD 0.09) in the matched pairs. The referee's proposed mechanism is confirmed where it applies: within the shared Δm ≤ 3 window, distance-ranked selection samples the eligible-satellite magnitude-gap distribution democratically (Control4C: KS D = 0.04 against the parent-group reference; CG4 itself: D = 0.06, p = 0.44), whereas brightness-ranked Control4B is strongly bright-weighted (D = 0.30, p ~ 10⁻¹¹⁵; mean satellite log M⋆ higher than CG4's by 0.21 dex) — the one amendment being that CG4's own satellites sample the window democratically rather than bright-weighted. Sect. 3.3 is rewritten as requested: the with- and without-mass-balance matches are presented as different estimands (composition at matched group scale versus at matched satellite mass), not ordered levels of bias correction and not a bracketing of a causal effect, with a new Sect. 4.1 caution that present-day M⋆ may itself encode the environmental history under test. The abstract, Sect. 3.2, Sect. 4.1, and the conclusion now carry the corrected-sample formulation: a strong adjusted excess against Control4B and RG4, a smaller but detected excess against Control4C, and a group-level composition hierarchy (significant/significant/marginal) that is stable under mass balancing — consistent with compact groups sampling the extreme local-projected-density tail of ordinary-group cores. Diagnostic values: `referee/values/T6.json`, `referee/T6_satmass_table.csv`.

# T1 summary — adjusted per-control families after the 55″ exclusion

We agree that robustness of the adjusted estimand must be shown on the adjusted estimand, and we now refit the complete Sect. 3.2 per-control adjusted families (all eight outcomes, identical covariates, cluster-robust SEs by physical Lim group, Holm within each contrast as a separately labelled sensitivity family) on the sample excluding every galaxy whose nearest projected same-group neighbour lies within 55″ — removing 19% of CG4 members versus 6%, 10%, and 5% of Control4B, Control4C, and RG4. # T2 summary — within-host models with and without host-centric radius

We refit the Sect. 3.4 conditional logit and FE-GLM (elliptical; quenched) without the projected host-centric radius term, on the identical complete-case sample and strata, and now report both p-value conventions for both estimators (Holm across the two outcomes within each estimator, the published family convention). With radius, the elliptical odds ratios are 1.47 (conditional logit; Holm p = 0.216) and 1.57 (FE-GLM; nominal p = 0.041, Holm p = 0.083) — under the like-for-like correction the two estimators agree, resolving the apparent 0.216-versus-0.041 inconsistency. Without radius the elliptical excess is strong in both estimators: OR = 1.90 (Holm p = 0.008) and 2.06 (Holm p = 0.0013), while quenched remains null in all four cells. As Sect. 3.4 now explains, the radius-conditioned model asks whether CG members differ *beyond central location* and the radius-free model *including location*; under the paper's local-density interpretation the conditioned coefficient is expected to be strongly attenuated, so the positive radius-free coefficient supports the location reading rather than contradicting the published within-host null. Full grid: `referee/T2_within_host_table.csv`, values in `referee/values/T2.json`.

# T3 summary — σ_v documentation and no-σ_v sensitivity

Sect. 2.4 now documents that σ_v is, for every sample, the quartet-level gapper (Wainer & Thissen 1976) on the four selected members' redshifts (c σ_z/(1+z̄)) — including Control4B/4C quartets embedded in richer hosts, which is why the spurious Lim 3688 quartets reach ≃2000 km/s — and quantifies its noise by Monte Carlo: for N = 4 Gaussian tracers, σ_gapper/σ_true has median 0.96 with a 16–84% range of 0.57–1.42 (10⁵ draws). The vague "where sufficiently complete" is replaced by the actual rule (a candidate covariate enters when non-missing for ≥65% of the model frame; matching variables need ≥70%), noting that σ_v is complete for every quartet and always enters, and the models that use σ_v are now enumerated: the per-control adjusted families, the pooled models, the galaxy-level propensity model, and the group-level matching covariates. All of these were refit without σ_v (same seeds, samples, B = 9999, clustering, and Holm conventions, as a labelled sensitivity family): none of the 32 adjusted models changes Holm-level significance in either direction (max |ΔOR| = 0.34, well within one standard error), the galaxy-level match keeps the same 234 CG4 galaxies with a slightly stronger elliptical-fraction difference (0.155 vs 0.139), and the per-control group-level contrasts remain positive with Control4C still the weakest (Δ = 0.28, 0.10, 0.25; permutation p = 0.0001, 0.056, 0.0016). The σ_v adjustment is therefore, if anything, conservative, and no conclusion in the paper depends on it. Full tables: `referee/T3_no_sigma_table.csv`, values in `referee/values/T3.json`.

# T4 summary — pairwise tidal index: definition, common support, wording

Sect. 3.6 now states the exact construction extracted from the code: T_i = Σ_j M⋆,j/R_ij³ sums over the galaxy's *selected quartet co-members only* (not the full Lim host), with no velocity window, R_ij from the angular-diameter distance at the quartet's median redshift, co-members with unmeasured mass contributing zero (a lower bound), quartets missing any coordinate/redshift dropped whole, and — now stated explicitly — models that adjust only for stellar mass and satellite status on the pooled, non-deduplicated control catalogue with physical-group clustering. Common-support diagnostics are reported: the CG4 and control log T_i distributions overlap with coefficient 0.54, but medians differ by ≈1.1 dex and 15% of CG4 galaxies lie above the pooled-control 95th percentile, while collinearity is mild (point-biserial r = 0.18; VIF of the CG4 indicator 1.03). Sensitivity refits show the residual CG4 elliptical coefficient at fixed T_i is not robust: OR = 1.39 (linear, p = 0.03), 1.40 (natural cubic spline), 1.27 (deduplicated pool, p = 0.12), and 1.22 (trimmed to common support, p = 0.20), reinforcing the manuscript's conditional-attenuation (not mediation) reading. The Sect. 4.3 fibre-collision sentence is softened as requested: the 19%/6% sub-55″ fractions demonstrate differential crowding among *catalogued* galaxies, a downward bias of T_i from spectroscopically missing neighbours is labelled plausible but not demonstrated, and a photometric-companion index is deferred to future work. Values in `referee/values/T4.json`, per-variant fits in `referee/T4_support_table.csv`, distribution figure in `referee/T4_logT_support.pdf`.

# T7 summary — cosmology/size audit, group-mass covariate, code health

Hand-recomputation confirms the inherited convention: `size_Group_Bary_kpc` (and hence t_cr) in every `*_Groups` table converts angular size with the *luminosity* distance (the CSV value reproduces the D_L computation to machine precision), overestimating proper sizes by (1+z)² = 3.3–9.6% (median 7.2%), while M_virial uses the angular-diameter distance — an internal inconsistency inherited from the published Paper I pipeline, whose ⟨R_ij⟩ = 313 kpc reproduces only under D_L. Because no fitted model uses size or t_cr as a covariate (verified), we document rather than regenerate: a Sect. 2.1 footnote quantifies the bias, together with the second inherited convention that the exported `dist2BGG` column is radians×3600 (orderings unaffected; every consumer in this paper divides by 3600 and converts with D_A, as verified for the tidal R_ij, host-centric distances, radial diagnostics, and Simard/Petrosian sizes; the normalized radius R_norm inherits the smooth (1+z)² scale factor, diagnostics only). On group mass, the tables' `lMass_200` turns out to be the Yang–Lim abundance-matching M_180m of the *host* halo converted to M_200c (r = 0.99999 with host logM_180; r = 0.19 with the quartet virial mass), so Appendix F.4 now explains its exclusion (host-halo semantics that differ across samples, near-collinearity with the group-luminosity covariate, selection entanglement) and reports the requested sensitivity fit: adding log M_200c leaves every elliptical conclusion unchanged (pooled OR 1.77 → 1.62; per-control 2.00/1.56/2.65, all p < 0.005). The `np.clip(-1, 1, x)` argument-order defect was found at three sites in the circumcircle helper (and one benign site in legacy `common.py`), shown to affect no shipped quantity (its only caller is the legacy 'Circ' branch the pipeline never executes), and fixed. Values: `referee/values/T7.json`.
