# Paper II referee reanalysis

## 1. Executive summary

- **Compactness.** The full-sample adjusted CG4–Control4C difference in satellite E-class fraction was **+15.4 percentage points** (95% CI +8.0 to +22.8). Within observed compactness support the adjusted difference at 175 kpc fell to **+2.4 pp** (−8.8 to +13.5), allowing different slopes. Quartet matching gave **0.0 pp** (−12.6 to +12.6, 37 pairs with usable satellite outcomes on both sides). The reduction is compatible with a common morphology–compactness relation over the shared range. The interval still allows a meaningful residual, and the most compact CG4s have poor support. **A CG4 offset at the same compactness remains inconclusive.**
- **Stellar mass.** The intermediate-mass raw fractions in the request are confirmed. The continuous morphology interaction has no precise estimate. For quenching, CG4 versus Control4C yields a positive log-odds interaction of **+1.57 per dex** (0.46 to 2.68, p = 0.0055, Holm p = 0.033 across six tests). It weakens under the BGG-dependent red-galaxy completeness restriction (**+0.99**, −0.90 to +2.89). There is evidence of mass dependence for this comparator in the observed sample, with no secure general or completeness-robust result and no evidence of a peak in log-odds at 10–10.5.
- **Within hosts.** The 791-galaxy fit gives **OR 1.90** (1.23–2.95) without radius and **1.47** (0.92–2.35) with linear radius from the Lim BGG. The 46 exact-core CG4s have no overlap in BGG-centric radial ranges between their eligible satellites and eligible nonmembers of the same host. Only six physical hosts offer such overlap after E/S and mass requirements, and the ±50 kpc analysis retains just four informative strata. The fitted coefficient is sensitive to radial form. **The existing models cannot separate membership from selection into central locations with adequate empirical support.**

## 4. Morphology–compactness comparison

Appendix F.2 reproduces the four published per-sample-SD ORs (CG4 0.653, Control4C 0.702, Control4B 0.800, RG4 0.923). The original code labels clusters by sample and quartet ID, which treats two CG4 quartets in the same host as independent. The revised fits use physical hosts and an unstandardized log separation, adjusting for stellar mass. The four mean-separation slopes in log-odds per dex are **−2.45** (−4.00 to −0.91) for CG4, **−1.34** (−1.75 to −0.94) for Control4C, **−1.04** (−1.53 to −0.55) for Control4B and **−0.28** (−3.08 to +2.51) for RG4. The closest-separation population is sparse in RG4. The median-separation specification gives similar per-dex slopes. The comparison uses the paper's satellite covariates (stellar mass, galaxy redshift, quartet log luminosity, velocity dispersion). Both window fits use 575 labelled galaxies across 226 quartets and 225 hosts. At 175 kpc the common-slope CG4 log-odds offset is **0.195** (−0.206 to +0.597, p = 0.339), corresponding to 48.7% versus 44.0% standardized E fractions and **+4.6 pp** (−4.9 to +14.2). The interaction fit gives a CG4 offset of **0.100** (−0.370 to +0.571, p = 0.674) and a slope difference of **−1.41 per dex** (−5.35 to +2.53, p = 0.481). The slope-difference interval admits substantial differences in both directions. At 175 kpc the interaction model predicts 46.8% versus 44.4%, or **+2.4 pp** (−8.8 to +13.5). ### Mass completeness

The Paper I selection is BGG-volume-limited with satellite eligibility that depends on the BGG. We used the brighter of the two limits, 17.77 − DM(z) − k_eff and the BGG absolute r magnitude plus three. Here k_eff is an empirical 95th-percentile offset linking Tempel apparent magnitudes to Lim–Tempel absolute magnitudes, with extinction, filter and catalogue conventions absorbed jointly. The 95th percentile of log stellar mass + 0.4 M_r in 4,005 red (0.75 ≤ g − r ≤ 1.3) Lim–Tempel parent galaxies sets the mass limit. This is a conservative empirical sensitivity region for luminous hosts with these measurements. It cannot model all colour, target or redshift failures. For HMCGs it applies a common absolute eligibility envelope in addition to their actual apparent-r selection.

## 6. Within-host identification problem

Using the released Lim host membership and exact SDSS objIDs, each non-isolated CG4 was mapped to its Lim BGG and the BGG's three nearest eligible members. The 46 exact identities include 12/19 Embedded and 34/37 Predominant CG4s. In every exact identity, the member ranks within the eligible BGG-centric radius ordering are exactly 1–4. Eligible nonmembers are all beyond the selected core's outer satellite. Some have a small radial gap across its boundary, but no exact-core quartet has overlapping eligible-satellite radial ranges inside its host. This is a structural lack of positivity conditional on the host and BGG-centric radial rank. It persists when the marginal radial distributions overlap after pooling different hosts.

The original variable `dist_host_kpc` comes from `PC_Gals.dist2BGG` and uses angular-diameter distance at the Lim redshift. We independently recomputed great-circle separations from each host BGG, agreeing within the 0.008 kpc test tolerance. Distance from the catalogue barycentre is a separate observable. The host-level CG flag is the union of member IDs in the two multi-CG hosts. At this unit there are 44 exact-only hosts, eight non-identical-only and two mixed. Raw eligible satellite ranges overlap in eight hosts (six non-identical-only and two mixed). The E/S and mass complete data leave six (five and one). A minimum gap of ≤25 or ≤50 kpc occurs in seven and nine hosts respectively, including one and three exact-only hosts. Close pairs across an already deterministic boundary provide sensitivity checks, and range overlap alone does not establish exchangeability at fixed mass and luminosity rank. Before assignment there are 24,092 caliper-compatible edges overall and 19,467 satellite edges. Each of the 234 complete CG4 galaxies has an admissible candidate and is matched. The 14 unmatched CG4 rows have missing stellar mass and fail before propensity matching. The manuscript's explanation in G.2 that those rows lie outside propensity support or exceed a caliper is inconsistent with this reconstruction. ### Colour selection

Four derived magnitudes are available for 217 CG4 galaxies. The actual retained matched-photometry set is **110/248**, with 931/2,792 Control4Bs, 1,153/2,812 Control4Cs and 102/224 RG4s. Average CG4 log mass is 10.12 in the retained set and 10.69 among others with measured mass. Among labelled galaxies 49.5% of the retained set and 68.6% of the rest are E class. ## 8. Robustness and limitations

- Omitting any one CG4 physical host from the interaction fit moves the 175 kpc difference from +0.7 to +4.2 pp. A 0.05-dex caliper gives +2.7 pp (−11.3 to +17.1), and 0.12 dex gives −0.4 pp (−11.9 to +10.5). Adding a 120 km/s caliper to the quartet match gives +3.4 pp (−9.8 to +17.5, 39 valid pairs). All of these intervals allow scientifically relevant residuals. [Host influence](tables/compact_treated_host_influence.csv)
- The Control4C quenching interaction persists without dispersion (+1.52 per dex, 0.42 to 2.61). In the common E/S and sSFR complete sample, it is +1.66 (0.47 to 2.85) before and +1.49 (0.14 to 2.85) after an E/S covariate. This is a selected-sample sensitivity and morphology could be downstream of the environment. It does not restore evidence under the joint completeness cut. The uncertainty intervals are pointwise, and the primary Holm family includes the two outcomes across three comparators. [Mass model sensitivities](tables/mass_model_sensitivities.csv)
- Selection into quartets conditions on galaxy positions and luminosities. Projected radii and dispersion are imperfect proxies for physical density. The matching covariates and overlap criteria address observed support. The residual morphology effect could differ in the excluded or most compact regions. Small CG4 counts and selected outcome denominators limit model complexity, particularly in the high-mass and same-host local-radius tails. The proposed nonlinear within-host sensitivity demonstrates functional-form dependence. A flexible mass interaction was deferred because it would be driven by sparse high-mass observations.
- The completeness envelope and isolation measure rely on local photometry and recovered spectroscopy. Neither can reproduce all imaging failures, membership redshifts and associated selection in the HMCG search. The quantity called fibre aperture here is the ratio of a Legacy-sized fibre to a Petrosian angular radius, rather than a flux fraction. Total sSFR recovery still requires usable alternative spectra and MPA–JHU measurements. No regression convergence failures occurred in the primary between-sample fits. The two narrow within-host specifications were explicitly withheld for lack of informative rows.

## 9. Implications for Paper II

The observed morphology contrast relative to the full ordinary-group comparison samples is reproducible. The attenuation at comparable mean compactness and the dearth of really close same-host controls narrow the interpretation to an association with observed dense projected cores. Available comparisons do not establish that the HMCG selection contributes an additional offset beyond compactness. The joint completeness check preserves the intermediate-mass E excess against Control4C, but the mass-interaction evidence is specific to quenching versus Control4C in the broader observed population. The star-formation diagnostics are consistent with less recent activity at intermediate mass, with uncertainty in aperture and sample coverage. These results do not justify a claim that a distinctive CG4 effect peaks at an intermediate stellar mass.

### Paper I and Paper II consistency

Paper I Sect. 3.3 labels 19 as Predominant and 37 as Embedded, while its Table 5 labels the same counts 19 Embedded and 37 Predominant. The exact membership reconstruction confirms that the 19 have CG4-to-host luminosity fractions between 0.064 and 0.478 and the 37 between 0.515 and 0.982. Paper II Sect. 2.1, Appendix C and the CG class tables use the latter, physically correct definitions. The Paper I prose about the relative excess of Embedded systems needs reinterpretation with the corrected class counts. Paper II's pooled comparisons and the within-host selection of both non-isolated classes are invariant to a label swap. [Paper I Sect. 3.3 and Table 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.20357v5)

Paper I Table 2 reports a 313 kpc median for Control4C. Rebuilding its eligible 704-group sample with the archived Table 2 check gives 313 kpc with luminosity distance and 293 kpc with angular-diameter distance. Current Paper II Table 1 reproduces the 293 kpc figure on its final sample, and the loader overwrites the inherited size and crossing-time columns with angular-diameter distance values. The Appendix F compactness fit computes pair angles and angular-diameter distance directly. Sect. 2.1's general statement that projected separations use angular-diameter distance “as in Paper I” conflicts with the crossing-time note. Replace it with a note limited to the diagnostics actually corrected. The stored CSV sizes are much closer to the luminosity-distance calculation than the angular-diameter-distance calculation, including the released Control4C CSV. In the current final samples the percentage reduction relative to luminosity distance, given by 1 − (1 + z)⁻², ranges 3.21–8.74% and is about 6.7–7.5% by sample median. Wording should specify the denominator before quoting a range. For example, relative to the angular-diameter distance value the excess is (1 + z)² − 1. Recomputed crossing-time correlations are close, but some change at the second or third decimal. For CG4 the crossing-time Spearman correlation with virial mass is −0.683 in the raw export and −0.684 after correction.
